1 · COMPUTE

Google's results divide along advertising and cloud

Google's parent company reported third quarter revenue of one hundred and twenty seven billion five hundred and seventy seven million dollars, up twenty five percent. Revenue was slightly below market expectations. Advertising revenue rose about eleven percent to eighty two billion twelve million dollars. Search and other revenue grew twelve percent to sixty three billion three hundred and fifty million dollars.

These businesses are growing, but they did not meet the market's expectations. Management says A I is driving search volume, including more commercial queries. More usage does not immediately produce the same advertising revenue per query. The transition involves changes in advertising formats, placement and monetization.

If traffic grows before revenue conversion catches up, the traditional profit engine faces pressure. That is a mechanism to test next quarter. One report does not prove that Search has lost its competitive position.

Google Cloud was the clear bright spot. Revenue rose one hundred and three percent to thirty billion seven hundred and nine million dollars. Operating profit increased from three billion five hundred and ninety four million dollars to eleven billion one hundred and twenty one million dollars. The operating margin was about thirty six point two percent.

Cloud growth and profitability both improved. The group selloff cannot be equated with failed A I demand. At the same time, strong cloud results do not erase the advertising disappointment.

Earnings per share under generally accepted accounting principles were two dollars and twelve cents, down from two dollars and eighty seven cents a year earlier. Equity investment losses reduced this quarter's earnings per share by fifty five cents. Operating growth, investment marks and market expectations need separate assessments. Investment losses should not all be charged to Search and Cloud.

2 · FOCUS

Give Google another quarter, while watching the cash shortfall

Google generated forty two billion two hundred and ninety million dollars in quarterly operating cash flow. It spent fifty one billion three hundred and fifty five million dollars on property and equipment. Free cash flow was negative nine billion sixty five million dollars. This was the second consecutive negative quarter.

Free cash flow over the past twelve months was nineteen billion seven hundred and forty seven million dollars, down about seventy three point one five percent from a year earlier. Rapid cloud growth has not yet taken the group out of its heavy investment phase. The cash shortfall still matters.

The company maintained its twenty twenty six capital spending forecast of one hundred and ninety five billion to two hundred and five billion dollars. It said spending would increase significantly in twenty twenty seven, with details to follow. It did not raise this year's guidance again in the quarter. It also did not establish a firm spending figure for next year.

At quarter end, twenty seven point six billion dollars remained available under the company's at the market issuance program. That is capacity for future issuance, rather than shares already sold. Dilution needs to be reassessed if additional shares are issued. Negative quarterly free cash flow does not mean cash is about to run out, but it brings funding costs and payback periods into sharper focus.

A selloff does not establish that the shares are cheap. One report also does not invalidate the entire A I strategy. Watch the fourth quarter for advertising revenue catching up with query growth, continued cloud profitability and a narrower cash shortfall.

Two consecutive quarters of traditional business weakness alongside rising investment would call for a reassessment of the holding thesis. Improving advertising and cash conversion would provide a fundamental case for recovering some of the selloff. Give the operating evidence time to develop.

3 · CAPEX

Microsoft's A I commercialization shows up in cash flow

Microsoft reported the first quarter of fiscal twenty twenty seven, ended in September. This covers the same period as Google's calendar third quarter. Revenue rose nineteen percent to ninety two billion two hundred and seventy nine million dollars. Operating profit was forty five billion nine hundred and fifteen million dollars, with a margin of about forty nine point eight percent.

Earnings per share under generally accepted accounting principles were four dollars and ninety two cents. The adjusted figure, excluding the effect of OpenAI investments, was four dollars and eighty nine cents. Keep those two measures separate.

Azure revenue rose forty eight percent to thirty three billion one hundred and sixty five million dollars. Growth was the same on reported and constant currency bases. Commercial remaining performance obligations reached six hundred and seventy seven billion dollars. These contracted commitments have not all been recognized as revenue.

Cloud growth, profit and positive group free cash flow provide firmer evidence of commercialization than demand alone. That does not make every contracted dollar current revenue.

Quarterly operating cash flow was fifty eight billion dollars. Cash purchases of property and equipment were thirty nine billion three hundred and sixty seven million dollars. Subtracting the second figure from the first leaves approximately eighteen billion six hundred and thirty three million dollars in free cash flow. Operating cash flow grew, but cash capital spending rose faster. Free cash flow stayed positive while falling about twenty seven percent year over year.

Retaining cash after heavy investment shows greater financial capacity. It does not establish an across the board improvement in cash returns. Cash property and equipment spending excludes some new finance leases. It is not total capital expenditure. Group free cash flow is also not the stand alone profit of the A I business. The comparison shows financial capacity, rather than a direct ranking of A I returns.

There were weak spots. Combined Windows original equipment manufacturer and Devices revenue fell twenty two percent. Microsoft three sixty five commercial seat growth was five percent. The count now includes paid GitHub seats, so it is not directly comparable with the old definition.

Microsoft lowered its calendar twenty twenty six capital spending forecast from one hundred and seventy five billion dollars to about one hundred and seventy billion dollars. The change reflects the timing of third party data center deliveries. Spending may still accelerate in the second half of fiscal twenty twenty seven. That period corresponds to the first half of calendar twenty twenty seven.

Those periods cannot be combined into a spending commitment for the whole of calendar twenty twenty seven. Slower cloud growth or weaker cash conversion would also require a less constructive view. Strong cloud results do not mean every product is accelerating.

A pullback into four hundred and ninety to five hundred and thirty four dollars can be a range for reassessing Microsoft. It is not guaranteed support. The medium term case depends on cloud growth and cash conversion, rather than the after hours gain alone. Wait for regular trading to confirm the bid, then distinguish volatility from weaker operating expectations.

4 · SEMIS

Neutral for semiconductors, with high expectations still vulnerable

Cloud demand remains intact, and Google has not softened its message on twenty twenty seven investment. That supports the demand outlook for computing suppliers. Without a clear new spending figure, it is difficult to argue that chip valuations should rise across the board.

For Nvidia and S O X X, intact demand is only part of the case. Order conversion, customer funding capacity and current valuations still need to support the shares. More customer spending can lift supplier revenue while depressing the customer's own free cash flow. Shareholder returns can diverge across the supply chain.

High expectation technology stocks such as Marvell, Fortinet, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto and Palantir require more than a favorable industry outlook. If their prices depend on years of rapid growth, even a modest disappointment can compress valuation multiples. Growth and valuation need to be checked company by company. Treat the entry price as a separate decision from the industry outlook.

Sharp after hours declines show widening disagreement over expectations. They do not confirm a trend for the entire industry. Thin after hours liquidity and hedging demand can amplify volatility. The first sharp decline cannot establish the eventual direction by itself.
