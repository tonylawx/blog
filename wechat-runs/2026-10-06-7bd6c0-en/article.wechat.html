0 · LIQUIDITY

A record close, on lighter volume

Tuesday was three up and one down. Only the Russell finished lower. The S&P 500 finally printed a record close, but volume shrank. The Nasdaq one hundred made its third straight record close. Inside the S&P 500, three hundred thirty-nine names rose and one hundred sixty-two fell. Ten of eleven sectors finished higher. Health care was the only one down.

The tape is grinding higher, and accounts are making new highs, but there is not much new information in it. The next push has to come from earnings. Third-quarter reports start next Thursday. October is already a high-volatility month, and the midterm election is November third, so the range should widen once the prints begin. The headline is a twenty-seven percent year-over-year gain in S&P 500 earnings per share, the highest pre-season growth forecast since twenty twenty-one. Under the surface it is an oligopoly. The artificial-intelligence semiconductor leaders are already priced for a lot of that growth. How far the index goes from here depends on whether the median name, expected to grow earnings per share only nine percent, can beat.

1 · MOMENTUM

Utilities: that candle looks like a squeeze

Utilities were the standout sector. Constellation Energy signed a power deal with Google and jumped about twelve percent, pulling the group up almost three percent. Plenty of power names were up seven or eight points, some ten.

The utilities sector fund was already a crowded short, with the setup for a squeeze. Tuesday looked like one: a gap up, heavy volume, and a wide candle with almost no wick. That is not a change in the utility business. A new contract helps Constellation's longer-dated orders. It does not mean every name in the sector just got the same contract. Nobody can say how far a squeeze runs. On the chart, the utilities fund still has not cleared forty-two point four, and the supply from forty-two point four up to forty-six point eight is a stack of real levels.

If you were in utilities from lower, watch for a topping print overhead and take the gain. Treat the sector pop as short covering.

2 · MARKET

Third quarter: sixty-eight percent of the twenty-seven comes from ten names

The street expects S&P 500 third-quarter earnings per share to grow twenty-seven percent year over year. Goldman calls that the highest pre-season growth forecast since twenty twenty-one. Open it up and it is oligopoly earnings.

The evidence is the contribution table. Artificial-intelligence infrastructure names are expected to drive more than half of the index's earnings growth. The top ten contributors are more than two-thirds of it, specifically sixty-eight percent, versus forty-seven percent in the first quarter and forty-eight percent in the second. In Goldman's split, Micron alone is nineteen percent and Nvidia is fifteen percent. Together they are more than a third of the index's earnings growth, and their earnings increase is larger than the bottom four hundred ninety names combined. Then Meta at seven percent, Google at six percent, and Broadcom at five percent. The list also includes the oil-linked earnings from Exxon, Chevron, and Marathon Petroleum, plus Boeing.

Goldman splits artificial intelligence into two buckets that do not overlap. Infrastructure excluding hyperscale data centers is fifty-four percent of the quarter's growth. Hyperscale data centers add another nineteen percent. Anything tied to artificial intelligence is roughly three-quarters of this quarter's S&P 500 earnings growth.

Micron shows the mechanism. Last week's report beat on adjusted earnings and revenue, and gross margin went from forty-five point seven percent a year ago to eighty-seven percent. The stock did not rip. The buy side was already ahead of the sell side, the big earnings jump was priced, and the guide was not raised enough. A name up nearly three hundred percent year to date, with earnings per share up one thousand three percent, has digested that print. Morgan Stanley's line is the right one: the argument has moved from how high earnings can go to how long this can last. A lot of semiconductor companies are running gross margins above seventy percent. A normal cycle average is about fifty-five percent. Twenty or even thirty points above that does not stay forever.

Hold the leaders. Do not count on them to supply the next leg of index upside.

3 · MARKET

The median is only nine percent. That is the variable

Median S&P 500 earnings growth for the third quarter is only nine percent, below the second quarter's fourteen percent, with the fourth quarter seen accelerating to twelve percent. The nine percent is a cut. Median profit expectations were taken down about eleven basis points. Input costs are the reason: materials and energy are pushing inflation through the factory, and a lot of companies cannot pass it on, so margins are under pressure. Only technology was revised up in size. Some communication services was revised up. Every other sector's third-quarter estimate was cut.

Sell-side numbers on this middle group have been coming down since the start of the year. A lower bar is easier to beat. Most companies beat in a normal season. The question is by how much. The leaders' growth is already in the price. How high and how fast the S&P 500 can go now depends on how far the nine-percent median names beat. A wide beat can produce catch-up rallies, with semiconductors holding a high, slow grind if they merely meet or beat.

If the book is outside artificial intelligence, or a cyclical that has been soft, this quarter matters. Watch the beat rate in that middle group.

4 · MARKET

Vietnam: the reform list, and the Vietnam E-T-F

This section is not the U.S. earnings tape. It is a separate look at Vietnam.

The reform list is already in motion. It was not a surprise dropped on October sixth. First, English is being pushed toward a second language in schools. The government scheme runs from twenty twenty-five to twenty thirty-five, and the aim is for English to be compulsory from grade one nationwide by twenty thirty. Today it is generally compulsory from grade three. Second, the minimum wage keeps rising. Regional minimum wages rose about seven point two percent on average from January first, twenty twenty-six. Third, under the twenty twenty-four social insurance law, the minimum contribution period for a pension dropped from twenty years to fifteen. Fourth, social security and health insurance coverage is widening, including compulsory enrollment for registered business households, plus electronic social-insurance books and electronic health-insurance cards. Fifth, the state is cutting government agencies and administrative headcount, and replacing repeat paper filings with database lookups. Sixth, household and residence registration is going digital. Applications can go through the national public-service portal and the national I-D app, and agencies are not supposed to ask residents to resubmit information the database already has.

All six point at the same slow variables: skills, pay, social insurance, and a thinner bureaucracy. That is institutional capital, not a one-day earnings impulse.

The VanEck Vietnam E-T-F closed October sixth at sixteen dollars and ninety-three cents, up three and a half cents, or about a fifth of one percent. It opened at sixteen dollars and ninety-nine cents, high seventeen dollars and two cents, low sixteen dollars and ninety-two cents, prior close sixteen dollars and eighty-nine and a half cents. Volume was about two hundred forty-four thousand shares, turnover a little over four million dollars, and the day's range was just over half a percent. After hours it was seventeen dollars, up about four tenths of one percent.

On the daily chart the price is pinned under a cluster of short moving averages, roughly sixteen ninety-two to seventeen thirty-three. The prior swing high at nineteen dollars and seventy-seven cents is still a long way up. Sixteen dollars and thirty-four cents is the prior low. A gain of about a fifth of one percent on that narrow a range is not a breakout.

The reforms are a slow-money case. Nineteen seventy-seven is still overhead. Do not use the six items as a reason to chase seventeen.

5 · SOFTWARE

Single names: Marvell, Advanced Micro Devices, Intuitive Surgical, Constellation

Marvell, M-R-V-L, raised its revenue outlook and the stock gained almost six percent. The company put a twenty-thirty opportunity at four hundred billion dollars, and fiscal twenty twenty-eight revenue at about twenty billion, up from a prior fifteen billion. That is roughly a thirty percent lift to the fiscal twenty twenty-eight revenue number. A rough pass, assuming profit up twenty percent, is about fifty dollars of value. Allowing for margin mix, the working range is forty to fifty dollars, which would put the January twenty twenty-eight forward value around two hundred eighty to three hundred. Tuesday's rally landed in that zone. The company's own report still has to confirm it. Overhead, three hundred eighteen is the marked-up super level. Through it, there is no supply, and the path is the old high and then a new high. Failure there is a stall risk. Tuesday tagged three hundred one and was sold. The bounce never cleared the upper-half supply, and the close stayed under it. If the fundamental lift is only forty to fifty dollars, anything above three hundred is already a year expensive.

Advanced Micro Devices, A-M-D, closed at a recent high on volume. A close at the recent high is neutral. Do not let the intraday shape talk you out of that. Support sits at six hundred five to six hundred twenty-six, then there is a gap down to below five hundred sixty-two. The twenty twenty-seven forward value is five hundred fifty-six, so the price is already trading the twenty twenty-eight forward. After the last report, the setup was a profit surge in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-seven, with a floor growth rate above fifty percent. Another one hundred fifty to two hundred dollars into fiscal twenty twenty-eight would put a number near seven hundred. When calendar twenty twenty-seven arrives and the market is pricing the twenty twenty-eight forward, that can be reasonable. Right now it is early by about a year and a half. Holders can stay on. Semiconductors have broken out and the trend is up. No need to be the first one off while the group is still being carried.

Intuitive Surgical, I-S-R-G, needs a cooler read. Growth is mid-single-digit to low-double-digit. On the twenty twenty-seven forward, anything above four hundred ten is rich for that pace. The old fifty-times prints came with growth above twenty-five percent. Growth is about half of that now, so fifty times and higher does not fit. A reasonable band is inside four hundred fifty. Above five hundred is clearly rich. Support below is thick and supply above is thick. A slow crawl to the top of the range at four hundred seventy would already be a good outcome. A push through five hundred needs the market to be pricing it around May or June of twenty twenty-seven. There is no strong reason to force that break this year. The add zone is under three hundred thirteen. If it does not get there, leave it alone. The lower end of the range can still reach under two hundred eighty, because the growth rate still has room to be cut.

Constellation, C-E-G, is a chart call. The power-shortage trade had a run and then went quiet. Most of Tuesday's jump belongs to the squeeze and short covering, not to a broad fundamental reset. Three hundred twenty-eight is the level that would put it on the right side of a larger uptrend. It is not there. The day high was three hundred nine and it was sold. Still a defense. If you were in from lower, take the gain.

Amazon had a decent Tuesday. Same comment as Google: discussed enough already.
