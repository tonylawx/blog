The Market Split Beneath the Index

On Monday, November second, twenty twenty six, lower oil prices gave Wall Street an early lift. But the technology rally did not last.

The S and P five hundred closed at seven thousand five hundred and forty one point four, down about half a percent. The Nasdaq Composite fell just over one percent. The Nasdaq one hundred finished just below twenty eight thousand. The Dow finished slightly higher.

The equal weight S and P five hundred actually gained roughly zero point seven percent. Most of the damage came from large technology companies with heavy index weight. This matters because a weak headline index does not necessarily mean the whole market is being sold.

Two simple reference points are worth watching. Can the S and P five hundred regain seventy six hundred? Can the Nasdaq one hundred get back above twenty eight thousand and stay there? These are observation levels, not guaranteed support.

Oracle Raised Twenty Billion Dollars at Once

Oracle is at the centre of the funding debate. On October twenty eighth, it told investors that additional market financing through fiscal twenty thirty would be limited to twenty billion dollars, excluding debt refinancing. Half was planned as equity. Half was planned as debt.

Just five days later, Oracle launched both parts at once. Ten billion dollars in new shares and ten billion dollars in bonds. The stock was priced at one hundred and sixteen dollars a share. The bonds run from twenty twenty nine through twenty forty six, with coupon rates starting at seven percent and reaching eight point six five percent.

Oracle did not exceed the stated limit. It used the entire allowance far sooner than investors might have expected. New shares dilute existing ownership. New debt requires interest payments, even if a data centre takes longer to produce cash.

Oracle shares fell by nearly nine percent on Monday. The company expects free cash flow to turn positive only in the second half of fiscal twenty twenty eight. It targets at least thirty billion dollars for fiscal twenty twenty nine. For now, the spending is certain, but the cash recovery is still a forecast.

This is also why the earlier statement about bonds needs care. In February, Oracle expected not to issue more bonds during calendar twenty twenty six after a planned sale. That was a forecast, not a binding ban. Watch what the company actually collects from cloud customers and what it has to pay to fund the buildout.

Apple Gives Investors Another Kind of Technology Exposure

Apple reported roughly one hundred and fourteen and a half billion dollars in quarterly revenue. That was up twelve percent from a year earlier. Earnings reached two dollars and ten cents a share, an increase of about fourteen percent.

The iPhone business generated about fifty seven and a half billion dollars. Services reached about thirty one and a half billion, although its growth rate slowed to just under ten percent.

One detail matters. Tariff refunds helped Apple's earnings by about five cents a share. The same refunds lifted reported gross margin by around one percentage point. That is a real accounting benefit, but it should not be assumed to repeat.

Apple generated about twenty eight billion dollars of operating cash flow in the quarter and returned a similar amount to shareholders. It has a very different funding profile from an A I infrastructure company issuing both shares and debt. The test now is whether businesses with reliable cash generation can hold up better than heavily financed growth companies. If even those stocks sell off sharply, technology valuations may be undergoing a wider reset.

Marvell and Lumentum Are Combining

Marvell has agreed to acquire Lumentum in an all stock deal. Every Lumentum share will be exchanged for four point five Marvell shares.

Current Marvell holders are expected to own about two thirds of the combined company. Lumentum holders will own about one third. The companies expect the deal to close in the first half of twenty twenty eight, if shareholders and regulators approve it.

Marvell brings custom chips and electrical connectivity. Lumentum brings lasers and optical networking technology. They could make large A I clusters more efficient by connecting computing systems faster and with less power. Management sees more than one billion dollars in potential annual cost savings. But those are projected savings. Investors still face a long integration process, regulatory uncertainty and a fixed exchange ratio that links the two share prices.

The business combination may make sense. That does not tell us whether either stock is cheap today.

A Seasonal Rally Still Needs Cash Flow

November through January has historically been a better stretch for American stocks. Yet seasonal patterns do not pay interest on bonds or fund a data centre. In a stronger market, Oracle completes its funding, credit pressure eases and customer payments turn into cash. Technology stocks then rebuild their upward trend.

In a mixed market, most stocks stay reasonably healthy while big technology shares trade sideways. In a weaker market, financing costs keep rising and selling spreads into the broader index. For a portfolio concentrated in technology or semiconductors, start with position size and near term events. A put option or collar may reduce some downside, but protection has a cost. Sometimes reducing exposure is simpler.

The question has changed. Demand for A I may be enormous. How soon does that demand generate enough cash to cover the investment?
