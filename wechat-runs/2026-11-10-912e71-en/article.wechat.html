Inflation Keeps the Funding Pressure On

On November tenth, the Bureau of Labor Statistics released October's consumer price index. Headline prices rose zero point four percent month over month, against zero point three percent in a Reuters poll of economists. Core prices increased zero point three percent, compared with zero point two percent in the same survey.

Shelter rose zero point three percent and contributed more than thirty percent of the overall increase. Food rose zero point five percent, while energy gained zero point seven percent. These are seasonally adjusted monthly changes, and price pressure remains sticky. The report gives investors little reason to assume monetary policy is about to ease.

Fed Governor Waller said core monthly inflation around zero point three percent or higher would warrant considering fifty basis points of tightening. That is his conditional position, rather than a decision by the Federal Open Market Committee. The distinction matters when interpreting what the inflation report means for policy.

The ten year Treasury yield moved above five percent during the session. The Treasury's daily reference reading around three thirty p.m. was four point nine eight percent, up ten basis points from four point eight eight percent the previous day. For A I infrastructure built before customers pay, higher rates affect both borrowing costs and the present value of future revenue.

The equity market did not show broad panic. The S and P five hundred closed at seven thousand five hundred and twenty point nine three, down zero point three eight percent, while the Nasdaq one hundred fell zero point six zero percent. Regular session volume declined from the previous day, and the volatility index also eased to nineteen point four six.

Index weakness and funding stress at individual highly leveraged companies need separate judgments. Borrowing terms and customer receipts will say more about the next stage of risk. One day's price move cannot show whether companies will turn construction spending into cash on schedule.

SoftBank's Equipment Loss Needs a Cash Timeline

On November tenth, SoftBank reported a net loss attributable to shareholders of about one point one two trillion yen for July through September. Its financial statements for the six months ended September thirtieth also recognized an equipment procurement loss of three point seven three billion dollars. That loss needs to be distinguished from cash spent during one quarter.

The loss comprised two point eight five billion dollars on previously paid, nonrefundable advances, plus eight hundred and eighty million dollars in contract modification and settlement costs. Reserving equipment capacity carries a cost when requirements and construction plans change. The total does not mean another three point seven three billion dollars left the company in a single quarter.

SoftBank says cash payments associated with the eight hundred and eighty million dollars are expected from October through December and beyond. Future purchases could earn conditional rebates, but the amounts and timing remain uncertain. No related asset was recognized at the end of September.

Paid deposits, recognized losses, and future payments are different stages of the same commitments. Adding them together would double count costs, while deducting unrealized rebates would recognize a recovery too early. The next checks are whether procurement changes end and project specifications, payments, and delivery stabilize.

Companies pay early or reserve manufacturing slots to secure equipment for A I expansion. If power plans, equipment requirements, or project schedules change later, those commitments may leave less flexibility than expected. Cancellation terms, refundable amounts, and modification costs therefore matter alongside the size of a project. The largest signed commitment does not establish the lowest eventual construction cost.

GE Vernova Has Not Confirmed an Order Loss

The SoftBank news brought renewed scrutiny to power equipment suppliers. That market reaction needs to be separated from confirmed operating effects. Reuters reported on November tenth that a GE Vernova spokesperson said SoftBank's recent announcement had no positive or negative effect on the company.

SoftBank also denied a default and said there had been no discussion of reducing the Ohio project. The evidence does not establish a confirmed order cancellation at GE Vernova. It also does not establish a sudden disappearance of power equipment demand.

GE Vernova sells power, electrification, and wind equipment and services. Advances, manufacturing, and delivery schedules affect its cash collection, so it should not be valued as though it were a regulated utility with stable returns. Reserved manufacturing slots, firm orders, and revenue from delivered products need separate checks.

The next evidence is whether order and delivery guidance changes, advances convert into revenue, and contract changes enter formal disclosures. Those are the operating facts investors can monitor. Long term returns depend on orders, delivery, profit, and working capital together.

CoreWeave's Backlog Still Needs Near Term Funding

In its November tenth third quarter results, CoreWeave reported one hundred and thirty two point five billion dollars in revenue backlog at the end of September. Revenue recognition depends on delivering capacity and making services available. Backlog is neither balance sheet cash nor an amount that can be netted directly against debt.

On a consistent basis, debt carrying amounts rose from thirty five point zero six eight billion dollars at the end of June to forty six point eight seven three billion dollars at the end of September. That is an increase of eleven point eight zero five billion dollars in one quarter. The change in the debt balance is not an equivalent net financing cash inflow.

Both periods combine current and noncurrent recourse and nonrecourse debt. They exclude separately reported finance lease and operating lease liabilities. The debt total therefore does not represent every future payment obligation.

Current debt was about ten point five seven two billion dollars at the end of September. Cash and equivalents were about four point one two two billion dollars. Including both categories of restricted cash brings the cash total to roughly six point four zero four billion dollars.

Restricted funds cannot be assumed available to repay any debt. Equally, subtracting one day's cash balance from obligations due over the next twelve months does not establish an immediate shortage. Operating receipts, new financing, advances, and construction spending will keep changing the position. Their timing matters.

CoreWeave expects fourth quarter interest expense of one point zero five to one point one five billion dollars. That is expense guidance, not cash interest already paid. Multiplying the midpoint by four gives an annualized pace, rather than a full year bill already incurred.

Construction and interest require funding before backlog is recognized as revenue. The useful checks are borrowing costs, collateral and draw conditions, and whether delivery delays also postpone revenue. Large orders alone cannot prove safety, just as rising debt alone cannot predict bankruptcy.

Management offered a potential buffer. It expects about nine billion dollars in customer advances and deposits over the next three months and plans financing such as loans matched to customer contracts. The company release, Reuters, and MarketWatch reported the expectation, but the money cannot be added to September's cash balance in advance.

Watch whether receipts arrive on schedule, financing closes on affordable terms, and delivery progresses. Meeting those conditions would ease near term funding concerns. Delayed receipts, more expensive borrowing, and slower delivery would compound the pressure.

Anthropic Starts Its Roadshow and an Office Push

Anthropic launched its initial public offering roadshow on November tenth and filed an amended prospectus, proposing to trade on Nasdaq under A N T. Reuters reported an expected trading start on November nineteenth. Final pricing and the offering remain pending, so a proposed listing is not a completed listing.

Its new office products have different release status. Sheets and Whiteboards remain in beta, while the former Dashboards became Claude Data and is generally available. The suite cannot all be treated as mature software.

Sheets addresses some spreadsheet and collaboration tasks, but the official help page still lists limits involving macros, V B A, Power Query, and other functions. That creates another route into office workflows without establishing damage to Microsoft three sixty five revenue. Microsoft and Anthropic also have investment, cloud, and model distribution relationships.

Application rivalry can coexist with infrastructure cooperation. Microsoft's intraday share price move does not establish a loss of paid office seats. Investors still need to see what happens to paying business users and complex workflows.

The next tests are user retention, compatibility and migration costs, and measurable changes to Microsoft's seats, renewals, and monetization. An initial public offering can broaden financing channels, while product delivery and commercialization still need to be established separately. Shares can price competitive expectations quickly. Revenue effects need time and financial results, rather than a conclusion drawn from one launch event.

Bank of America Keeps Its Buy Ratings After Top Pick Changes

Bank of America removed Nvidia, Micron, and Lam Research from its U S One investment list on November tenth while retaining Buy ratings. That list is a narrower selection within the bank's Buy rated coverage. Removal from the group and a downgrade to Sell are different decisions.

Without a publicly stated explanation from the bank, outside views on valuation, governance, or capital spending remain outside interpretations. They cannot be presented as the bank's stated reasons. Those views also do not establish that the investment case for the whole semiconductor sector has failed.

With the S and P five hundred at seven thousand five hundred and twenty point nine three, the first nearby reference is the session low around seven thousand four hundred and seventy. The next is the previous session's low near seven thousand four hundred and twenty two. Losing those areas as selling broadens and volume rises would weaken the recovery case. Stabilizing prices and yields that stop climbing would allow it to be tested further.

The seven thousand to seven thousand one hundred range belongs to a deeper pullback scenario, well below the current close. It is not support being tested now, and it is not a guaranteed bottom. Near term price behavior and broader selling conditions need to be assessed first.

Demand, contracts, delivery, and cash require separate checks. Strong demand does not make financing costs irrelevant, and large contracts do not prove all spending is funded. Procurement losses and top pick list changes also need to be judged on their own terms.

Tracking yields, actual receipts, loan terms, and delivery gives investors a more concrete test. It helps distinguish price moves that amplify concern from an increase in fundamental risk. No single order total, loss figure, or analyst list decision answers that question alone.
