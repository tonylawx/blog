Nvidia Reserves Memory Capacity for Future Shipments

Nvidia reported fiscal third quarter sales of one hundred fifteen point eight four four billion dollars on November seventeenth. That was twenty percent higher than the prior quarter and a hundred and three percent higher than a year earlier. The consensus cited by Reuters was a hundred and nine point one one billion dollars.

Data center sales were a hundred and seven point six billion dollars. Adjusted earnings were two dollars and sixty eight cents per share and adjusted gross margin was seventy four point nine percent. Free cash flow was forty three point seven seven seven billion dollars. The timing of tax payments also affected the change from the prior quarter.

For the fourth quarter, Nvidia expects revenue of about a hundred and thirty billion dollars, plus or minus three percent. That compares with a consensus of a hundred and twenty five point two five billion dollars. The quarter has fourteen weeks and the forecast excludes data center compute sales in China. Nvidia expects adjusted gross margin of seventy one to seventy two percent.

On the earnings call, management raised its revenue growth outlook for fiscal twenty twenty eight from about seventy percent to about seventy five percent. The company added a hundred and fifty billion dollars to its buyback authorization in September. A total of two hundred twenty seven point three billion dollars of authorization remained at quarter end.

Supply and capacity commitments rose nearly forty percent from two hundred and seventy nine billion dollars to three hundred and ninety billion. Multiyear memory purchases drove the increase. Suppliers get a basis for planning production, and Nvidia secures memory capacity for future shipments.

About three hundred sixty four billion dollars, or ninety three percent, is scheduled through fiscal twenty twenty nine. Twenty six billion falls after that. Nvidia did not disclose how much went to each supplier.

Nvidia also reported thirty two billion dollars in purchase agreements with emerging cloud providers. Those partners have to secure financing, deploy operational capacity and pass Nvidia's acceptance checks. Nvidia can reduce or cancel the commitments if the partners don't meet the conditions.

Maximum gross guarantee exposure rose from a hundred and eight point five billion dollars in the prior disclosure to a hundred and eighteen billion. The prior figure already included Ohio. That includes a hundred and five billion for the Ohio campus first disclosed in August, nine and a half billion for power and systems, and three and a half billion for other cloud sites. Nvidia had made no payments under those guarantees as of the disclosure.

The Ohio guarantee applies in phases as buildings become ready. Nvidia expects the first phase in fiscal twenty twenty nine. Financing and acceptance of operational capacity still determine when emerging cloud providers can turn those purchase agreements into delivery and revenue.

Nvidia's Search for a New Chief and the Muddy Waters Short

Chair Harvey Jones said the board has no plans at this point to bring Jensen Huang back as chief executive. Colette Kress remains the interim chief executive and chief financial officer while Nvidia considers internal and external candidates. The board also formed a committee to oversee national security and export controls.

For shareholders, the next questions are how the new chief executive handles export compliance and whether financing reviews for new customers tighten.

Before Nvidia reported earnings on November seventeenth, Muddy Waters Research said it already held a short position tied to Nvidia securities or derivatives. The report raised questions about chip shipments linked to China and export compliance. The firm said it planned to start closing most, and possibly all, of its position when it released the report, to manage risk. The disclosure gives no entry date or position size. How much the firm actually covered remains unclear.

Buyers Face Another Test Around Seven Twenty Eight

Bank of America's November survey showed fund managers cutting their net overweight in technology from thirty seven percent to twelve percent. That was the largest reduction since June of two thousand six. Respondents still held a net overweight.

Goldman Sachs prime brokerage clients' technology long to short ratio fell to one point five zero. That's the lowest in a series that began in twenty sixteen. These samples show less appetite for tech exposure, and marketwide short positions remain a separate question.

If the fund tracking the S&P 500 slips through seven hundred twenty eight dollars and breaks the November seventeenth low of seven hundred twenty seven dollars, watch whether it can recover. Failure to reclaim those levels would weaken the case for a rebound.

If the S&P 500 reclaims seven thousand five hundred and fifty and more sectors participate, short covering could help the advance. A bounce led by a handful of chip stocks still needs buyers once the covering is over. Anthropic is expected to begin trading on November nineteenth, subject to completion of the offering. Demand for the new listing and follow through in the broader market can help gauge risk appetite.
