The Indexes Waited; PCE Did Not Help

Wednesday, August 26, the tape handed the session to two events: July PCE in the morning and Nvidia after the close.

The Dow fell 0.21 percent to 53,464. SPX was almost unchanged at 7,676. Nasdaq slipped 0.06 percent to 26,136.

SOXX closed 515.40, up only 0.26 percent. Tuesday’s chip bounce, built on lower yields and crude, was given back. The indexes did not price first. They waited.

July PCE did not give the tape a tailwind. Headline prices rose 0.2 percent month over month and 3.7 percent year over year, a touch hotter than about 0.1 percent and 3.6 percent.

Core rose 0.2 percent and 3.3 percent year over year, in line, but still stuck at 3.3 percent and has not made a real move toward 2 percent since April.

Personal income rose 0.4 percent. Spending rose 0.2 percent. Real spending was about flat. Households are still spending in nominal terms. They are not buying more goods.

The 10-year yield backed up from about 4.64 percent the prior day to about 4.66 percent. Sticky inflation, stalled real consumption, and no ease from rates.

Nvidia closed 209.66 in the cash session, down 1.59 percent, after trading as low as 209.23, opening 212.64 and high 213.60.

After seven down days, Tuesday’s bounce from 208 to 213 was handed back. Into the print, that was more about clearing space for after-hours than a direction.

Tuesday’s bounce was not treated as confirmation on Wednesday. Kevin Warsh speaks at Jackson Hole on Friday. Wednesday’s real pricing started after the close.

After-hours prints are not Thursday’s cash open. Extended hours bounced toward 219, about 5 percent above the cash close. That is extended hours, not Thursday cash.

The cash session has to re-price it. Semi multiples were already thin versus rates. After Nvidia rewrote growth as customer financing, the stock is more sensitive to the 10-year, not less.

1 · EARNINGS

Revenue Made a New High; Guidance Goes to 108 Billion

The second quarter ended July 26. Revenue was 96.2 billion dollars, up 18 percent quarter over quarter and 106 percent year over year.

Company guidance had been about 91 billion plus or minus 2 percent, so the ceiling was only about 92.8 billion. The Street was near 92.2 billion. The print cleared the company’s own cap.

Sequential dollar growth was about 14.6 billion, a record, and well above the roughly 9.4 billion implied by the prior guide.

Prior sequential adds were about 10.3 billion, 11.1 billion, and 13.5 billion. This quarter did not slow.

Data Center was 89.0 billion, also up 18 percent sequentially and 117 percent year over year, about 92 percent of revenue. Edge Computing was 7.2 billion, up 13 percent.

GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin were both 75.0 percent. From the first quarter of fiscal 2027, non-GAAP already includes stock-based compensation, so the comparison with older non-GAAP is not clean.

Adjusted EPS was 2.22 versus about 2.09 to 2.10. GAAP EPS was 2.46.

Third-quarter revenue guidance is 108.0 billion plus or minus 2 percent, about 105.8 billion at the floor and 110.2 billion at the cap.

The Street had been near 104 billion, so the midpoint is about 4 billion above consensus. China Data Center compute revenue is still assumed at zero. That is the third consecutive outlook written that way.

GAAP operating expenses were 8.4 billion this quarter. Next quarter is guided to about 9.2 billion GAAP and 9.0 billion non-GAAP.

Vera Rubin is in full production, with racks at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle, and Nebius. Blackwell Ultra remains the workhorse this quarter.

Management’s line is that AI has reached an inflection, compute is revenue, and demand is still accelerating. This quarter cleared on dollar adds. The percentage rate will fade because the base is already huge.

The test is whether the next quarter can add another 100 billion run-rate of new revenue.

The 108 billion guide implies about 11.8 billion of sequential add. That is a step down from this quarter’s 14.6 billion, but still above what the Street had written.

While supply is the constraint, the company knows the floor better than the ceiling, so beating the guide again is not proof that new demand was found.

If the constraint flips from supply to demand, it will show up in guidance first, not in product already shipped. The guide still beats the Street.

The shape changed. Volume is still given, margin starts to give, operating expenses step up, and China stays at zero.

2 · MARKET

Thick Profits, Thin Cash; Receivables Swelled First

GAAP net income was 59.7 billion dollars, only a touch above last quarter’s 58.3 billion, up about 2 percent. Revenue rose 18 percent.

Operating income rose from 53.5 billion to 63.7 billion, also about 19 percent. Net income barely moved because other income was already thick last quarter, and this quarter booked about 7.8 billion of other income, including about 7.8 billion of equity-security gains.

Operating cash flow was only 24.1 billion, about 40 percent of net income. Last quarter operating cash was 50.3 billion, converting at about 86 percent.

Free cash flow was 21.3 billion versus about 48.6 billion last quarter. Accounts receivable rose 22.3 billion in one quarter, to 63.1 billion.

On this quarter’s revenue, days outstanding stretched from about 45 to about 60. Inventories rose another 5.8 billion, to 31.6 billion. The income statement is thick. The cash statement first parked the goods with customers.

In the same quarter the company issued about 24.9 billion of net debt. Long-term debt rose from 7.5 billion to 32.4 billion.

Buybacks were 19.7 billion and dividends 6.0 billion, about 26.0 billion returned, with about 99 billion of authorization left. Free cash flow did not cover that return. Debt filled the gap.

Growth did not stop. Growth pulled working capital and the balance sheet wider with it.

If shipments bunched at quarter-end, days can stretch temporarily and retrace next quarter. If days stay near 60, terms themselves changed, and financing pressure moves one step up the supply chain.

The old question was whether the vendor still had to guarantee the customer. The sharper question now: the chips have shipped. When does the cash come back.

Cash conversion worsened. Shipment timing, collection terms, and customers using chips as financing assets are three layers the statements do not split.

What is visible is three facts: mark-to-market gains inside profit, receivables swelling, and new debt funding the buyback. Stack those three, and 59.7 billion of net income cannot be read as cash in hand.

3 · MARKET

Guarantees Rewritten as 500 Billion; Gross Margin Steps Down

The company also said it is building independent compute financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR, aiming to mobilize more than 500 billion of third-party capital for AI infrastructure once agreements are definitive.

The old worry was that the vendor would guarantee customer purchases. The new wording is that third-party funds buy the compute assets. The agreements are not fully closed. The figure is an intention, not cash received.

If it works, demand can still expand and less of it sits on Nvidia’s own balance sheet. If it goes wrong, a chip company starts to look like a credit intermediary. The issue moved from the print to the financing structure.

Gross-margin guidance steps from this quarter’s 75.0 percent to 74.0 percent, plus or minus 50 basis points. That is the first explicit one-point cut in this cycle. Memory cost is the stated pressure.

On 108 billion of revenue, one point is about 1 billion of gross profit. After hours the stock dumped then recovered, toward 219, about 5 percent above the cash close.

The last four quarters often rhymed: the number cleared, and the next cash session gave the premium back.

Keep the decision layers separate. Fact: second-quarter revenue 96.2 billion, Data Center 89.0 billion, operating income 63.7 billion, GAAP net income 59.7 billion, operating cash 24.1 billion, free cash flow 21.3 billion, receivables 63.1 billion, inventories 31.6 billion, debt issued about 24.9 billion, third-quarter revenue 108.0 billion plus or minus 2 percent, gross margin 74.0 percent plus or minus 50 basis points, China Data Center compute at zero for a third consecutive outlook.

Those line up with company materials. Inference: why cash conversion worsened is not split in the statements.

Judgment: 210 is where the facts were digested. It is not confirmation of a new trend. After-hours 219 is extended hours. Do not write it as support before Thursday’s cash open.

The tree is clear. Bullish: receivables retrace, 108 billion can be delivered, third-party financing closes, and 210 holds.

Neutral: the beats continue, cash conversion stays thin, the stock digests around 210, and semis follow rather than lead.

Bearish: days outstanding do not come back, the financing platform stays a letter of intent, gross margin keeps sliding, and 196 to 200 becomes the map again.

Watch Thursday cash first, then Friday’s Warsh remarks. The revenue gate cleared. Cash and financing are what this trade is pricing now.

Adding size just because the print beat does not hold. The beat already happened. Wednesday cash did not pay a premium.

The add conditions are: days outstanding retrace, third-quarter deliveries make 108 billion real, and 210 holds. Shorting the growth story is over does not hold either.

Growth is still there. What changed is the accounting quality of that growth. Longs need the cash to follow. Shorts need the terms and the financing commitment to fail to match. Neither is settled today.
