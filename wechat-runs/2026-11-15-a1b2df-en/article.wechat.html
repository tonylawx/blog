The resignation repriced governance before the order book

On November twelfth, Nvidia's board said cofounder Jensen Huang had resigned as chief executive and as a director, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim chief executive and stayed on as chief financial officer. Harvey Jones became the company's first board chair. The board said it would look inside and outside for a permanent successor. Cable News Network, Axios, and Business Insider all carried the appointment the same day. The stock traded as low as one hundred forty eight dollars and eighty cents and closed at one hundred sixty six dollars and nineteen cents, down four point three percent from the prior close of one hundred seventy three dollars and seventy three cents.

The ledger splits in two. Goldman Sachs analyst James Schneider kept a buy rating and cut his target to two hundred twenty dollars from three hundred dollars. The note treats the next twelve to eighteen months of earnings as cushioned by the existing backlog, so the direct hit to profits is described as limited. What got repriced at once was the multiple. Customers, the foundry, and export permissions had often pointed at one person. Contracts can still ship. The multiple waits until a new chief executive resigns those relationships. From the November sixth close of one hundred eighty six dollars and sixty four cents to the November thirteenth close of one hundred sixty two dollars and eighty five cents, the stock fell about twelve point seven percent in five sessions.

The two ends of the chain do not want the same thing. Nvidia has to show that the roadmap, shipments, and gross margin do not get rewritten because one person left. Microsoft, Google, and Amazon want clusters that can be powered on, not a succession press release. The board wants a search it can explain to shareholders. Kress is signing both as finance chief and as operator. Customers will not add orders because a statement says operations continue. They will read whether the November seventeenth outlook still stands behind the order book from before the resignation.

Set against the management shocks around the telecom spending peak in the late nineteen nineties, fiber contracts were still on the books then, and what fell first was the multiple investors would pay for the people running them. Racks ship by the quarter. The trust discount moves by the day. This time the cushion is written as a twelve to eighteen month backlog, not dark fiber already in the ground. The multiple can fall without revenue recognition collapsing the same day.

The check sits in the next two quarters, not in the name of a permanent chief executive. If November seventeenth keeps the following two quarters inside the old frame, the governance discount is still in the multiple and not yet in orders. If guidance is cut, or management stops describing that backlog as cover, the cushion is falsified. An internal memo reported by Business Insider puts an all hands meeting on November eighteenth. That is a calendar item. It does not replace the numbers.

The shovel seller cut its own target and still wrote a check

On November tenth, Anthropic updated its registration statement. The offering is eight hundred twenty five million Class A shares, priced from seventy to seventy eight dollars. Reuters and TechCrunch both wrote that the top of the range implies a fully diluted value of about one point four trillion dollars, and that the offering itself would raise up to about sixty four point four billion dollars. The same filing arranges a twenty six billion dollar private placement of non voting Class N shares at the offering price. Buyers include Google, Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and Taiwan Semiconductor. Both reports put Google and Nvidia at about ten billion dollars each. Reuters said trading is expected to start on the Nasdaq on November nineteenth under the letters A, N, T.

The structure matters more than the headline value. In the same week Nvidia cut its own target from three hundred dollars to two hundred twenty and agreed to put up about ten billion dollars for a model lab's non voting stock. The chip designer is marking down its own equity and funding a customer's balance sheet at the same time. The other five buyers sum to six billion dollars. Their separate checks are not split out here.

Profit sits in different places on the chain. Nvidia, Taiwan Semiconductor, and Broadcom earn the gross margin on chips, wafers, and interconnects. Anthropic receives cash it can spend on compute, payroll, and the next training run. The supplier's check lands on the customer's books, and a large part of it can come back as spending on the supplier's cards. One round of spending is written as two entries. Depreciation still sits with whoever buys the accelerators. Non voting means the ten billion dollars buys a financial exposure, not a board seat.

Physical delivery did not stop for the governance headline. The board said customer commitments and the product roadmap continue. If that holds, advanced packaging, liquid cooled racks, and signed clusters still follow the old contracts. The discount hits the multiple. It does not hit a rack that is already ordered. If the search runs long, more of the next orders could shift toward accelerators Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom already sell. That is share moving, not demand vanishing on November twelfth.

Two things can falsify this. First, whether Nvidia completes the roughly ten billion dollar Class N purchase at the amount in the filing. Second, whether the language after November seventeenth treats the money as customer financing rather than a financial investment unrelated to supply. If the placement is cut, or the report stops calling the backlog a twelve to eighteen month cushion, this section has to change.

2 · EARNINGS

Model revenue is rising, and the valuation still leans on suppliers

The same November tenth update said Anthropic's run rate revenue reached eighty one billion dollars as of October thirty first. Reuters and TechCrunch both cite that figure. After a financing round this past May the company was valued at nine hundred sixty five billion dollars. The top of this range is about one point four trillion dollars fully diluted. On an undiluted basis Reuters put the value at about one trillion dollars. Revenue is higher. The offering value sits in a range suppliers were willing to buy at the offering price.

That run rate is contracts still running, not free cash already in the bank. One point four trillion dollars is about seventeen times that run rate. One trillion dollars is about twelve times. The multiple buys the claim that revenue can keep stepping up. It does not buy profits already earned. Suppliers buying non voting shares write part of the demand side in as shareholders early. What enterprise customers still pay for is usage, evaluation, permissions, and human review after a mistake. Those costs do not leave the contract because Google or Nvidia bought shares.

The incentives pull in different directions. Google is putting up about ten billion dollars and also selling its own models. Nvidia sells cards to every lab and holds a financial exposure to Anthropic. A product that is only a thin interface gets squeezed by model price cuts and by the cloud vendors' own agents. What remains is permissions, audit, and where the data sits inside a workflow. Each time open weights get closer to a closed model, another layer of usage premium comes off. A supplier's equity check does not lock that premium in place.

Set against the cloud price cuts early in the last decade, compute prices were marked down again and again, software margins were repriced, and what remained was workflow and data location. Model calls are becoming metered infrastructure. The difference is that chip suppliers now appear on the customer's shareholder list. The dark fiber of the late nineteen nineties was supply ahead of demand. This time demand is still signing clusters, and the equity line writes buyer and seller into the same prospectus.

The next two quarters only need two items already in the documents. One is whether the next revenue disclosure after listing still supports the October thirty first snapshot of eighty one billion dollars. The other is whether the seventy to seventy eight dollar range is formally changed. On November eleventh the Financial Times, citing people familiar with the process, said bankers were discussing a price above seventy eight dollars. That is a report, not a filing. If the final price is below seventy dollars, or the run rate falls clearly off the path in the prospectus, this section has to be rewritten.

3 · MARKET

Tuesday checks the order book, not the successor's name

The week split this spending into two sheets. One is Nvidia's orders and gross margin. The November twelfth resignation did not strike them out of the contracts, and the November thirteenth close of one hundred sixty two dollars and eighty five cents did not finish below the low of one hundred forty eight dollars and eighty cents. The other sheet is the multiple investors will pay for a roadmap still explained by the same person. That sheet changed the day of the resignation. The prospectus shows the companies that sell the cards using their own cash to become shareholders of the model lab at the public offering price.

Over the next two quarters, three numbers can be checked. Whether Nvidia's outlook on November seventeenth still sits inside the old frame. Whether the roughly ten billion dollar share purchase is completed at the amount in the filing. Whether Anthropic's next revenue figure after listing still matches the October thirty first run rate of eighty one billion dollars. If two of the three fail, the orders are loosening too. The first day move on November nineteenth does not answer those three.

A founder leaving changes the multiple. A supplier buying in changes where the cash on the customer's balance sheet comes from. Neither has rewritten clusters that are already signed. Watching only listing day misses the harder check, which is whether management still stands behind that order book on November seventeenth.
