0 · MARKET

The indexes recovered most of their early losses

U.S. stocks sold off sharply on Wednesday, November twenty fifth. They then staged a fast rebound around midday. The S and P five hundred fell as much as three point two percent intraday. It closed down just zero point three percent. The Nasdaq Composite briefly fell about four point six percent. It finished down zero point six percent.

The Dow gained zero point three percent. The Russell two thousand rose zero point seven percent. About three quarters of S and P five hundred stocks finished higher. The weakness remained concentrated in large technology and A I related shares.

The semiconductor ETF fell more than eight percent from the previous close at its intraday low. It touched one hundred and eighteen dollars and eighteen cents. It then recovered and closed at one hundred and twenty seven dollars and ninety eight cents. That was only down half a percent for the day.

The VIX reached thirty four point nine five intraday. It closed at twenty eight point two nine. Fear eased from the high, but volatility remained elevated.

1 · MARKET

Three possible catalysts explain the timing, not the cause

The market began to rebound quickly around noon Eastern time. Three possible catalysts were discussed. First, the FCC authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million orbital compute modules. This is an authorization, not one million data centres already built and earning revenue.

Second, Michael Burry said he had closed the remaining half of his Nasdaq one hundred put position. He also said his portfolio was temporarily almost entirely long. He held about twenty five percent in cash. That is a real update from one investor. It does not prove that the whole market covered its shorts at the same time.

Third, some investors pointed to possible unwinding of yen funded carry trades. But the daily dollar yen range did not confirm a major move large enough to explain the reversal. My read is that the move looked more like short covering after a sudden selloff. Short term money may have amplified the move in a thin liquidity window.

That can explain a fast bounce. It does not prove that institutions have rebuilt long exposure. A durable rebound needs three things: shorts covering, institutions adding net longs, and fundamentals improving.

2 · MACRO

Inflation is sticky, while growth and jobs remain resilient

October headline P C E inflation rose zero point three percent month over month. That was slower than September's zero point five percent. The annual rate increased to three point eight percent. Core P C E rose zero point three percent month over month. Its annual rate increased from three point one to three point three percent. That was above the market expectation of three point two percent.

The slower monthly headline number is a relief. But annual inflation is still moving higher. Core inflation is not cooling enough to give the Federal Reserve much room to ease policy. The second estimate of third quarter G D P was revised up to four point three percent. The advance estimate was three point four percent. The revision mainly reflected stronger investment and consumer spending.

The government did not attribute sixty five percent of G D P growth to A I investment. Its report listed investment, consumer spending, and government spending as contributors. We should not turn an outside estimate into an official figure.

The labour market also remains firm. Initial jobless claims fell to one hundred and eighty nine thousand for the week ended November twenty first. Continuing claims fell to one point six nine seven million for the week ended November fourteenth.

Hotter inflation, stronger growth, and lower initial claims make an early shift to easier policy harder to justify. High multiple technology stocks still face a challenging discount rate backdrop.

3 · FOCUS

Nvidia's A A rating is on negative watch

On November twenty fifth, S and P Global put Nvidia's A A issuer and senior debt ratings on CreditWatch Negative. The review can last up to ninety days. The concerns focus on board independence, management oversight, and succession planning. This is not a statement that Nvidia is struggling to pay its debts.

S and P expects any potential downgrade to be limited to one notch. That could change if further investigations or disclosures harm the company's reputation or business outlook. Nvidia's own balance sheet is one question. The financing dependence of downstream compute operators is another. Some lenders may reassess loans linked to customers, equipment, or contracts.

That does not mean every downstream loan will automatically become more expensive. There is no verified public evidence for one blanket guarantee amount that would be triggered immediately. The timing matters. The sharp market reversal began around midday. The S and P rating action came out at about one thirty eight in the afternoon Eastern time. It did not cause the initial rebound.

4 · RISK

Positioning pressure may be easing, but confirmation is ahead

The VIX fell from thirty four point nine five to twenty eight point two nine. The sharpest fear eased, but the VIX was still up more than seven percent on the day. Burry closing his puts may reduce some selling pressure. Seasonal buying after Thanksgiving could also add marginal demand. Neither signal replaces confirmation from trading volume and price.

The S P Y ETF closed at seven hundred and sixteen dollars and seventy cents. Seven hundred and fifty three dollars is about five percent above that close. A high volume move above seven hundred and fifty three dollars, followed by a hold, would strengthen the case for a year end rally. Until then, the market remains range bound.

The Q Q Q ETF closed at six hundred and thirty three dollars and ninety three cents. The six hundred and thirty three to six hundred and thirty six dollar area is the near term pivot. The more important lower support is around five hundred and eighty five dollars. Resistance sits above seven hundred dollars. The Q Q Q ETF needs follow through buying to improve the rebound structure.

5 · MOMENTUM

S O X X has room to rebound, but the technical bear market is not over

The semiconductor ETF closed at one hundred and twenty seven dollars and ninety eight cents. It touched one hundred and eighteen dollars and eighteen cents intraday. The first rebound zone runs from one hundred and thirty seven to one hundred and forty four dollars. That is roughly seven to thirteen percent above the close.

The next reference is one hundred and sixty three dollars. That is about twenty seven percent higher. The one hundred and eighty to one hundred and eighty one dollar area is a major ceiling. These are potential rebound zones, not promised targets. Short covering may be enough for the first area. Higher levels need improving earnings expectations and stronger fund flows.

The ETF remains about twenty seven percent below its October twenty eighth high. It is still in a technical bear market. A move to one hundred and sixty three dollars would not end that condition.

Taiwan Semiconductor closed at three hundred and forty two dollars and thirty four cents. It touched three hundred and forty three dollars and seventeen cents intraday. The three hundred and forty two to three hundred and forty three dollar area is near the top of its prior trading range.

The stock tested that level, but the close did not confirm a decisive breakout. A close above the band and a successful retest would make the move more credible. Netflix closed at fifty nine dollars and forty one cents, up zero point nine two percent. Volume reached about forty five point six million shares. That was higher than the previous session's thirty six point one million.

Volume improved, but one stronger session does not confirm a break of the short term downtrend. Price needs to close above that trend line and hold the retest.

6 · RISK

What would confirm a real return of risk appetite

Three things need to improve together. First, the S P Y ETF needs to break above seven hundred and fifty three dollars on stronger volume. The Q Q Q ETF needs to hold six hundred and thirty three to six hundred and thirty six dollars. The semiconductor ETF should at least reclaim one hundred and thirty seven to one hundred and forty four dollars.

Second, volatility and credit pressure need to ease. The VIX should not remain stuck in the high twenties. Credit spreads across data centre borrowers also need to come down. Third, operating fundamentals need to support the valuations. A I customer spending, compute utilization, and earnings forecasts must stop deteriorating.

A quick bounce with high volatility and tight credit still looks like a positioning repair. A stronger advance needs better volume, easier credit, and stable earnings expectations. Immediate selling pressure has eased. A sustained new bull trend is not confirmed yet.
