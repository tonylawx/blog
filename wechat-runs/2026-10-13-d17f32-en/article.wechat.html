Four indexes, four ways to fall

Tuesday's four main indexes all finished slightly lower. They did not fall the same way.

The Dow and the Russell opened higher and then gave it back. The S&P 500 was whipped around and still closed a fraction lower. The Nasdaq gapped down, took most of the loss back, and then sat in a narrow range.

The Dow closed down zero point three five percent. The S&P 500 was almost unchanged. The Nasdaq composite closed down zero point one five percent.

Semiconductors were better than Monday. The Philadelphia semiconductor index opened weak and climbed back. Twenty members rose. Ten fell.

Memory was still the soft pocket. Health care was the worst sector, down one point three five percent, after Johnson and Johnson's report.

There was no economic number. The session was bank earnings, and one chart of spending growth.

JPMorgan's adjusted revenue was fifty point nine billion, below fifty-one point four billion expected.

Bond trading was the weak book. Equities trading beat. Investment banking grew, and still missed.

Advisory beat. Underwriting of stocks and bonds did not.

3 · MACRO

The growth rate peaked

The chart is year-over-year growth, not the dollar level of spending.

Second-quarter growth was about eighty-seven percent. The quarter just ended was about one hundred and sixteen percent. That is the peak in the speed.

The later bars are still positive. Spending is still rising. It is rising more slowly. By the end of twenty twenty-seven the pace is about twenty percent. That is still growth.

The money leaves the big spenders and lands first with the shovel sellers. Nvidia, A.M.D., Broadcom, optical modules, and memory.

The shovels become revenue only when enterprises buy the output.

Slower growth hits margins at those sellers first. Earnings growth bends down after that.

Demand can still exist while the profit does not.

These bars are consensus, not the filings. The real numbers come at the end of October at the earliest, and next January at the latest.

If later growth stays near one hundred percent, this chart is not a warning.

5 · MARKET

The indexes are high. Many stocks are not.

Three of the four major indexes are up by double digits this year. The Dow is the exception.

Nine sectors are up. Nearly two-thirds of S&P members are higher.

One hundred and twenty-three stocks are up twenty-five percent or more. Forty-two are down that much.

The median stock trades about fifteen percent below its fifty-two-week high. That gap is the widest of this kind since the internet bubble.

The index does not show it. A concentrated mistake hurts more than a fractional decline in the index.
