1 · MARKET

Meta's funding plan is still taking shape

Meta launched about thirty five billion dollars of offerings. That includes twenty two point five billion dollars of Class A common stock through forward sales, and twelve point five billion dollars of depositary shares representing mandatory convertible preferred stock.

A separate Class C stock sale program could raise up to sixty billion dollars through sales at market prices. It is expected to begin no earlier than the first quarter of twenty twenty seven, after Class C shares begin trading. No distribution agreement has been signed. The combined ninety five billion dollars is a potential plan that still needs to be carried out in stages.

The timing of cash receipts also differs. Meta initially receives no cash from the Class A forward sale. It receives proceeds at future settlement. The preferred stock's final dividend and conversion terms still need to be priced.

Common stock issuance, eventual conversion, and returns on the new capital all affect per share value. The potential financing amount gives the scale. The effect on earnings per share depends on the price, share count, conversion conditions, and investment returns.

Meta fell one point eight eight percent to six hundred thirty dollars and fifty six cents while semiconductors rallied. Both moves can happen together. Funding at the customer can support purchases and suppliers' revenue expectations, while existing shareholders bear the cost of raising that capital.

Meta still lists operating cash flow, investment grade debt, strategic partnerships, and equity as funding sources. Equity supplements that mix. Follow the final size, the timing of proceeds, and the return on the additional investment.

2 · EARNINGS

Anthropic's contracts offer different revenue visibility

Anthropic has compute agreements with SpaceX allowing spending of up to one hundred twenty point five billion dollars through twenty twenty nine. Monthly fees would total about three point one billion dollars once all capacity is delivered. Anthropic expects full capacity in December twenty twenty six.

The respective initial non cancelable periods end in January and March twenty twenty seven. After those periods, either party can cancel with ninety days of written notice. The headline amount is a ceiling with exit rights. Realized revenue depends on capacity delivery and how long the agreements remain in place.

The Broadcom arrangements consist of several five year equipment finance leases. Total rent is about one hundred sixty one point two billion dollars, excluding interest. The leases are generally non cancelable except upon default. Some tensor processing unit capacity began coming online in twenty twenty six. More is expected from twenty twenty seven. Stronger contractual obligations improve revenue visibility. Delivery, the customer's ability to pay, and revenue recognition still have to follow.

A separate forty two billion dollar convertible note financing facility supports the related rental payments. Draws depend on revenue, liquidity, and performance conditions. No amount was outstanding at the end of June. The facility supports the customer's payments while tying the supplier more closely to the customer's credit.

Evaluate the lease commitments and payment capacity together. The headline contract amount is not current period revenue or profit. Adding the two companies' amounts also combines different tenors and cancellation terms, rather than establishing incremental chip revenue for one year.

Firmer contracts help the supplier. Fixed payments also put more pressure on the customer if its revenue growth slows. Both sides belong in the assessment of order quality.

3 · MARKET

SpaceX secures a commitment ahead of closing

SpaceX has secured a binding commitment for about forty billion dollars of financing leases for graphics processors and related equipment from Nvidia. Closing conditions remain. The transaction is expected to close before the end of twenty twenty six.

Equipment delivery and lease commencements are expected in the first half of twenty twenty seven. The interest rate still needs to be priced. The commitment improves visibility into future purchasing, while cash closing and equipment revenue recognition come later.

After delivery, the equipment still needs usable data center capacity, power, and deployment. If hardware arrives before a project can operate, interest, rent, and revenue may start at different times. Delivery schedules matter even when the financing commitment is large.

These developments ease demand concerns without eliminating differences in access to funding. Companies with established businesses, operating cash flow, and several financing channels generally have more room to absorb construction delays. Operators dependent on external capital need repeated checks of available cash, committed funding, interest expense, and delivery progress.

A large borrower's financing agreement does not establish easy credit for every operator. A few signs of stress also do not establish that all independent cloud financing has shut down. Each balance sheet needs its own assessment.

4 · CAPEX

Four ways to read capital spending in earnings

Microsoft and Google report after the close on Wednesday, October twenty eighth. Amazon follows on Thursday, October twenty ninth. Meta already reported on October twenty first. Its subsequent financing arrangements are the current focus.

In the first case, capital spending accelerates and relies increasingly on expensive debt. Suppliers may receive more orders first. Cloud operators carry interest, depreciation, and cash conversion pressure. Read higher spending alongside operating cash flow before treating it as good news across the industry.

In the second case, higher financing costs slow capital spending. Distinguish a voluntary return hurdle from an inability to fill a funding gap. The former can improve the balance sheet. The latter can delay projects and reduce supplier orders.

Weakening demand alongside financing pressure would be harder on both customers and suppliers. The size of a spending reduction alone does not settle the question. The reason for the cut matters.

In the third case, demand stays strong, spending holds steady or rises, and equity becomes a larger funding source. Cash proceeds can reduce debt pressure. Existing shareholders absorb dilution and the associated cost of capital. Meta offers one example, with the result depending on final issuance terms and investment returns.

In the fourth case, capital spending rises as operating cash flow and sustainable free cash flow accelerate. Customers and suppliers could then benefit together. Revenue, margins, and cash collection need to improve at the same time. A target to reach balance within a year or two requires quarterly evidence of progress.

Microsoft and Google have other mature businesses that provide a funding buffer. Their shares remain exposed to valuation pressure. Compare cloud growth, revenue guidance, cash capital spending, finance leases, and free cash flow over consistent periods. Focus on the capital required to deliver growth and the time it takes to earn that capital back.

Operating cash flow can benefit from advance payments, delayed supplier payments, or working capital releases that may not recur. Check new finance lease commitments and future payments alongside cash capital spending. Cash outlays often precede the full depreciation charge and investment return. One quarter's earnings per share or free cash flow can miss obligations coming later.
