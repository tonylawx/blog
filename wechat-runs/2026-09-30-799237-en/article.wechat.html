The index held up, but participation remained uneven

September thirtieth closed out both the month and the third quarter. The Nasdaq still finished higher after a late selloff, while the other major indexes were weaker. Quarter end rebalancing may have amplified the final stretch of trading, although that does not establish that all the selling was a one time flow.

A resilient index does not guarantee a comfortable portfolio. Semiconductors supported the market while many laggards failed to recover. Strength remained concentrated in a few groups, with much of the market still waiting for a sustained improvement.

The first test for the fourth quarter is whether those weaker names stabilize. If memory stocks and a few large technology companies keep carrying the index alone, concentration risk remains. Broader participation, with laggards finally stopping their decline, would provide better confirmation.

1 · MACRO

Cooler P C E has not removed the pressure from rates

August core P C E rose zero point two percent month over month and three percent year over year. Headline P C E increased zero point three percent on the month and three point four percent from a year earlier. The softer than expected inflation report reduced concern about another near term rate hike. Goldman Sachs pushed its forecast for the next hike from October to December, while market pricing put the probability of an October increase at roughly thirty eight percent.

Both the forecast and futures pricing can change. The Fed has not committed to that timetable. Statistical revisions also affect the inflation readings, so a lower print does not mean households' price pressures have disappeared.

September A D P private payrolls increased by ninety thousand. Employment remains resilient, and inflation has yet to return quickly to target. That leaves room for further changes in policy expectations. A D P and the official payroll report use different methods, so the private survey cannot be extrapolated directly to Friday's release.

The ten year Treasury yield initially fell after the inflation data, then finished higher. Long term rates did not ease alongside expectations for the next hike. Technology stocks still need a sustained decline in long end yields to receive more dependable valuation support.

2 · MARKET

Micron's growth is strong, but margin expectations are harder to meet

Micron reported fiscal twenty twenty six fourth quarter revenue of fifty four point two three billion dollars, adjusted earnings per share of thirty three dollars and forty two cents, and an adjusted gross margin of eighty seven percent. For fiscal twenty twenty seven's first quarter, it guided to revenue of sixty one point five billion dollars, plus or minus one point five billion dollars, and adjusted earnings per share of thirty eight dollars and fifteen cents. Revenue and earnings continue to rise.

The next quarter's margin outlook deserves closer attention. Gross margin guidance of eighty six point three percent is below the fourth quarter's eighty seven percent. The company attributed part of the change to incentive compensation adjustments and expects the first quarter to be the low point for gross margins in the new fiscal year. That remains a management forecast, subject to confirmation in subsequent results.

Fourth quarter operating cash flow of forty four billion dollars and free cash flow of thirty three point two billion dollars support the quality of that quarter's earnings. The company also expects approximately twenty five billion dollars of capital spending in fiscal twenty twenty seven. A single quarter's cash flow cannot be offset directly against a full year's investment, but both help assess cash returns as capacity expands.

The relatively restrained earnings reaction may reflect the high growth expectations already in the stock. A revenue beat alone does not establish that another rally is about to begin. Memory pricing can materially change forward earnings, and a low price to earnings multiple may use profits close to a cyclical peak.

For Micron, one thousand thirty dollars remains a near term downside reference, with one thousand one hundred twenty five dollars to one thousand two hundred eleven dollars the area to watch above. A break below one thousand thirty dollars would weaken the short term setup. Eight hundred fifty eight dollars marks a broader defensive threshold. These levels define conditional trading scenarios. They do not guarantee future prices.

3 · RISK

SanDisk's downside distance deserves its own risk budget

SanDisk benefits from memory demand, but its trading risk needs a separate assessment. The area from one thousand nine hundred fifty three dollars to two thousand ninety one dollars is an upside reference. Below, a loss of one thousand seven hundred four dollars would bring attention to support near one thousand six hundred fifty dollars. The farther away support sits, the more clearly a trader needs to define an acceptable loss before entering.

Increasing position size to follow a sector rally while placing a stop far below the entry can compound portfolio risk. Even with improving fundamentals, an oversized position can make a pullback difficult to absorb. Entry price, stop distance, and position size need to be considered together.

4 · MARKET

Rotation and defensive stocks require different confirmation

Google and Amazon remain worth watching for rotation within technology. A longer term valuation case needs support from earnings and cash flow. Near term confirmation requires a sustained improvement in price. One positive session does not establish a broad shift in capital, and recent underperformance alone does not make a stock cheap.

Travelers has a weaker technical setup. Whether a decline creates a longer term opportunity depends on selling slowing and support forming. For investors with substantial existing exposure, waiting to add and managing current downside are separate decisions.

For Eli Lilly, the area from one thousand dollars to one thousand eighty dollars remains the focus. Holding it would allow continued monitoring of the growth outlook. A break below one thousand dollars would shift attention toward nine hundred twenty eight dollars and lower levels. Weakness may reflect changing expectations, but a price break alone cannot confirm that institutions have cut their fiscal twenty twenty eight growth forecasts.

5 · MARKET

Let confirmation determine fourth quarter exposure

Over the next several sessions, the bullish conditions are cooler inflation, resilient employment, falling long end yields, and semiconductors holding up while more sectors recover. Broader participation would confirm that setup. Rising yields accompanied by breaks in the leaders would invalidate it.

The neutral scenario has yields staying elevated, stronger stocks consolidating, and laggards still unable to take over. Keeping exposure flexible and respecting each position's downside threshold would fit that market. An index rebound without broader improvement is insufficient reason to raise risk across the portfolio.

The bearish scenario combines another rise in yields, further declines in weaker stocks, and memory names losing near term support. That would pressure valuations and risk appetite together, making concentrated positions and stop loss plans the first things to review.

After this week's payroll report, assess yields and market breadth over the following three to five sessions. A renewed rise in long end yields argues for tighter technology exposure. More sectors holding support and joining the advance would justify a gradual increase in risk.
