0 · MARKET

Market participation remained uneven

September thirtieth closed out both the month and the third quarter.

The Nasdaq still finished higher after a late selloff. The other major indexes were weaker.

Quarter-end rebalancing may have amplified the final stretch of trading. That does not establish that all the selling was a one-time flow.

A resilient index does not guarantee a comfortable portfolio. Semiconductors supported the market, while many laggards failed to recover.

Strength remained concentrated in a few groups. Much of the market still needed a sustained improvement.

The first test for the fourth quarter is whether weaker stocks stabilize.

If memory stocks and a few large technology companies keep carrying the index alone, concentration risk remains. Broader participation, with laggards stopping their decline, would provide better confirmation.

1 · MACRO

Cooler inflation has not removed the pressure from rates

August core personal consumption expenditures inflation rose zero point two percent from the previous month. It rose three percent from a year earlier.

Headline inflation increased zero point three percent on the month. It rose three point four percent from a year earlier.

The softer-than-expected report reduced concern about another near-term rate hike.

Goldman Sachs pushed its forecast for the next hike from October to December. Market pricing put the probability of an October increase at roughly thirty-eight percent.

Both the forecast and futures pricing can change. The Federal Reserve has not committed to that timetable.

Statistical revisions also affect the inflation readings. A lower reading does not mean households' price pressures have disappeared.

The September private-payroll survey from A-D-P showed an increase of ninety thousand jobs.

Employment remains resilient, and inflation has yet to return quickly to target. That leaves room for further changes in policy expectations.

A-D-P and the official payroll report use different methods. The private survey cannot be extrapolated directly to Friday's release.

The ten-year Treasury yield initially fell after the inflation data. It then finished higher.

Long-term rates did not ease alongside expectations for the next hike. Technology stocks still need a sustained decline in long-end yields for more dependable valuation support.

2 · MARKET

Micron's margin outlook needs a closer look

Micron reported fiscal twenty twenty-six fourth-quarter revenue of fifty-four point two three billion dollars.

Adjusted earnings per share were thirty-three dollars and forty-two cents. Adjusted gross margin was eighty-seven percent.

For fiscal twenty twenty-seven's first quarter, revenue guidance was sixty-one point five billion dollars, plus or minus one point five billion dollars.

Adjusted earnings-per-share guidance was thirty-eight dollars and fifteen cents. Revenue and earnings continue to rise.

First-quarter gross-margin guidance was eighty-six point three percent. That is below the previous quarter's eighty-seven percent.

The company attributed part of the change to incentive-compensation adjustments.

Management expects the first quarter to be the low point for gross margins in the new fiscal year. That forecast still needs confirmation in subsequent results.

Fourth-quarter operating cash flow was forty-four billion dollars. Free cash flow was thirty-three point two billion dollars.

Those cash flows support the quality of that quarter's earnings.

The company also expects roughly twenty-five billion dollars of capital spending in fiscal twenty twenty-seven.

One quarter's cash flow cannot be offset directly against a full year's investment. Both still help assess cash returns as capacity expands.

The relatively restrained earnings reaction may reflect the high growth expectations already in the stock.

A revenue beat alone does not establish that another rally is about to begin.

Memory pricing can materially change forward earnings. A low price-to-earnings multiple may use profits close to a cyclical peak.

For Micron, one thousand and thirty dollars remains a near-term downside reference.

The area from one thousand, one hundred and twenty-five dollars to one thousand, two hundred and eleven dollars is the upside reference.

A break below one thousand and thirty dollars would weaken the short-term setup.

Eight hundred and fifty-eight dollars marks a broader defensive threshold. These levels define conditional trading scenarios and do not guarantee future prices.

3 · RISK

SanDisk needs its own risk budget

SanDisk benefits from memory demand. Its trading risk needs a separate assessment.

The area from one thousand, nine hundred and fifty-three dollars to two thousand and ninety-one dollars is an upside reference.

Below, a loss of one thousand, seven hundred and four dollars would bring attention to support near one thousand, six hundred and fifty dollars.

The farther away support sits, the more clearly a trader needs to define an acceptable loss before entering.

Increasing position size to follow a sector rally can compound risk when the stop is far below the entry.

Even with improving fundamentals, an oversized position can make a pullback difficult to absorb.

Entry price, stop distance, and position size need to be considered together.

Rotation and defensive stocks need different confirmation

Google and Amazon remain worth watching for rotation within technology.

A longer-term valuation case needs support from earnings and cash flow. Near-term confirmation requires a sustained improvement in price.

One positive session does not establish a broad shift in capital. Recent underperformance alone does not make a stock cheap.

Travelers has a weaker technical setup.

Whether a decline creates a longer-term opportunity depends on selling slowing and support forming.

For investors with substantial existing exposure, waiting to add and managing current downside are separate decisions.

For Eli Lilly, the area from one thousand dollars to one thousand and eighty dollars remains the focus.

Holding it would allow continued monitoring of the growth outlook.

A break below one thousand dollars would shift attention toward nine hundred and twenty-eight dollars and lower levels.

Weakness may reflect changing expectations. A price break alone cannot confirm that institutions have cut their fiscal twenty twenty-eight growth forecasts.

Let confirmation determine fourth-quarter exposure

Over the next several sessions, bullish conditions include cooler inflation, resilient employment, and falling long-end yields.

Semiconductors would need to hold up while more sectors recover. Broader participation would confirm that setup.

Rising yields, accompanied by breaks in the leaders, would invalidate it.

In the neutral scenario, yields stay elevated. Stronger stocks consolidate, and laggards still cannot take over.

Keeping exposure flexible and respecting each position's downside threshold would fit that market.

An index rebound without broader improvement is insufficient reason to raise risk across the portfolio.

The bearish scenario combines another rise in yields, further declines in weaker stocks, and memory names losing near-term support.

That would pressure valuations and risk appetite together. Concentrated positions and stop-loss plans would be the first things to review.

After this week's payroll report, assess yields and market breadth over the following three to five sessions.

A renewed rise in long-end yields argues for tighter technology exposure.

More sectors holding support and joining the advance would justify a gradual increase in risk.
