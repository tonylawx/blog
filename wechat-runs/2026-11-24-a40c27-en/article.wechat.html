Chips are taking the hit, but the broader market is holding up

U.S. stocks split on Tuesday, November twenty fourth, twenty twenty six. The S and P five hundred closed at seven thousand two hundred and one, down zero point five percent. The Nasdaq fell one point five percent. The Dow gained zero point eight five percent. The Russell two thousand added zero point five seven percent.

Market breadth remained positive. Three hundred and forty eight S and P five hundred stocks rose, while one hundred and fifty four declined. Most stocks outside technology were still higher. The weakness was concentrated in large technology names, especially semiconductors.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell seven point nine percent. All thirty constituents closed lower. It had jumped fourteen percent in the prior session, then gave back almost eight percent the next day. Across the two sessions, it was still up about five percent. The important point is that buyers did not follow through after the sharp rally.

Nvidia fell five point nine percent. A M D dropped eight point nine percent. Broadcom lost four percent, and Micron slid eleven percent. The wider market did not show broad panic. Chip investors simply were not willing to chase the rebound.

Tariff headlines added uncertainty. The U.S. Trade Representative was nearing the release of findings from an industrial capacity investigation covering sixteen trading partners. New tariffs were possible, but the rates were still unknown.

Consumer confidence improved. The November index rose eleven point four points to one hundred and three point three. That was above the market expectation of ninety two point five. Better confidence and employment expectations do not fit a picture of an economy suddenly falling apart.

1 · EARNINGS

The A I buildout has a timing gap between revenue and risk

Revenue recognition and risk absorption happen at different points in the A I supply chain. Chip and networking suppliers can book orders once equipment is delivered. Data centre operators have to fund land, power, facilities, servers, and networking up front. The money may come from operating cash flow, bonds, loans, leases, equity, or customer prepayments.

Once the capacity is online, operators still carry utilization, depreciation, interest, and maintenance costs. The final test is whether enterprise customers and end users keep paying for training, inference, and software services. That is why rising A I capital spending and attractive investment returns are two different things. Hardware orders can turn into reported revenue early. Data centre utilization and recurring customer payments take longer to prove.

If construction runs ahead of demand, the first pressure may show up at operators with heavy debt, frequent refinancing needs, and concentrated customers. It does not have to hit every chip supplier equally.

Credit markets are starting to examine this chain. Public five year credit default swap prints put Oracle around three hundred and sixty eight basis points. CoreWeave was around one thousand and ninety six basis points. Nvidia was around one hundred and forty three. Broadcom was around one hundred and eighty three.

Meta was near one hundred and fourteen basis points. Amazon was around ninety three. The latest trade dates differ by company, and single name trading can be thin. These are public trade prints, not firm live quotes.

2 · MARKET

Wider C D S spreads are not a default verdict

A wider credit default swap spread means investors are demanding more money for credit protection. It reflects a higher risk premium and can signal more expensive funding. It does not mean a company has defaulted. It also cannot be translated directly into a certain default probability. A rating downgrade needs a formal rating agency notice. The rated entity and the specific debt also matter.

The important divide is between companies that can fund investment internally and those that need outside capital to keep expanding. Cash rich cloud platforms can slow spending or change project priorities. Compute operators that rely on rolling debt and higher cost funding face more direct pressure.

Even when companies share the same view on A I demand, their funding costs determine how quickly they can grow. Funding costs also determine how much stress their balance sheets can absorb.

Investors are watching circular financing and off balance sheet commitments as well. A market concern is not the same as a confirmed transaction structure. Complicated financing does not make every order fake. But when customers, suppliers, and lenders depend on one another, weaker customer payments can travel through contracts and balance sheets.

3 · SEMIS

Multiple compression comes first; earnings cuts would be the next test

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index hit an intraday high of eleven thousand seven hundred and thirty one on October twenty eighth. It closed at eight thousand eight hundred and thirty three on November twenty fourth. That was about twenty five percent below the high.

That puts the index in technical bear market territory. It describes the price drawdown, not the collapse of the A I thesis. Share prices can compress valuation multiples before earnings expectations are revised lower. There are two separate risks. The first is multiple compression. Investors are willing to pay less for the same expected earnings.

The second risk is an earnings decline. Orders, revenue, margins, or cash flow start coming in below prior expectations. The first can happen without earnings cuts. The second puts both earnings and valuation under pressure. Memory stocks run on a different cycle. Micron fell more than eleven percent on the day. But memory pricing, inventories, and supply conditions can change quickly.

A low forward price to earnings ratio does not automatically mean a memory stock is cheap. If memory prices fall, earnings per share can turn lower before the valuation looks expensive. For the broader semiconductor group, watch whether earnings estimates for Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom are cut together.

Bring the single names back to their price zones

A M D closed at four hundred and thirteen dollars and twenty seven cents on November twenty fourth. Its intraday low was three hundred and ninety six dollars and one cent. It also closed below its roughly four hundred and thirty two dollar two hundred day moving average.

The four hundred to four hundred and twenty four dollar area is the first zone it needs to reclaim. If it keeps closing below that band, the next historical volume area worth watching sits below three hundred and sixty six dollars. That is not a guaranteed downside target. The more useful signal is a quick reclaim after a breakdown, followed by buyers defending the retest.

T S M closed at three hundred and forty nine dollars and twenty cents. After the gap higher on November twenty third, it traded as low as three hundred and thirty nine dollars and seventy three cents the following day. It moved below the prior gap band, which ran from three hundred and forty three dollars and seventy five cents to three hundred and sixty one dollars and sixty six cents.

It then closed back inside that range. The gap fill weakened the rebound setup. The next test is whether the stock can reclaim three hundred and sixty one dollars and sixty six cents. Buyers also need to show up on a pullback.

A S M L closed at one thousand three hundred and thirty six dollars and six cents. Its structure was similar. Around twelve hundred dollars is a nearer watch level. The stronger support reference remains the one thousand to one thousand and ninety dollar area.

Broadcom closed at three hundred and one dollars and eight cents. Its intraday low was two hundred and ninety seven dollars and eighty cents. That undercut the November twentieth low of three hundred and one dollars and two cents. A lower wick near a fresh low does not prove buyers have taken control.

Marvell closed at one hundred and thirty one dollars and fifty seven cents, after touching one hundred and twenty eight dollars and fourteen cents. The one hundred and three to one hundred and twenty five dollar area is a lower historical trading zone to watch. It only becomes meaningful support if a retest produces a long lower wick and follow through buying.

Nvidia closed at one hundred and fifty five dollars and thirty eight cents. Its intraday low was one hundred and fifty dollars and fifty cents. It remains just above the one hundred and thirty two to one hundred and fifty dollar support zone.

Resistance sits between one hundred and seventy four and one hundred and ninety four dollars. A bounce after a weak open is still only a repair attempt if volume does not improve. Price also needs to reclaim key levels before calling it a new trend.

5 · MARKET

The S and P five hundred is sitting near seven thousand two hundred

The S and P five hundred closed at seven thousand two hundred and one, almost exactly on seven thousand two hundred. That is the near term line to watch. Seven thousand one hundred is the more important downside stress test.

If the index holds seven thousand two hundred and forms a second leg of strong buying near the prior lows, staged entries become more reasonable. A break below seven thousand one hundred would make the short term structure materially weaker.

There is little reason to rush orders in the first thirty minutes after the open. The final hour matters more. The last thirty minutes are especially useful for judging whether buyers are willing to absorb supply. The real test is not whether A I demand disappears in one day. It is whether the spending can turn into cash returns.

If data centre utilization, cloud revenue, and customer payments keep growing while operating cash flow covers funding costs, the current move looks more like a volatile valuation reset. If funding costs keep rising, utilization disappoints, capital spending slows, and earnings estimates for Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom are cut together, the risk moves from valuations to fundamentals.

The semiconductor index is already in a technical bear market. Whether the underlying business thesis has broken still depends on those operating and cash flow measures.
