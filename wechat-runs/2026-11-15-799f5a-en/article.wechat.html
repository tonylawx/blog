Google opens $1.50-per-million-input-token Gemini 4 Hydrogen to all Search users

On November tenth, Google said in an official blog post that Gemini four Carbon was open to paying developers and enterprise customers. The introductory price is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens. A footnote says that introductory price ends on November thirtieth, after which the rate becomes four dollars per million input tokens and twenty dollars per million output tokens. The same day, the frontier model reached the Gemini app and A I Mode in Search only for Google A I Ultra subscribers. On November twelfth the same author posted again and introduced Gemini four Hydrogen at one dollar and fifty cents per million input tokens and seven dollars and fifty cents per million output tokens, flat across a one million token context window.

The price only means something next to the door it walks through. The Hydrogen post says Search is available to all users, and that free users meet the model when automatic selection picks it. Google describes Hydrogen as the tier teams move to from Gemini three point eight Flash, built for fast execution, while Carbon and Argon stay on the longer problems. One million input tokens cost one dollar and fifty cents on Hydrogen, two dollars on Carbon during the introduction, and four dollars after November thirtieth. Output is seven dollars and fifty cents against ten dollars, and twenty dollars after the introduction ends. What fell is the price of a call. Training spend already on the books does not stop depreciating because Search changed its default.

Upstream chip sellers get paid by the cluster. Downstream model sellers get paid by the token. Google put the cheaper execution model into Search and used its own traffic, so the bill does not pass through another cloud. Labs still selling an API by the call now face a one dollar and fifty cents tier sitting in the search box. Enterprise buying will not stop at a leaderboard rank. Permissions, where the data sits, and who reviews a bad answer stay in the contract. The cut hits the premium first. Integration, evaluation, and human review stay where they were.

Set this next to the cloud price wars that opened the last decade, when compute and storage list prices were cut round after round and the margin that remained often sat in data that could no longer be moved. The rhyme is that a metered door moved down another tier. The difference is distribution. The door is the search box, and anyone who opens a browser is already standing on the price list. Fiber laid in the ground in nineteen ninety nine still needed a last mile before a user could be billed. A default model in Search does not. A call can start the day the post goes up.

The next two quarters should be checked against the price list, not a benchmark table. If Hydrogen's input price leaves one dollar and fifty cents before the end of the first quarter of twenty twenty seven, or if Search stops offering that tier to every user, the claim that a cheap model became the default door has to be rewritten. Whether Carbon actually moves from two dollars to four dollars on November thirtieth is the next line on the same sheet. Carbon's Search door that day was Ultra only. Hydrogen's post says all users. Ranks in the company blog do not replace either price change, or that difference in who can walk in.

SpaceX plans over $74 billion in computing campuses, with on-site power in Texas

On November twelfth, Reuters and the Wall Street Journal both reported that SpaceX plans two supercomputing campuses, in Texas and Arizona, at more than seventy four billion dollars combined. The Rio Grande Valley project near Starbase is about twenty four billion dollars. The company said it will generate its own power and will not draw it from the public grid. Bloomberg wrote the same day that the Texas site includes a gas fired plant. The Journal's figures are about twenty four billion dollars in Texas and about fifty billion dollars in Cochise County, Arizona. Reuters said the Arizona project still needs county approval.

Seventy four billion dollars is a plan, not cash already spent. Reuters put the jobs at about five thousand five hundred construction roles and one thousand permanent roles in Arizona, and more than five hundred permanent roles in Texas once the site is running. The Starbase City Commission is due to consider a property tax abatement on December ninth. County approval, the plant, and the tax break are all still in process. What was written down on announcement day is an investment intention and a site, not load already on a grid. The bars below are only the two amounts two news wires have both described. They are not finished capacity.

The same week a model price moved down by the token, another company wrote a power plant into its own fence at a scale of tens of billions of dollars. The Texas project says it will not use the public grid, so the user of the power and the generator sit inside one project instead of on a utility bill. Arizona still needs local approval. Residents, the county, and the company are still at the same permit table. For the last few years, cloud buyers have waited in interconnect queues. This plan puts generation inside the wall. Whether margin stays depends on whether power is an owned cost or a purchased operating bill.

Set that next to nineteen ninety nine, when North American telecom laid backbone fiber ahead of paying users. What was missing then was the on ramp, not the cable. What this buildout is short of, more often, is power and permission, not the dollar figure in the release. A gas plant on site moves the wait from a grid queue to equipment delivery and emissions permits. Bloomberg's plant, if it is really inside the fence, means the substation line is no longer the first door. The constraint changed tables. It did not disappear. Racks can be ordered by the quarter. Plants, county hearings, and the December ninth tax vote run on another calendar.

The next two or three quarters only have to watch three things already in the reporting. Whether Cochise County approves the site. Whether the December ninth tax abatement passes. Whether Texas still says the power is self generated, rather than quietly connecting to the public grid. If two of the three fail, more than seventy four billion dollars is still a Thursday statement. Reuters also reported that A I was already more than half of the company's third quarter revenue. That is a mix already on the books. It does not replace a permit.

Microsoft 365 outage tops 24 hours; OpenAI unveils an approval-based wallet

Around midday in the United States on November twelfth, Microsoft three sixty five started failing across a wide area. The Associated Press and Reuters both reported that Outlook, Teams, and Copilot were hit, and that the company did not say service was fully restored until Saturday, November fourteenth. Reuters called the outage more than twenty four hours. Microsoft told the Associated Press the cause was an earlier network capacity upgrade, that it was not a cyber incident, and that it was not caused by A I agents. The Associated Press, citing the company's own site, said more than four hundred and thirty million people use Microsoft three sixty five. Outage reports came from more than seventy countries and territories. Government offices in Japan, Singapore, and Australia said publicly that they were affected.

Mail, meetings, and the assistant stopped inside one suite. Reuters reported that Euronext, the exchange operator, told listed companies to use the phone and to verify who was on the line. The same report said some airlines warned customers that communication would lag. Most institutions said the core trading systems were still running. What slowed was the talk around those systems. One company's network upgrade became the external line for agencies and airlines in several countries. Central buying is convenient on a normal day. From Thursday to Saturday it was one failure domain. Copilot going down with the inbox also means the assistant had no separate way out.

On November thirteenth, OpenAI published ChatGPT Wallet on its own site, with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa named as partners. The agent does not receive the full card number. The user reviews the merchant, the items, the total, and the selected card, then approves with a face scan or a fingerprint. The terms also let a user grant, in advance, the authority to approve each purchase. The wallet holds no balance and extends no credit. The first rollout is only for ChatGPT Pro users in the United States, aged eighteen or older, using a card issued in the United States. The privacy notice is effective November twelfth. The product post is dated the next day.

The outage and the wallet landed in the same week, and they are not the same fact. Companies have already handed calendars and mail to a few vendors, and the fallback when those stop is the telephone. A consumer agent that wants to spend money still needs a person to nod, and only inside one country and one subscription tier. There is no public count of completed purchases, so the unit economics of agent checkout are not on the page yet. What can be checked now is whether approval stays with the user, and whether the card networks will keep the real number outside the agent by using a one time credential.

The next quarter has two documents that can force a rewrite. If Microsoft's final incident note puts the outage under twenty four hours, or moves the cause off the network capacity upgrade, the outage paragraph changes. If Wallet is still only U.S. Pro at the end of the first quarter of twenty twenty seven, and no transaction volume is disclosed, agent payments are still a narrow trial. If the advance authorization clause becomes the default, the per purchase nod is no longer the rule written today. The entrance to the card networks has not moved because a wallet post went up.

What to verify next: Gemini prices, campus permits, Microsoft's outage, and wallet adoption

This week's technology news does not fit inside one personnel story. On November tenth Google opened a frontier model to paying developers at an introductory two dollars per million input tokens. On November twelfth an execution model at one dollar and fifty cents per million input tokens reached Search for every user. The same day SpaceX announced more than seventy four billion dollars of campus plans, with Texas power kept inside the project. From Thursday into Saturday, Microsoft three sixty five was down for more than twenty four hours. On Friday OpenAI let an agent pay with a one time credential after a U.S. Pro user approved the charge.

The financing calendar was the same week, and it does not explain the three items above. On November tenth Reuters reported that Anthropic plans to sell eight hundred and twenty five million shares at seventy dollars to seventy eight dollars, raising up to sixty four point four billion dollars, with a fully diluted value of about one point four trillion dollars at the top of the range, plus a twenty six billion dollars private placement of non voting shares. Run rate revenue as of October thirty first was eighty one billion dollars, Reuters wrote. Trading on Nasdaq is expected on November nineteenth. That is a lab raising money. It is not why Search cut a price or why a Texas plant was put inside a fence.

Read as four lines, the directions are separate. The price of a call is moving down, and Search made the cheap tier a default door. Compute spending is moving up, and one buyer has started writing the power plant into the project boundary. The office suite failure shows that a single point in a work process reaches more people in a day than a model leaderboard does. Agent payments are still stuck at a per purchase approval and a single subscription tier. A lab whose run rate is eighty one billion dollars still has to face an offering range next week. That is a different book.

The next two quarters need four checks. Whether Hydrogen's one dollar and fifty cents input price is changed. Whether Cochise County approves the site and whether the December ninth tax abatement passes. Whether Microsoft's incident note still says a network capacity upgrade and more than twenty four hours. Whether Wallet leaves U.S. Pro, and whether Anthropic's offering price leaves the seventy dollars to seventy eight dollars range. If two of the four fail, the reading above has to change. There is no need to wait for the next leaderboard.

The public line is short. Models got cheaper, power and permits got more expensive, and people remembered the telephone when the office suite stopped. The one dollar and fifty cents price in Search has to survive the sheet after November thirtieth. The fifty billion dollars Arizona campus has to survive the county. The wallet has to show that someone actually uses the per purchase approval. The next dates on the page are November thirtieth and December ninth. None of the three has an answer yet.
