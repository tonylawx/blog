Payrolls rose twenty-nine thousand, and the October hike odds fell into the low teens

September payrolls rose by only twenty-nine thousand. The consensus was ninety thousand. August was revised from one hundred and sixty-two thousand to one hundred and thirty-three thousand. July and August together were revised down by another sixty thousand. The unemployment rate rose from four point one percent to four point two percent. Participation rose from sixty-one point six percent to sixty-one point eight percent. Private payrolls added forty-six thousand. Government payrolls fell seventeen thousand. Average hourly earnings rose zero point one percent on the month, versus zero point three percent expected, and three percent on the year, versus three point two percent.

The labor market is not as strong as the prior print implied. Before the report, an October hike was priced around twenty-two percent. After it, rate futures fell to about thirteen percent. If August had first printed one hundred and thirty-three thousand instead of one hundred and sixty-two thousand, the September hike itself might not have happened. Revisions this large can rewrite a meeting. The first print is a draft.

3 · SEMIS

Amazon wants eight billion dollars of chips off the balance sheet

The long-term view on Amazon has not changed. What changed is the balance-sheet design. The Financial Times reported that Amazon has spent recent weeks talking to investors about moving about eight billion dollars of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips into a special-purpose vehicle. That vehicle would borrow to buy the chips and lease them back to Amazon. Amazon would not own the vehicle. It would offer at most a ten percent equity stake. The chips are already installed in more than a dozen U.S. data centers. The talks are ongoing and can change.

The stated aim is a lighter balance sheet: move depreciation and debt off the books and protect the credit rating. What remains is rent, not the depreciation of this chip vintage.

That is the opposite of Nvidia's circular financing. Nvidia has funded customers, directly or indirectly, so those customers can turn around and buy Nvidia chips, which supports reported revenue. Amazon is trying to sell the asset while it still has value, and to run the cost as rent later. The historical parallels are mechanisms, not forecasts of the same ending. Cisco financed customers into equipment and then fell about ninety percent from the peak after the two thousand internet bust. Enron moved problems off the balance sheet and failed in two thousand one because revenue could not cover what had been hidden. The structure can be legal. Disclosure is not concealment.

While industry revenue is still growing and the liquidity chain still holds, this is operating leverage. Near-term depreciation is swapped for future cash. If cash flow, or revenue across the A.I. complex, comes in below what is priced, the off-balance-sheet rent becomes a real constraint. A long-term hold in a high-quality name can stay in place. The condition is that the published balance sheet and the earnings still match. Heat is not the condition. Amazon is not the only company moving assets off the books. If the car is heading for the ditch, the story is not a reason to stay in it.

Nvidia made a new intraday high on Friday, near two hundred and thirty-eight, and closed back around two hundred and thirty-four. The close is not yet through the prior closing highs around two hundred and thirty-five to two hundred and thirty-six. It is close. A gap up that fades but still finishes near the recent high is a neutral hold, not a strong top.

4 · SEMIS

Semis broke out, and the divergences did not heal together

S-O-X-X was a fraction short yesterday. Friday gapped open to five hundred and ninety and completed the breakout, the same break S-M-H made. After a break, the path is a slow look at the prior high and then a new high: higher lows, higher highs, a spiral, not a straight line. A down day next does not cancel the break. It cancels the idea that the path is a one-day ramp.

The flaw is still there. S-O-X-X still has a daily R-S-I bearish divergence. S-M-H repaired its divergence with Friday's rally, and the cost was an overbought reading. Repair the divergence, and the next job is often to repair the overbought condition. A pullback can still come. Friday's close is a recent high, so this candle is not evidence of a pullback top. Once the trend is higher lows, a pullback can be held. Chasing a few tenths of a point is not the trade.

AppLovin is at the next shelf, and a break points under one hundred and eighty

The August shelf on AppLovin was three hundred and seventeen and three hundred and fifty-three. A break of three hundred and seventeen was the hedge. The stock never reclaimed three hundred and fifty-three. Rallies kept failing, and a fresh headline hit it again. Price is now in two hundred and fifty-two to two hundred and ninety-seven.

If that zone holds, it can be a staging low: base, then look for a bounce and for the fundamentals to improve. If it breaks, the chart opens an air pocket toward below one hundred and eighty. There is no point trimming again at three hundred and seventeen. That trim is late. Friday showed some defense, but the close was a new low, so the stabilization is weak. If the position is still on and the earlier hedge was missed, the question is whether this shelf actually holds.
