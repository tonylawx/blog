Focus: Valuation Cuts and Infrastructure Bottlenecks

Two clear signals appeared across the artificial intelligence industry over the past week. OpenAI is in talks for a fresh funding round of roughly thirty billion dollars. The target valuation was lowered from one point four trillion dollars to one point two five trillion dollars. That is an eleven percent cut.

During the same week, Amazon reached an agreement with Loudoun County in Virginia. Amazon decided to abandon its data center plan on the former George Washington University campus. The one hundred twenty two acre property will now become residential and mixed use housing.

These two developments point to one conclusion. The market has moved past benchmark claims. Investors are now focused on real compute returns and capital spending. The cost of model inference has dropped by nearly ninety percent over the past year. Open source models have closed the gap with proprietary systems. That pressure triggered price cuts that squeezed gross margins on A P I calls.

At the same time, major cloud providers continue to increase capital spending by double digits. They need to fund training clusters and data centers. If smarter models do not generate high margin software revenue, massive compute investments quickly turn into a balance sheet drag.

1 · COMPUTE

Compute, Chips, and Power Limits

Amazon's decision in Loudoun County highlights the main physical bottleneck for artificial intelligence. That bottleneck is electrical power. Loudoun County handles roughly thirty percent of global internet traffic. Its transmission lines and substations are running close to their limits.

Large training clusters need hundreds of megawatts of continuous electricity. Public utilities cannot deliver that power fast enough. Major cloud providers are working around traditional power grids. They are moving to secure dedicated clean energy. Microsoft agreed to buy power from the reopened Three Mile Island nuclear plant. Google partnered with Kairos Power for five hundred megawatts of small modular reactors. Amazon Web Services deepened its direct relationship with Talen Energy.

Getting electricity, zoning permits, and grid connections is now the main challenge for compute growth. In chip manufacturing, Taiwan Semiconductor continues to run its three nanometer and two nanometer lines at full capacity. Nvidia resolved packaging yield issues with its Blackwell chips. Even so, server rack deliveries are still delayed by cooling systems, heavy busbars, and optical transceivers.

Profits remain concentrated with chip designers and foundries. Cloud platforms are left carrying heavy depreciation expenses.

2 · MARKET

Enterprise Friction and Ecosystem Shifts

Enterprise adoption of generative artificial intelligence is moving more slowly than expected. Core business operations require zero errors. High value workflows in accounting, system code refactoring, and legal compliance cannot tolerate hallucinations. Baseline token prices have dropped. However, building the systems needed for production accuracy remains expensive.

Companies must build custom retrieval pipelines, testing suites, and human review steps. These engineering costs often exceed the raw A P I bill. Open source models are also challenging proprietary software moats. Open models have improved rapidly in coding and complex reasoning.

More companies are choosing to host and fine tune open models on their own private servers. This keeps proprietary data safe and eliminates recurring per token fees. Proprietary models must demonstrate clear performance advantages in multi step planning to justify their higher prices.

3 · MARKET

Long Term Thesis and Outlook

Looking at capital flows and physical bottlenecks, we see two clear trends. First, simple wrapper applications and A P I resellers will face an industry shakeout over the next two to three quarters. Falling model prices and new built in features from major platforms will squeeze shallow software businesses.

Lasting pricing power will belong only to specialized agent platforms. These platforms must integrate deeply into company workflows and protect proprietary data. Second, investor interest is expanding from chips into power generation and electrical equipment. Nvidia's delivery speed through next year will depend heavily on power connections and substation upgrades.

Power equipment manufacturers, independent nuclear operators, and optical transceiver makers will continue to draw long term capital. The valuation surge has cooled down. The industry is now entering a phase of disciplined capital allocation.
