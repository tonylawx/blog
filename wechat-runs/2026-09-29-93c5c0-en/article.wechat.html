Utilities led, and the long end is still rising

Tuesday was not a broad rally. The Dow closed at fifty-one thousand, three hundred and forty-nine point nine two, down zero point two six percent. The S&P 500 closed at seven thousand, six hundred and seventy point eight four, down zero point one seven percent. The Nasdaq composite closed at twenty-six thousand, seven hundred and ninety-seven point five four, down zero point zero nine percent. The Nasdaq one hundred was the one that actually rose, up zero point two one percent. Four of the eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher and seven finished lower. Utilities led on rising volume, up about one point one percent.

Read that gain on its own. Utilities were the squeeze candidate yesterday, and flow showed up today. The height of the bounce is not something to forecast, and it is not a fundamental turn. If you participate, treat it as a short-term flow and take the gain.

The New York Fed's John Williams was dovish. After the September hike, there is no need to rush the next one. Front-end yields fell, and the October hike odds dropped from about seventy percent to roughly a coin flip. The long end did not follow. The ten-year touched about five point two nine percent and closed at five point two six percent, still the highest close since two thousand seven. Lower oil limited the equity damage. It did not pull the long end down.

Confidence and job openings both missed

September consumer confidence fell from eighty-eight point six to eighty-one point nine. The consensus was eighty-nine point two. That is the lowest reading since April twenty fourteen. The present-situation index dropped seven point nine points to one hundred and nine point three. The expectations index dropped five point nine points to sixty-three point six. The share calling business conditions bad rose from seventeen point three percent in August to twenty point four percent. The share saying jobs are hard to get rose from twenty point three percent to twenty-one point nine percent. In the written answers, mentions of prices, the high cost of goods and services, and oil and gas prices hit a new high.

August job openings fell from seven point three three five million to seven point zero seven nine million, below a forecast near seven point two three million. The openings rate slipped from four point four percent to four point three percent. Quits eased from three point zero eight nine million to three point zero six six million, and the quits rate stayed at one point nine percent. Total separations fell from five point one two eight million to five point zero seven million, and that rate stayed at three point two percent. Layoffs fell from one point seven zero two million to one point six four one million, and the layoff rate fell from one point one percent to one percent. Demand is cooling. Layoffs have not blown out.

Personal consumption prices, payrolls, the manufacturing purchasing-managers index, and Micron's earnings are still ahead this week. Those prints decide whether five point two six percent pauses or keeps climbing.

The tactical upgrade is a view, and the caveats stay attached

JPMorgan's institutional market-intelligence desk has been accurate on the big S&P 500 turns for the past two years. On August thirty-first they shifted to tactically neutral. The S&P 500 bounced a little and then weakened, and it is now back near the late-August level. This time they moved to an explicit tactical long. The window is the one or two months from third-quarter earnings through year-end. It is not a long-term bull case.

On the macro side, they say the flash purchasing-managers index is stronger than the growth already in the price, which should support hiring, spending, and corporate earnings. S&P 500 earnings per share track nominal growth, not real growth, and the current economy makes sell-side earnings estimates look too low.

On the consumer, they still see spending beating expectations. They do not accept that a split economy has already broken demand, and they say the labor market is improving. That does not line up with today's confidence survey. Confidence is at a twelve-year low, and openings missed. Strong consumption is their view. It is not a settled fact.

On earnings, after fifty-two percent year-over-year growth in earnings per share in the second quarter of twenty twenty-six, the market expects about twenty-nine percent growth in the third quarter. They think that is low, because all eleven sectors are expected to post positive revenue and positive earnings per share. The implication is a broad set of beats.

On rates, they expect firmer growth and inflation data that are flat to dovish. The ten-year holds near current levels and finishes the year around five point zero five percent. A lower yield would cut bond volatility and take some downside pressure off equities.

On positioning, seasonality is improving, retail may come back, and the book is light.

The long problems are still on the page. Is the bond market underpricing this hiking cycle? If the flash survey is consistent with about five percent real growth and inflation pressure is still rising, a labor market that keeps hiring can force a stronger cycle than what is priced. As of yesterday, money markets still had three hikes over the next twelve months, with a small chance of a fourth. Does the market start pricing fifty basis points at each meeting? When does food inflation reach the core? Can industries outside technology earn a decent return?

The playbook is specific. Technology stays the core long. A drop in oil and a slide in Treasury yields still create squeeze risk, the same line as yesterday. So they stop pairing a technology long with a Russell short. The Russell is the most sensitive to the ten-year. If the call is for a lower yield, that short does not belong. If the squeeze comes, it will be fast, and they want options rather than a cash short. Semiconductors and the big seven may broaden and beat the technology sector itself. Software is warming slowly and is still not a mainstream bullish bet. They still like the A.I. theme.

Outside technology, cyclicals need a steeper yield curve before the move can last. Within that group they prefer banks. Growth may restart, the curve may steepen, the capital-markets backdrop is better, and the group has already pulled back. Cyclicals are not untradable. They are not already launched.

A view is a view. The August neutral call was followed by a weak tape. That does not mean this bullish call will be. Two sessions remain in September, and the S&P 500 is still down about zero point two percent for the month. In a midterm year, September and October are about volatility, not a straight line. The seasonal improvement usually shows up in November, after the election.

Systematic money does not sell unless the drop is large

Goldman-tracked systematic trend funds sold about fifteen billion dollars of global equities over the past week and about seventy billion dollars over the past month. The global equity long is down to about seventy-two billion dollars, the fifty-fourth percentile, a middle-of-the-road book. In a flat or rising market they have no reason to sell more. A real sale needs a real drop. Roughly, the S&P 500 has to fall through seven thousand five hundred, toward seven thousand three hundred to seven thousand four hundred, before that trigger is live again. The level has already shifted once after it was hit, and it has not been refreshed into a precise line. Treat it as a zone, not a button.

If price holds, this money does not come in to sell in the near term. What is actually pushing the market is third-quarter earnings, and the ten-year. It closed at five point two six percent. A move to five point five percent would be a large, negative equity reaction. A move down toward five percent would be a large, positive one. For this stretch, the ten-year is the main contradiction.

Meta's stabilization arrived too fast

Meta was a stall after Friday's top, and Monday completed the daily repair with one drop. Tuesday printed the stabilization, and buyers came in fast. The round trip was about four to six points. A slow hand either sold the low or bought the high.

After the repair, the larger direction is still constructive. Defense is six hundred and ninety to seven hundred and thirty. Overhead is a break of the prior high. If you are still in it, this stretch can be held a bit longer. The view changes if Monday's low breaks. The technical repair can be done while the pullback is not.

A-S-M-L and Lam: the break is close, and the valuation is not cheap

A-S-M-L has two straight days of stepped-up volume, and one thousand eight hundred and fifty-three is nearly through. A clear of that level points the chart at new highs, with two thousand above. The forward ceiling is two thousand two hundred and fifty-seven, about forty-five point seven times forward earnings. The chart can be a breakout. Another leg higher is expensive on the fundamentals. This is still the steady equipment name. What changed is the level, not the story.

Lam Research is still well below the prior high. A move through three hundred and thirty-five is what starts the higher-low, higher-high structure, a slow stair-step rather than a three-day sprint through roughly thirty-five points. Until it clears, the break has not happened. A loss of three hundred and two ends this push and puts the stock back in a range.

The fundamentals are not cheap. The June twenty twenty-eight forward figure is about two hundred and eighty-seven. Growth is faster than its own history, and even a stretch to thirty-five times is still not cheap. A straight run to four hundred is expensive. The swing can follow the structure. Do not treat a technical break as confirmed earnings before the third-quarter report.

Oracle needs one hundred and seventy before the decline is done

Oracle rose about four percent on rising volume. The catalyst was a report that OpenAI's annual recurring revenue is approaching seventy billion dollars, above the earlier line of an annualized run rate over forty billion dollars by the middle of twenty twenty-six. A separate report on Anthropic's documents described spending and losses that do not line up. Both are secondhand reads on private companies, and the angles differ. The tape used the OpenAI revenue headline to buy Oracle.

The level matters more than the headline. A break of one hundred and seventy is what confirms the decline has stopped. From one hundred and thirty-seven to one hundred and seventy is one wide supply zone, and the market price is still far from one hundred and seventy. Above that level, it can base. Below it, the stock is still inside supply. OpenAI is changing too fast to underwrite a six-month or one-year hold from here. If you can sit through it, that is your call. If you cannot, do not use time to bet on a stabilization that has not confirmed.

Three paths

Bullish: the ten-year works toward five point zero five percent or five percent, oil stops feeding the inflation scare, and both utilities and growth benefit because the discount rate came down. Semiconductors and the big weights beat the technology sector, and the Russell is no longer the rates short. That is a tactical window through third-quarter earnings and year-end, not proof of a new long bull market.

Neutral: the ten-year grinds around five point two six percent, and systematic money does not sell a flat tape. Meta holds six hundred and ninety to seven hundred and thirty. A-S-M-L and Lam Research neither break nor fail. The bullish call stays on the page, and price does not confirm it.

Bearish: the ten-year moves toward five point five percent, and the S&P 500 falls through seven thousand five hundred. Systematic money sells again. Banks and cyclicals can be the preferred fundamental group and still get pinned by the curve. If Oracle cannot clear one hundred and seventy, the revenue headline was a bounce.
