Lower Yields Leave Inflation and Cash Flow Unresolved

Last week's equity decline cannot be explained by rising long term Treasury yields over the week. The Treasury Department's constant maturity series shows the ten year yield fell from five percent on October twenty third to four point nine four percent on October thirtieth. That is a decline of six basis points. The two year yield fell from four point six nine percent to four point five six percent, down thirteen basis points.

The ten year reading did rise to five point zero five percent on October twenty eighth, the Fed decision day. It then fell over the following two days. Yields rose on the decision day but fell over the full week. The time windows matter.

Policy has not shifted toward easing. The Fed's October twenty eighth statement left the target range at three point seven five to four percent, on a vote of ten to two. Both dissenters favored a twenty five basis point increase. The statement still described inflation as elevated and the pace of economic expansion as strong.

Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said on October thirtieth that she believed at least two more twenty five basis point hikes were needed. That is her policy view. It is not a path promised by the committee.

On October twenty ninth, the Bureau of Economic Analysis released its advance estimate for third quarter G D P and its September personal consumption expenditures data together. Real G D P grew at a seasonally adjusted annualized rate of three point four percent. That compares with two point two percent in the second quarter.

September's headline P C E price index rose zero point five percent month over month and three point six percent year over year. Core P C E rose zero point three percent for the month and three point one percent from a year earlier. Resilient growth and inflation above target offer little justification for immediate easing. Both releases followed the Fed decision, so they cannot explain the previous day's vote.

The growth picture also has limits. The same monthly report from the Bureau of Economic Analysis shows September real consumer spending was roughly flat. Real disposable income fell zero point three percent month over month. Faster quarterly growth can coexist with weaker household purchasing power in September.

The three point four percent G D P reading does not establish an across the board acceleration in demand. Whether employment and consumption hold up will help determine how much room companies have to deliver on revenue guidance.

Energy offered little relief. Reuters' October twenty third and October thirtieth settlement reports show the same December Brent contract rose from one hundred and sixteen dollars and seventy two cents to one hundred and seventeen dollars and sixty four cents a barrel. The weekly gain was zero point seven nine percent. Oil remained elevated.

Investing's historical dollar index table lists an October thirtieth close of one hundred and one point eight eight. That was up about zero point zero seven percent from October twenty third. Such a small move does not support a strong dollar liquidity squeeze as the explanation.

A six basis point decline in long term yields provides only modest valuation relief. Reuters' October thirtieth intraday report linked the technology selloff to concerns over returns on A I investment. It also described safe haven demand for Treasuries. That fits falling stocks alongside rising bond prices, but does not establish a single cause for the week's entire decline.

For technology stocks, whether revenue growth can cover capital spending may matter more than a small decline in discount rates. So may the ability to turn profits into cash. Falling yields will not necessarily help if earnings expectations deteriorate at the same time.

The policy rate and long term yields also measure different things. The policy rate is the Fed's target range for short term funding. Long term yields also reflect expectations for growth, inflation and the risks of holding longer dated debt.

A six basis point drop in the ten year does not mean every company's funding costs fall by six basis points. It does not establish how much technology profits will improve. Claims that a specific amount of liquidity has returned would require transaction level flow and financing data.

Elections and Jobs Put Earnings to Another Test

The official I S M calendar puts October manufacturing P M I on Monday, November second, and services P M I on Wednesday, November fourth. Both are scheduled for eleven p.m. Beijing time. American daylight saving time ended on November first. Eastern time is now thirteen hours behind Beijing, so summer release times no longer apply.

Treasury has scheduled its quarterly borrowing estimate for November second and quarterly refunding statement for November fourth. Debt supply volumes and the maturity mix will offer another test of whether long term yields can keep falling.

The borrowing estimate sets out funding needs; the refunding statement details maturity allocations. More long duration supply could prompt investors to demand higher yields. If demand can absorb the supply, pressure on long term yields has room to ease. The borrowing total alone does not settle the outlook for technology stocks.

Tuesday, November third, is American midterm election day, as confirmed by the Federal Election Commission. Results will depend on each state's counting process. There is no basis for assuming every outcome will be settled at a specific time on Wednesday. September job openings are also scheduled for Tuesday at eleven p.m. Beijing time.

Elections may change expectations for taxes, spending and regulation. The implications depend on the seat distribution and subsequent policy information. A particular election result does not automatically imply rising stock prices.

Employment is the main focus before the weekend. The Bureau of Labor Statistics calendar schedules preliminary third quarter productivity and unit labor costs for Thursday, November fifth. The October employment report follows on Friday, November sixth. Both arrive at nine thirty p.m. Beijing time.

Weekly initial jobless claims are due at the same time on Thursday. On monetary policy, there is no Federal Open Market Committee meeting this week; the next rate decision is scheduled for December eighth and ninth, leaving the October employment report on Friday, November sixth as the key macro anchor before the weekend.

Technology earnings remain in full swing. Company announcements and Barron's November first weekly calendar list Apple and Palantir after the American market close on Monday, November second. A M D's October sixth announcement confirms an after close release on Tuesday, November third, while Qualcomm's October twenty second announcement confirms Wednesday, November fourth after the close.

The focus remains whether demand guidance supports profits and cash generation. Chip companies need revenue and gross margins to improve together. Software companies need to convert growth into operating cash flow. Revenue growth alone will not establish that this pullback is over.

After each report, compare the stock's reaction with the broader market and its industry. Strong results followed by only a brief rally may indicate that expectations were still too high. If guidance is delivered and the stock holds up in a weak market, the case for recovery becomes stronger.

Earnings and elections are concentrated in the same week. After hours gaps can prevent stops from executing at the intended price. Keep individual event risk contained.

Tech Must Reclaim Lost Ground

Keep seven thousand five hundred and eighty as the S&P 500's initial reference. Friday's intraday break calls for stronger confirmation. A run of closes above it, followed by a recovery of seven thousand six hundred and sixty, would support greater confidence in a sustained rebound. The next higher level to reclaim is seven thousand seven hundred and twenty.

If the index closes lower again and breaks Friday's low of seven thousand five hundred and twenty seven point five six, seven thousand five hundred becomes the next area to watch. Every step requires price confirmation. No level guarantees an automatic move to the next.

Technology's internal structure also requires differentiated analysis. Technical calculations across two hundred daily closes through October thirtieth put Q Q Q's two hundred day moving average near six hundred and seventy eight dollars and thirty nine cents. Its current price is still above that average. S O X X's equivalent average is about four hundred and seventy five dollars and ninety seven cents, above Friday's close.

They have not both broken below their long term averages. A semiconductor recovery without broader technology participation would still be a localized rebound. Subsequent sessions need to show whether it spreads to more large technology stocks.

Watch first for S O X X to reclaim roughly four hundred and seventy six dollars. Then watch whether it can hold around four hundred and eighty six dollars. For Q Q Q, the first recovery reference is about six hundred and ninety six dollars. The latter two are prospective watch levels that need sustained closes; they are not established support.

If I S M and employment data show a gentle slowdown and easing wage pressure, while earnings sustain profits and cash flow, falling yields are more likely to support technology valuations. If economic data stay strong and long term yields return above five percent, the case for chasing a rally becomes less attractive.

The combination that calls for more defense is yields continuing to fall while technology loses the levels above. That would add to the possibility that earnings worries outweigh valuation relief. A sharp weakening in employment would not automatically justify buying either. It might lower yields while prompting cuts to sales and profit expectations.

Watch whether technology's underperformance against the broader market narrows. Conversely, even strong data could be manageable if companies also deliver better cash generation. Earnings could then offset some valuation pressure. Limit risk on any single trade before major releases. Adjust exposure after price action and earnings reinforce each other. The question now is whether lower long term yields will first help technology valuations recover or expose earnings pressure.
