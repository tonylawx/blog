A Strong Open Gives Way to Broad Selling

On November nineteenth, the S&P 500 closed at seven thousand one hundred seventy five point eight three, down two point zero two percent. The Nasdaq Composite fell two point two five percent, the Dow dropped two point one nine percent and the Russell two thousand lost two point one eight percent. More than nine out of ten stocks in the S&P 500 declined. The volatility index touched twenty nine point one three before ending at twenty eight point four four.

The exchange traded fund tracking the S&P 500 rose to seven hundred thirty nine dollars and ninety cents before closing at seven hundred fifteen dollars and eighty seven cents. The Nasdaq one hundred fund ended at six hundred forty five dollars and four cents. The early advance did not hold, but the reversal alone cannot establish a single cause for the day's selling.

For the S&P fund, the first area to watch below the close runs from seven hundred two to seven hundred ten dollars. A deeper scenario points to six hundred seventy one to six hundred ninety four dollars. For the Nasdaq fund, six hundred forty two dollars is the nearby reference, followed by six hundred to six hundred thirty five dollars, including six hundred seventeen. These are ranges for assessing the market's response, not guaranteed floors.

Anthropic's Opening Gain Narrows

Anthropic priced its offering at eighty three dollars a share and opened at one hundred eleven dollars and fifty cents. The shares reached one hundred fifteen dollars and ten cents, fell as low as ninety two dollars and seventy two cents, and closed at ninety seven dollars and seventeen cents. That close was about seventeen point one percent above the offering price, but about twelve point nine percent below the opening trade.

At eight in the evening Eastern time, the after hours quote was ninety two dollars and fifty one cents. After hours prices change and can reflect relatively light trading. Investors who received shares at the offering price and those who bought in the open market faced different first day outcomes.

The next question is whether buyers return after the debut. One session does not determine the long term value of the company. A rebound in a single new listing also would not establish that the broader market has found support.

A Valuation Exercise Depends on Its Assumptions

Suppose estimated earnings per share for the S&P 500 are about three hundred eighty eight dollars. Applying a range of eighteen to twenty two times earnings gives an illustrative index range of roughly seven thousand to eight thousand five hundred forty. The estimate and the multiples are assumptions, not a forecast or a guaranteed destination.

Now suppose a group representing twenty five percent of that earnings base suffers a thirty percent earnings decline while the rest is unchanged. The combined reduction would be seven point five percent. The rough earnings estimate would fall to about three hundred fifty nine dollars. Applying the same eighteen times multiple gives an index value around six thousand four hundred sixty.

That arithmetic does not automatically produce a target of six thousand seven hundred to six thousand eight hundred. It does not establish that the earnings decline will occur. Earnings, company weights and valuation multiples could all differ. The exercise shows how the assumptions connect.

The four hundred sixty nine to four hundred eighty five dollar band is a lower scenario, not a range reached that day.

It reported about eighteen point six billion dollars in free cash flow and expects full year fiscal twenty twenty seven free cash flow to stay positive. Finance leases and other investing outflows are separate items and should not be silently combined with that calculation.

On October twenty ninth, Microsoft priced twenty five billion dollars of senior unsecured notes. Its stated general corporate purposes include possible capital spending, share repurchases, acquisitions and debt repayment. That does not establish that all of the proceeds were designated for A I infrastructure.
