0 · MARKET

The tape was easy. The structure was not.

Monday was a broad up day. All four major indexes rose, and the Nasdaq made another record high. Inside the S&P 500, three hundred and seventeen names rose and one hundred and eighty-three fell. Ten of eleven sectors closed green. Real estate was the only one down.

The information content was small. The structure did not change. Money is still crowded into the technology and semiconductor names with the hardest earnings. The S&P 500 itself is a worse and worse stand-in for most stocks. Hedge-fund books say it plainly. Gross leverage is falling, the technology long was added only a little, and every other sector was a net sale. If you already own the A.I. and semiconductor cycle, let the tape carry it. If you are not in, do not chase a new high. Look for names that have not started.

1 · MARKET

Services: mixed, and prices are hot

The September non-manufacturing purchasing-managers index fell from fifty-five point four in August to fifty-four point nine, a touch under the fifty-five consensus, still above the fifty-four point one average of the past twelve months. The business-activity index dropped from sixty-one point seven to fifty-six point five. New orders weakened with it. Those two lines did most of the dragging.

The other side ran hot. The prices index rose from seventy-two point six to seventy-four, above the seventy-three point three consensus. Employment, after two months of contraction, moved back above fifty, from forty-seven point eight to fifty point one, better than the forty-eight point eight consensus. Thirteen industries reported growth, one more than in August. Four reported contraction, one fewer.

Services are still expanding, at a moderate pace, and slower than the S&P Global services survey. Higher prices and employment back in expansion are not friendly for rate hopes. The indexes ignored the print. It is background, not the trade.

2 · MARKET

The S&P 500 has come unhooked from almost everything

Trying to read whether the market is fine off the S&P 500 gets more confusing the longer you look.

The index and the equal-weight book have diverged. The three-month realized correlation between the S&P 500 and the equal-weight index fell to zero point five nine eight on September twenty-second, a fifteen-year low, four standard deviations under the zero point nine three long-run average. As of Friday it was only zero point six three. The S&P 500 is sitting near a high. The equal-weight index had already given a lot back. That is why the Nasdaq can print records while consumer stocks you actually own keep falling.

A.I. and the rest of the index have split. Since August, the correlation between an A.I. portfolio and the S&P 500 excluding A.I. constituents is the lowest, and the most negative, on record.

The macro anchors have slipped too. Three-month realized correlation with crude is negative zero point five eight, not far from June's record negative zero point six two, against a fifteen-year average of positive zero point two three. Correlation with the ten-year yield is negative zero point five six, versus a June low of negative zero point six four and an average of positive zero point two four.

Sectors have stopped moving with the index. Staples, energy, utilities, and health care all have sixty-three-day correlations with the S&P 500 at zero or below. Staples and energy are at twenty-year lows. Bank of America's equity-derivatives desk says uncertainty keeps repricing A.I. and policy winners and losers, which has crushed both cross-industry and within-industry correlation. Their chart puts stock-to-industry correlation over the past twelve months near zero point one, below the fifth percentile of data since nineteen ninety-one. Breadth is ugly too. The five-day average of S&P 500 members at a fifty-two-week high minus those at a fifty-two-week low has fallen to negative twenty-five percent, the worst in eighteen months.

The mechanism is simple. The good A.I.-linked stocks are rising. Everything unrelated is either ignored or still falling. Index weight sits in the first group, so a new index high and a falling majority can exist at the same time. Do not use the index to grade a single-stock position. Ask whether that stock is on the side where estimates are still being revised up.

3 · LIQUIDITY

Hedge funds cut leverage and sat on technology

Last week, net leverage in U.S. fundamental long-short books fell for a third straight week, another two point two percentage points to forty-six point four percent from forty-eight point six percent. That is the lowest since around the April twenty twenty-three low, near a five-year low, the second percentile. The long-short ratio fell one point nine percent to one point five seven, the first percentile on a five-year look and the zeroth percentile on a one-year look. The lowest. The market rose. The aggregate long did not.

They did buy something. Goldman data show U.S. technology was the only sector with net buying in September, and the highest net-buying share since February twenty twenty-three. Cyclicals, defensives, and consumer discretionary were all net sold. The technology buying was not a chase. It was enough not to fall behind a market that kept pushing technology higher.

There is almost no conviction to be long the S&P 500 as a whole, and no direction. The book is sitting on the technology names with the strongest earnings, adding a little, waiting to be carried. Why is the money piled there? Because inside the S&P 500's earnings, technology, A.I., and semiconductors are doing the most of the lifting, and the revisions are the fastest. Earnings come first. The buying follows. The other sectors are not the hot trade, and a weak tape does not give them much to say.

If your book looks like that hedge-fund book, you are the one being carried. If there is no add, let the existing position work. Do not buy weak groups just to own the other sectors.

4 · SEMIS

Nvidia, Microsoft, Taiwan Semiconductor

Nvidia finally closed at a record high. That is an attitude, not another session of leaning on the old high. Nearby support is two hundred and nineteen to two hundred and thirty-two, the gap it jumped through. The valuation case has not been expensive. The stock is only around two hundred and thirty to two hundred and forty. Even on twenty to thirty times, with the growth rate cut in half, the forward range into the fiscal year ending January twenty twenty-eight is two hundred and fifty-one to three hundred and seventy-seven. The midpoint, three hundred and fourteen, is still roughly twenty to thirty percent of room. Whichever forward set you use, it is not expensive. There is no fresh negative. Ignore the small technical noise and keep the position.

Microsoft closed at five hundred and twenty-five, a recent high, with a session high of five hundred and thirty-two. There is one medium supply zone overhead. Through five hundred and thirty-four there is almost nothing. The pocket from five hundred and thirty-four to five hundred and fifty is small enough to ignore. The medium-term look is six hundred and above. A fiscal ceiling for the year ending June twenty twenty-seven is six hundred and fourteen, and a midpoint for the year ending June twenty twenty-eight is six hundred and thirty-two. This is not a high-beta name. It will not be fast. Hold it and let another twenty percent or so come out. That is a reasonable path.

Taiwan Semiconductor printed a record high on three straight days of stepped-up volume. That is a strong-stock pattern. Leave it alone. The twenty twenty-seven forward range is three hundred and twenty-one to five hundred and ninety-three, midpoint four hundred and fifty-seven, and the price is already through the midpoint. It has run ahead of itself. If you are in, do not touch it. If you are flat, it is a hard buy. Chasing, then giving back even to four hundred, hurts. The only clean add is a pullback. The stock's own history is full of them. If the fundamentals are still intact on the way down, that is when to add. What was not bought is not bought. This semiconductor cycle is not finished.

5 · MARKET

The other names

SpaceX rose on rising volume for a second day and looks to have cleared the prior shelf, through one hundred and fifty and toward one hundred and seventy. The chart is strong. It has not traded for a year. Many zones have never been tested, so support and resistance are guesses. The area near one hundred was tested once. One hundred and fifty has been tested several times. You only learn whether one hundred and fifty still matters after price comes back and steps on it again. No clean technical map yet. The speculative bid is picking up, and Tesla is still working higher.

Tesla is around three hundred and eighty. The closer it gets to four hundred and fifteen, the more the offer matters. Until it gets there, hold it and do nothing.

Amazon and Google are still reasonable entry zones at the current price. Microsoft has already started and has run. Meta was not bought on the lows, and after a forty percent move it is hard to start. If these are names you want, the quiet part of the range is when the position gets built.

Broadcom is unchanged. It can be bought around here. A break of three hundred and twenty-four is the hedge. Three hundred and twenty-four is the watershed. Through it, institutions are marking down the growth rate in advance. If it holds and the larger trend is still turning up, there is no reason to sell in a hurry. The closer it trades to three hundred and twenty-four, the smaller the stop and the better the payoff against the risk, provided the break is actually sold and not sat through.

The Philadelphia semiconductor index is still chopping sideways. The semiconductor fund barely moved.

6 · MARKET

Three paths

Bullish: the Nasdaq keeps closing at highs, Microsoft holds above five hundred and thirty-four, and Nvidia holds the two hundred and nineteen to two hundred and thirty-two gap. Money stays on the technology line where estimates are still rising.

Neutral: the S&P 500 grinds near the highs, correlations stay broken, and semiconductors chop. Holders let the position work. Amazon and Google, the names that have not started, get built slowly.

Bearish: Broadcom loses three hundred and twenty-four, which is the market pricing a slower growth rate. Hedge first. If Taiwan Semiconductor pulls back, add only after the fundamentals still check out. Do not add size at the high.
