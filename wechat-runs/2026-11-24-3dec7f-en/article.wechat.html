0 · MARKET

The market tape

Tuesday, November twenty fourth, twenty twenty six. U.S. stocks split. The S&P 500 closed at seven thousand two hundred and one, down zero point five percent. The Nasdaq fell one point five percent. The Dow rose zero point eight five percent, ending at fifty thousand and eighteen.

Breadth across the S&P 500 remained positive. Three hundred and forty eight stocks rose, while one hundred and fifty four declined. Consumer staples and health care held up better. The pressure stayed concentrated in technology and semiconductors.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about seven point nine percent. All thirty constituents closed lower. The index had surged fourteen percent in the prior session, then fell almost eight percent the next day. That is a volatile rebound and reversal pattern, not steady accumulation.

Nvidia fell five point nine percent. A M D dropped eight point nine percent. Broadcom lost four percent, and Micron slid eleven percent. The weakness looked concentrated in technology positions, rather than a broad market panic.

Policy headlines added uncertainty. The U.S. Trade Representative was close to releasing findings from an industrial capacity investigation covering sixteen major trading partners. New tariffs were possible, but the final rates were still unknown. That added to the risk backdrop, but it does not explain the entire semiconductor decline.

Consumer confidence improved. The Conference Board index rose eleven point four points to one hundred and three point three in November. That was above expectations of ninety two point five, and the highest reading since January twenty twenty five. Consumers also became more optimistic about the labour market outlook.

1 · RISK

Credit markets are flagging funding risk

The A I trade is shifting from the size of capital spending to the cost of funding and the return on that spending. Building data centres is no longer the only question. The bigger test is whether the new capacity can produce revenue, earnings, and free cash flow.

Public credit trading data put Oracle's five year credit default swap spread around three hundred and sixty to three hundred and seventy basis points. CoreWeave was above one thousand basis points. Nvidia was around one hundred and forty three, and Broadcom around one hundred and eighty.

Meta was near one hundred and fifteen basis points. Amazon and Alphabet were around one hundred. Microsoft was near fifty two basis points. The latest trade dates differ across companies, and single name trading can be thin. These figures show direction. They are not firm live quotes.

A wider credit default swap spread means investors want more compensation for credit protection. It does not mean a company is about to default. It also does not replace a rating agency assessment. Some reported rating changes could not be matched consistently to agency announcements, so they should not be treated as confirmed facts.

The funding mix is the real difference. Large cloud companies with enough operating cash flow to fund investment are in a different position. Infrastructure providers that need repeated debt issuance, equity financing, or customer prepayments face a tougher test.

Wider spreads raise refinancing costs. They can also make lower return data centre projects less attractive. My view is that demand for A I infrastructure has not disappeared. But investors are raising the bar for returns. Watch data centre utilization, cloud revenue growth, capital spending, depreciation, and free cash flow. Orders and construction plans only become durable returns when end customers keep paying for the capacity.

2 · EARNINGS

Valuations are moving before earnings estimates

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is down more than twenty percent from its recent high. That puts it in technical bear market territory. It describes the price decline, but it does not prove the industry's fundamentals have collapsed. Much of the pressure still looks like multiple compression. The next stage would be more serious if earnings estimates began falling across the sector.

Memory stocks need a separate lens. Micron fell more than eleven percent on the day. But memory pricing, inventories, and supply conditions can change quickly. A low forward earnings multiple does not automatically make a memory stock cheap. When peak earnings roll over, earnings per share can fall quickly. That can make the headline valuation misleading.

Nvidia has its own set of risks. Investors need to watch customer funding, data centre construction, supply chain commitments, and corporate governance. A management change does not automatically fix financing links, customer concentration, or cash flow quality. Track earnings forecasts for Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom. Those changes will tell us more than one day's index move.

3 · SETUP

Stock setups — wait for support to hold

A M D closed at four hundred and thirteen dollars and twenty seven cents. It fell eight point nine percent and moved below its two hundred day moving average again. The four hundred to four hundred and twenty four dollar area needs to be reclaimed. Support there still looks fragile. If A M D cannot recover four hundred dollars, the next notable historical trading zone sits below three hundred and sixty six dollars.

That is an area to monitor, not a guaranteed downside target. T S M C and A S M L both filled the gaps created by the prior session's rebound. Their short term setups have weakened. The next test is whether each can stabilize inside the previous trading range and attract a second wave of buyers.

One quiet session after a gap fill is not enough to confirm a reversal. A stress case valuation floor for T S M C is separate from technical support. It should not be treated as an entry price. Broadcom broke below the prior week's low. Its rebound has also lagged parts of the group. When the stock closes at a fresh low, even an intraday lower wick deserves less weight.

Marvell has been volatile since losing one hundred and sixty dollars. A long lower wick, a recovery into the prior range, and follow through buying are needed before calling a near term base. Nvidia's support area sits around one hundred and thirty two to one hundred and fifty dollars. Buyers still need to show up there. Overhead resistance sits around one hundred and seventy four to one hundred and ninety four dollars.

A bounce after a weak open is not a new uptrend. Volume needs to improve, and price needs to reclaim key levels over more than one session.

4 · MARKET

The S&P 500 is sitting on seven thousand two hundred

The S&P 500 closed at seven thousand two hundred and one, almost exactly on seven thousand two hundred. That is the near term line to watch. Seven thousand one hundred is the more important downside stress level.

If the index holds seven thousand two hundred and forms a second leg of strong buying near the prior lows, staged entries become more reasonable. A break below seven thousand one hundred would make the short term structure materially weaker.

There is little reason to rush orders in the first thirty minutes after the open. The final hour matters more, especially the last thirty minutes. That is when buyers have to show whether they are willing to absorb supply.

The core view is straightforward. Semiconductors are in a technical bear market, but that alone does not invalidate the A I investment thesis. The real test is whether spending turns into cash returns before higher funding costs squeeze the expansion cycle. If earnings estimates avoid broad and sustained cuts, the market may remain in a volatile valuation reset.

If earnings expectations start falling as well, the risk map for the entire A I capital spending chain needs to be reassessed.
