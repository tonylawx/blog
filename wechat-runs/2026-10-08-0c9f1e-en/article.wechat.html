0 · MARKET

Breadth was fine

There is no public note on Friday. The next one is after the close on Monday.

Thursday the Nasdaq one hundred fell one point three nine percent, and the Nasdaq Composite fell one point two five percent. Plenty of tech names were down three or four points, which looks like a break if that is all you see. It was not. Inside the S&P 500, three hundred thirty-nine names rose and one hundred sixty-three fell. The equal-weight S&P rose zero point six percent. Eight of eleven sectors rose. Of the three that fell, health care and utilities were down only a fraction of a point. Tech itself was down nearly two points.

The logic is the same as the day before. Third-quarter names that already had low growth expectations have an easier bar to beat. Thursday was tech paying back the groups that had been weak. It was not a broad break.

Read the advance-decline line before the Nasdaq candle. A three hundred thirty-nine to one hundred sixty-three tape is not a market-wide selloff.

3 · MARKET

Samsung's miss is real. The OpenAI gap was an accounting note

The Philadelphia semiconductor fund, S-O-X-X, fell three point three five percent. The other semiconductor fund, S-M-H, fell two point eight four percent. Two headlines. Samsung's third-quarter operating profit and revenue both missed, and memory was weak before the open. That one is real, and it lands on memory.

During the session, artificial-intelligence names were offered together. The Financial Times said OpenAI's annual revenue was twenty billion dollars below the prior figure, fifty billion instead of seventy billion. That cooled the demand story. CNBC later said the gap is an accounting method. Semiconductors got some of the loss back after that. Thursday's group was mostly those two headlines pushing it around.

Price the memory group off Samsung. Do not price the demand story as if revenue had been cut in half. An accounting gap and an earnings miss are different.
