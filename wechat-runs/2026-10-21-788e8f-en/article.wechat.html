0 · MOMENTUM

The rebound does not mean A I leadership is back

U.S. stocks advanced on Wednesday, October twenty first. The S and P five hundred rose about zero point six percent, the Nasdaq gained one point one percent, and the Dow added roughly zero point seven percent. The rebound eased near term pressure, but one session does not prove money has returned to mega cap tech. The next test is whether earnings translate A I spending into durable revenue, margins, and cash flow.

1 · MARKET

Lam Research: Record results still need order follow through

Lam Research reported September quarter revenue of eight point two one nine billion dollars, up twenty two percent sequentially. GAAP operating margin reached forty percent, and diluted earnings per share was two dollars and nineteen cents. For the December quarter, management guided to revenue of eight point six billion dollars, plus or minus four hundred million dollars, and GAAP diluted earnings per share of two dollars and thirty three cents, plus or minus fifteen cents. Stronger revenue, better margins, and higher guidance point to continued support for wafer fabrication equipment demand.

The risk is extrapolating one strong quarter too far. Semiconductor equipment valuations already reflect substantial A I investment expectations. Watch customer capital spending, whether order growth broadens, and whether guidance keeps moving higher. If orders and revenue fail to match expectations, a pullback would not be surprising. Long term exposure should be confirmed by orders and guidance, not added solely on the strength of one report.

2 · CAPEX

Meta: Profit took a one off hit, but twenty twenty seven capital spending is still a blank

Meta generated sixty four point six zero one billion dollars of third quarter revenue, up twenty six percent year over year. Ad impressions rose ten percent and the average price per ad increased fourteen percent, although impression growth slowed from fourteen percent a year earlier. Operating margin fell to twenty percent from forty percent, including a roughly nine point eight nine billion dollar legal settlement charge. Excluding that charge, Meta reported about twenty two point eight eight billion dollars of operating income and adjusted earnings per share of roughly seven dollars and forty cents. The GAAP decline alone does not establish that the core business has deteriorated, but rising costs and margin pressure remain relevant.

Meta narrowed its twenty twenty six capital expenditure outlook to one hundred and thirty five billion dollars to one hundred and forty five billion dollars. It expects capital spending to increase further in twenty twenty seven and beyond, yet it did not provide a number. That leaves investors without the quantitative signal that matters most to the A I supply chain. Higher spending is not automatically bullish: investment pace, compute utilization, and monetization must exceed expectations. If spending growth proves modest or revenue conversion disappoints, chip suppliers will have a harder time defending premium valuations on narrative alone.

For the chart, six hundred and nine dollars to seven hundred and thirty dollars remains a broad area to monitor. A break below six hundred and nine dollars would put six hundred and four to six hundred and eight dollars and then five hundred and ninety seven to six hundred and three dollars into focus. Below five hundred and ninety seven dollars, the current range would weaken materially. These are reference zones, not automatic buy signals. Stabilization in price and clearer guidance would provide better confirmation.

3 · CAPEX

Tesla: Positive free cash flow, but the spending cadence still matters

Tesla reported third quarter revenue of about twenty eight point zero four six billion dollars and adjusted earnings per share of forty eight cents. Operating cash flow was six point two six three billion dollars, capital expenditures were four point seven three two billion dollars, and free cash flow was one point five three one billion dollars. Positive free cash flow is encouraging, but capital spending fell from five point seven eight nine billion dollars in the prior quarter. That makes it important to distinguish operating improvement from the timing of spending.

GAAP operating margin was about two point six percent, versus five point eight percent a year earlier. One quarter of stronger cash flow is not yet a durable earnings inflection. Watch whether investment in A I, autonomous driving, and energy storage continues while margins stabilize. If capital spending rebounds without corresponding revenue and gross profit improvement, free cash flow may come under pressure again. If spending is cut to protect cash, investors may question the pace of Tesla's growth projects.

The tighter trading range to monitor is three hundred and thirty one dollars to four hundred and eight dollars, with three hundred and fifteen dollars to four hundred and sixty five dollars capturing a wider range. The setup does not offer an obvious low valuation long term entry. Swing traders should define position size and exit levels rather than assume a range must be revisited.

4 · EARNINGS

Google: Let earnings test the ad growth concern

The key question for Google is whether ad growth slowed late in the quarter, as one research view suggested. That view is not a confirmed fact and should not be treated as next week's earnings outcome in advance. Meta's slower ad impression growth is a signal to watch, but it does not prove the entire ad market is cooling. Ad pricing, impressions, retail ad demand, and cloud results need to be assessed together.

Around three hundred and forty six dollars is the first long term level to watch; three hundred and eleven to three hundred and twenty five dollars and two hundred and ninety eight to three hundred and eight dollars are lower areas of interest. A break below two hundred and ninety eight dollars would raise structural risk. Until the earnings release adds clarity, focus on the result versus expectations rather than making a directional bet on one bearish note.

5 · SETUP

Short positioning and buybacks still need confirmation

Markets remain caught between semiconductor and A I investment expectations. Crowded short positioning can fuel a squeeze if prices rise persistently. But if indexes break key support while corporate capital spending disappoints, shorts may simply add to the downside. Positioning alone cannot determine which path wins.

Buybacks can support demand when repurchase windows reopen, but an authorization is not the same as an actual daily purchase and does not guarantee companies will buy at lows. Watch several sessions of volume, breadth, and large cap tech earnings reactions. A rebound supported by broader participation and expanding volume would be more credible. If only a few index heavyweights carry the tape, A I still has to prove itself through earnings.
