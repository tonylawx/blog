0 · LIQUIDITY

The dip shrank in volume

Wednesday was a down day across the four indexes. The Russell fell the most. Inside the S&P 500, three hundred sixty-one names fell and one hundred thirty-eight rose. Only health care finished higher. The other ten sectors fell. Industrials dropped two point one percent, and Caterpillar fell more than five percent, the worst Dow name of the day.

After several up days, a down print gets read as risk coming back. As of Wednesday, it is not. Outside the Russell, the other three indexes traded even less than Tuesday. A small decline on shrinking volume is usually noise.

The things that matter are the hike in the minutes, and the new SpaceX debt. The stock is S-P-C-X.

1 · MARKET

The minutes backed the September hike

Everyone at the September meeting, voters and non-voters, supported the twenty-five basis point increase. Most thought another hike before year-end could be appropriate. Inflation is still high, the labor market looks close to full employment, and the inflation risk is to the upside. Some participants said that upside has become clearer in recent months. A few also said the artificial-intelligence buildout could push demand ahead of supply over the medium term and add to inflation. The rise in long-term Treasury yields was pinned mainly on real rates. Treasury inflation-protected securities moved up with them.

The New York Fed's consumer survey came the same day. Median one-year and three-year inflation expectations edged up to three point nine percent and three point three percent. The one-year figure is the highest since May twenty twenty-three. The five-year stayed at three percent. The mean unemployment expectation fell half a percentage point to forty-three point nine percent. The average probability of job loss over the next twelve months fell to thirteen point five percent from thirteen point eight percent, the lowest since December twenty twenty-four. Expected household income growth rose to three point one percent from three percent, the highest since February twenty twenty-five. Perceptions and expectations of household finances both worsened.

The minutes leave another hike on the table. That is a valuation constraint for growth. It is a separate question from one light-volume down day.

2 · SEMIS

SpaceX wants to borrow again for chips

The Financial Times says SpaceX, S-P-C-X, is lining up another forty billion dollars through an Apollo-led special purpose vehicle, to buy Nvidia chips. About ten billion would be bank loans and thirty billion investment-grade bonds. The June I-P-O raised eighty-six billion. Less than two weeks later the company sold twenty-five billion of investment-grade bonds. If this deal lands, debt raised or about to be raised since the listing is sixty-five billion, about seventy-six percent of the listing proceeds.

The twenty fifty-six bond is down to eighty-four point seven cents. It was issued in June, as part of that twenty-five billion senior unsecured deal, at ninety-eight point eight three cents. Credit default swaps rose sixteen point two basis points to one hundred ninety-seven point five six, a record. That swap price looks like junk. The rating is still one notch above junk. The price has moved to a junk tape. The rating has not.

Starlink had thirteen point five million consumer users worldwide as of September twenty-sixth. September adds were three hundred seventy-two thousand, below August's six hundred thousand-plus, the low since March. The add curve is jagged. One slow month is not a trend break. For bond buyers the point is narrower: Starlink makes money, and the current pace of revenue still does not cover the chip spend. Borrowing against Nvidia chips is likely to become a habit. The prospectus is clear that the big valuation piece is the combined x-A-I business, not launches, and not Starlink on its own.

Data centers and power assets are all issuing. Investment-grade paper bought in June can already be deeply underwater. SpaceX is the example.

The stock has been public for less than a year, so support and resistance have not been tested enough. The heaviest trade is one hundred forty to one hundred fifty-five, then one hundred to one hundred twenty. Overhead supply is unclear because the history is short. Some of it may already have been sold on this decline. Tuesday was a stall with a record close, which reads neutral. Wednesday's debt headline took it down two point five one percent, and it is still a stall. Closer to one hundred fifty-five it starts to look like support. How far it can fall is not knowable if short-term money heats up.

Holders from one hundred to one hundred twenty can sit. Buying one hundred forty to one hundred fifty-five again is like buying the listing day a second time. That is not a short-term trade.

3 · SEMIS

Apple's home devices will not move the earnings much

Bloomberg says Apple is working with L-G Electronics on a wider smart-home line. A launch as soon as next Tuesday could include doorbells, locks, thermostats, indoor and outdoor cameras, and floodlight cameras. Amazon and Google already sell some of these. Apple's old home lineup barely touched locks and cameras. Ternus, who spent about twenty years on core product design and hardware engineering, thinks the home should not stop at the television and the home hub.

Phones, tablets, and computers do not have much left to invent. New models are mostly processors and hardware. A new line is useful. The hardware market is small next to Apple's revenue, on the order of a few hundred billion, and Apple will not take all of it. Doorbells and locks at twenty to thirty percent gross margin would already be decent. Security tied to alarms and monitoring can run sixty to eighty percent. Near term this is a positive headline. It will not lift results much.

The forward view is unchanged. A lot of the earnings-per-share lift is buybacks. Growth is slow, and the price is already high. The September twenty twenty-seven forward value is two hundred eighty-eight dollars. After the fourth-quarter report, the model rolls to September twenty twenty-eight. At three hundred thirty dollars, the stock is already near the top of that forward value.

On the chart, three hundred twenty-six to three hundred thirty-nine is medium support, not the key. The key is three hundred five to three hundred seventeen. A break of three hundred twenty-six opens a chop from three hundred five to three hundred thirty-nine. A break of three hundred five is the bearish turn, with two hundred eighty-one and below in view. The forward value can support a move back under two hundred eighty. If three hundred five holds, it keeps chopping.

The home launch does not change the fact that three hundred thirty is already at the forward ceiling. Watch three hundred five. Do not trade the break before it happens.

4 · RISK

Meta is back in the pullback zone, and today is not the buy

Meta fell about two points. Six hundred ninety to seven hundred thirty is the pullback zone after the breakout. Holding it keeps the structure pointed at a new high. A pullback into support is not a one-way rise. Wednesday was a full-bodied down candle, with no reversal and no defense during the day. That is not a buy. It is only a return to the zone. A buy needs a later sign that the decline has stopped.

A break of six hundred ninety opens a chop from six hundred fifty-six to seven hundred thirty. That is still not a bear trend. The trend change is a break of six hundred fifty-six. A right-side trade back to the old high waits for a stop inside six hundred ninety to seven hundred thirty. Longer money can also look at six hundred fifty-six to six hundred eighty. The older five hundred to seven hundred band is the long window, and the path in between is not straight.

Wednesday did not break a key level. Do not treat a return to six hundred ninety as a completed buy.

5 · MARKET

Caterpillar fell five point seven five percent, and the headline was not about Caterpillar

Caterpillar, C-A-T, fell five point seven five percent as industrials were sold. It is a large weight, so a sector sale hits it. The Federal Trade Commission and the Agriculture Department asked the public on Wednesday for information on competition in farm-equipment manufacturing and distribution. That is not a monopoly finding against Caterpillar, and nobody was fined. Deere is the purer agriculture name, and it fell less. Caterpillar itself does not have much farm business. Industrial and electrical equipment matter more.

The price is the problem. The twenty twenty-seven forward cap is eight hundred seventeen to eight hundred fourteen. After the drop the stock is still eight hundred thirteen. The group is weak, so it gets sold, and some of that is profit-taking. It is not in a downtrend yet. Mean reversion starts under seven hundred ninety-eight, and it has not broken. If it holds, it stays expensive. A break opens a path under seven hundred twenty. Even six hundred seventy-one to seven hundred twenty is still rich. A more reasonable zone is just above six hundred, with five hundred forty-six to six hundred six below that.

The power-equipment upcycle has stopped being revised higher. A year out, some of those forecasts are already flat or down. That is the cycle.

Until seven hundred ninety-eight breaks, this is not the mean-reversion trade. The Agriculture Department request is not a Caterpillar penalty.

6 · MARKET

Midterm fourth quarters rose twenty-one times out of twenty-three

Deutsche Bank counted the S&P 500 in the fourth quarter after the last twenty-three midterms. It rose twenty-one times and finished the quarter lower twice. Fourth quarters are a strong season with or without a midterm. The average gain is about seven percent, and the last few years pull that average up. A seven percent average is not a forecast for this year.

The quarter can be volatile. A midterm year is not an instruction that the fourth quarter must fall.

Seasonality is still there. Do not use seven percent as the target. Volatility and a light-volume down day are two different ledgers.
