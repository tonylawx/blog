The Market Was Up, but Not Every Stock Joined In

On Tuesday, November third, twenty twenty six, the S and P five hundred gained about zero point four percent. The Nasdaq Composite gained about zero point eight percent. The Dow fell about zero point four percent. Nvidia and other big technology stocks helped lift the major indexes. Yet more S and P five hundred members fell than rose. The rebound was real, but it was not broad.

September job openings rose to seven point five two three million. That was up from a revised seven point one nine million in August. The job openings rate and the voluntary quits rate both increased. The labour market still looks firm enough to keep interest rate worries alive.

A stronger market would need more stocks joining the advance. An index gain led by just a few technology giants is less reassuring.

A M D's Revenue Is Growing Faster Than Its Cash Flow

A M D reported third quarter revenue of thirteen point four billion dollars, up forty five percent from a year earlier. Data centre revenue reached eight point five billion dollars, nearly doubling. Adjusted earnings were two dollars and nine cents a share. Adjusted gross margin was fifty six percent.

The company expects about seventeen billion dollars in fourth quarter revenue. That would mean roughly sixty six percent growth from a year earlier. The company expects adjusted gross margin to ease to fifty five percent. Those are strong forecasts, but they are still forecasts. The latest cash flow numbers tell a more complicated story.

Operating cash flow fell from two point three six six billion dollars in the second quarter to one point two zero two billion in the third. Free cash flow dropped from one point five five eight billion dollars to five hundred and fifty four million. That is a decline of roughly sixty four percent.

Capital spending was actually lower. So the cash flow decline cannot be blamed on higher equipment purchases alone. Inventory rose thirty five percent in the quarter, reaching eleven point five billion dollars. Accounts receivable rose twenty six percent, reaching nine point two billion. Both increased faster than quarterly sales.

Some of that inventory may support future deliveries. But the company still needs to collect cash from customers. Total debt grew to about seven point one billion dollars. A M D also held about fifteen point eight billion dollars in cash and short term investments. That is important context. It is not the same as an immediate debt crisis.

A M D closed regular trading at four hundred and eighty three dollars and ninety cents. After its earnings release, the shares traded near five hundred and eight dollars. Look for a regular session hold above five hundred to five hundred and ten dollars before treating the rebound as more durable.

2 · MARKET

Palantir Has a Different Growth Problem

Palantir reported two point three one three billion dollars in quarterly revenue. That was up ninety six percent year over year. Its American commercial business grew even faster, up one hundred and forty eight percent. Commercial revenue in the United States passed government revenue for the first time.

Palantir also produced about one point two billion dollars of adjusted free cash flow. That is strong cash generation. The concern is the pace of new contracts. Total contract value signed during the quarter rose only six percent year over year. American commercial contract value grew eleven percent.

But contracts and recognized revenue are different measures. One slow quarter of new deal signings does not guarantee slow revenue next quarter. American commercial remaining deal value still increased seventy nine percent from a year earlier. Investors need to watch whether new bookings pick up again and whether contracted work becomes cash revenue.

Palantir finished Tuesday at one hundred and seventy five dollars and forty cents. It dipped below one hundred and sixty nine intraday, then recovered. That level is still contested, not a confirmed ceiling.

3 · MARKET

Election Odds Do Not Equal Election Outcomes

American voters cast ballots in the midterm elections on November third. Prediction markets briefly put the odds of a Democratic sweep of Congress near ninety nine percent. That number was a market price at a particular moment. It was not an official election result.

Later reports indicated that Democrats had secured control of the Senate. The House outcome still needed further counting when those reports appeared. Congress can investigate technology companies and their financing. Lawmakers may examine data centre power use, project approvals and the growth of private credit.

Even so, a presidential veto can block major new federal laws. State and local construction approvals have their own processes. Rather than trading on one prediction market percentage, watch for actual policies, investigations and changes in spending plans.

4 · SETUP

Watch Volatility, but Do Not Trade on One Number

The Vix index ended Tuesday around twenty one. It measures expected stock market volatility over roughly thirty days. A reading above twenty can mean investors are paying more for protection. It does not mean stocks must fall the next day.

In the best case, A M D delivers its strong revenue outlook and cash flow improves. Palantir's bookings recover. Election uncertainty fades. In a mixed case, revenue remains strong but stock prices swing as investors question margins, working capital and valuation.

In a weaker case, big cloud customers slow their spending and financial pressure spreads across the technology sector. If your portfolio is heavy in technology shares, check the concentration first. Make sure you can fund any short puts that get assigned.

Protective puts and collars can help, but protection costs money. Sometimes a smaller position is simpler. Strong demand for artificial intelligence matters. The cash that eventually reaches shareholders matters more.
