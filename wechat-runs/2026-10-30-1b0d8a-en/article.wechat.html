Slow the pace of buying

Friday's selloff calls for a slower pace of buying. On October thirtieth, Q Q Q closed at six hundred and ninety dollars and forty nine cents. It fell three point four seven percent.

S O X X closed at four hundred and seventy one dollars and fifty one cents. It fell eight point two four percent. Q Q Q lost the previously watched support at six hundred and ninety six dollars. S O X X lost support at four hundred and eighty six dollars.

Semiconductors fell harder than the Nasdaq ETF. That weakens the case for chip stocks to lead a technology rebound. Any bounce next week needs to be judged by whether those lost levels can be recovered. Defensives held up better. The consumer staples ETF, X L P, rose one point four one percent. That is consistent with weaker risk appetite and a preference for defensives. A few ETFs cannot establish a market wide liquidity crisis.

Underestimating the decline can encourage more buying. Treating it as a full collapse can lead to selling everything during a sharp decline. The size of the decline alone does not settle either question.

Fed officials Logan and Musalem continued to argue for further monetary tightening on Friday. Technology stocks must contend with funding costs and discount rates alongside A I demand. First assess how much more downside existing positions can absorb. A large decline does not remove the need to assess valuation before buying.

1 · MARKET

The A I buildout needs funding

The A I buildout requires spending before customer payments and investment returns typically arrive. Data centers, chips, power commitments, and leases all need funding. Whatever operating cash flow cannot cover must come from creditors or shareholders. Borrowing rates, collateral terms, and maturities all affect how long expansion can continue.

Microsoft can still secure large commitments of long term debt capital. On October twenty ninth, the company priced a twenty five billion dollar bond offering. It had ten tranches. Maturities ranged from roughly two to forty years.

Settlement was expected on November fourth. Bloomberg reported peak orders of about one hundred and fifteen billion dollars. That was four point six times the offering size. The order book measures demand; the offering itself remained twenty five billion dollars.

Microsoft disclosed several general corporate uses for the proceeds. It did not commit all the funds to A I Credit pressure at CoreWeave drew attention in mid October. Reuters reported on October fifteenth that the cost of five year default protection had exceeded one thousand basis points.

Higher protection costs reflect credit market concerns. They could raise the hurdle for subsequent borrowing and refinancing. They do not mean a default has occurred. The Financial Times reported on October thirtieth that Blackstone expected Anthropic might initially fail to obtain an investment grade rating after going public. This was an expectation about a future rating.

A below investment grade rating could affect the pool of buyers and financing costs. Distinguish plans for an initial public offering from financing commitments when assessing a funding gap. Also check whether committed funds have actually settled. Strong bond demand and an attractive stock are separate judgments. Creditors focus on receiving principal and interest as agreed. Shareholders also bear the risk of poor investment returns and lower valuations.

Even a company that can borrow may see its shares come under pressure. Capital expenditure can keep consuming operating cash flow. Free cash flow may fail to improve.

Compare debt maturities with the timing of customer payments. Expansion funded by existing operations carries a different risk from expansion dependent on the next financing round. If debt matures before customers pay, the funding gap needs to be addressed in advance.

Higher required returns on new financing squeeze project profitability. They do not immediately reset the contractual interest on existing fixed rate debt. These credit differences can help screen companies. They are not enough to explain Friday's entire technology selloff. The impact of a single credit headline cannot be isolated from the index decline.

2 · SETUP

Match conviction with valuation and position size

Stocks tied to the same investment cycle are already delivering very different returns. From the September thirtieth close to the October thirtieth close, Microsoft rose about one point seven six percent. Nvidia fell about ten point three nine percent. Taiwan Semiconductor lost about ten point nine seven percent.

These are price returns excluding dividends. Assess each company's valuation and capital intensity. Check the expectations already embedded in its price. Sector demand alone cannot determine the value of every stock.

If you no longer expect A I to create adequate corporate returns, positioning should reflect that view. Use rebounds to trim related exposure. Add cash or short duration assets with strong credit quality. Continuing to look for reasons to buy the dip would put actions at odds with that judgment.

A bearish view still needs a workable short setup. Sharp rebounds after a selloff can interfere with the trade. The cost of carrying a short position also matters. Investors who remain uncertain can wait. Focus the review on companies with large funding gaps. Weaker credit and frequent refinancing needs also deserve attention. A lower share price may not compensate for funding risk.

If the long term view is unchanged, valuation and position size still come first. Cash generation and stronger credit can provide a better basis for buying in stages.

Before buying, ask how much future profit the current price requires. Then ask how much of that profit can become cash. A return forecast may work only with the most optimistic assumptions for utilization, margins, and financing. Conviction should not be used to justify a larger position in that case.

For existing holdings, revisit the original reason for buying. A multiyear cash flow investment needs a review of order conversion and customer concentration. Free cash flow and funding terms also matter. A short term trend trade needs price discipline when a predetermined level breaks. Both strategies can coexist. Relabeling a losing trend trade as a long term investment abandons the original exit discipline.

Cash also has a purpose. Keeping funds available allows investors to act in stages when valuation improves. A confirmed price recovery can offer another reason to act. Assess the next trade's potential loss against the whole portfolio. Different company names do not guarantee diversification. Several holdings may depend on the same A I spending cycle. They can sell off together when funding tightens.

A sharp decline does not require every decision to be made in one session.

3 · FOCUS

Watch the moving averages

Q Q Q has its next reference around six hundred and seventy eight dollars and forty cents. That is its two hundred day moving average. It uses adjusted daily closing prices through October thirtieth.

The close at six hundred and ninety dollars and forty nine cents remained about one point seven eight percent above that average. The Nasdaq ETF had therefore not broken its two hundred day average. For a short term recovery, watch whether a rebound can reclaim six hundred and ninety six dollars. It then needs to hold that level over the following sessions.

Q Q Q could stabilize near the two hundred day average. If it then recovers six hundred and ninety six dollars, watch whether buying interest follows through. A decisive break below the average would weaken that case. A failed attempt to reclaim it would leave room for a deeper correction.

Six hundred dollars belongs only in an extreme downside scenario. It is not a confirmed target. It is not a sound basis for a trade at this stage. S O X X is weaker. On the same basis, its two hundred day average was about four hundred and seventy six dollars. Its close was roughly zero point nine four percent below that average.

Reclaiming the moving average would be the first step. A sustained recovery above four hundred and eighty six dollars would strengthen the case for a recovery. If rebounds fail at those levels, semiconductors could keep weighing on technology.

These levels are decision points. They do not guarantee that prices will stop there. Intraday breaks and brief recoveries can be misleading during a busy news cycle. Closing levels and follow through over subsequent sessions carry more weight than a single touch.

4 · FOCUS

Watch elections, oil, and funding in November

The United States holds its midterm elections on November third. The results will change expectations for the ability to advance policy. Watch the implications for fiscal, tax, and industrial policy. Then check whether Treasury yields and equities give consistent signals.

The winning party alone cannot settle the direction of the index. Some of the result may already be priced in. Developments in the Middle East and Iran keep oil in focus. Brent remained above one hundred and ten dollars a barrel on Friday. The election result will not automatically remove supply risk.

Escalation could push oil and inflation expectations higher. If long end yields also stay elevated, expensive growth stocks would face greater valuation pressure. Negotiations could reduce the risk premium. Easing rate pressure would improve the backdrop for technology, but prices still need to confirm that improvement.

The third issue is access to funding. Watch A I related bonds, loans, and initial public offerings. Look at required returns and investor protections as well as the amount raised.

A small group of strong borrowers may be able to obtain long term capital. Other companies may face higher costs and shorter maturities. That could widen divergence within the supply chain. Better funding terms and stronger operating cash receipts would provide a firmer basis for adding exposure.

In the next session, first watch whether Q Q Q can reclaim six hundred and ninety six dollars. Also watch whether S O X X can recover its two hundred day average. A bounce that recovers only a small part of the decline would be less convincing. If the close remains below lost support, keep position sizes controlled and wait for confirmation.
