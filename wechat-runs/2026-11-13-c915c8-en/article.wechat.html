Chip Stocks Fell While the Dow Held Up

American stocks finished Friday, November thirteenth, with another split in performance. The Dow gained about one tenth of one percent. The S and P five hundred fell about four tenths of one percent. The Nasdaq Composite fell almost one percent.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost roughly three point four percent. Nvidia continued to fall after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive. A M D, Broadcom and Marvell also came under pressure. The deeper issue is not only who leads Nvidia. Investors are asking who will keep funding the new data centres that purchase its chips.

1 · MACRO

Producer Prices and Interest Rates Are Still a Problem

The government reported that producer prices rose zero point three percent in October. That was slower than September's increase of one point four percent. But annual producer inflation reached six point five percent. The ten year Treasury yield briefly climbed above five point one percent on Friday.

Higher borrowing costs can weaken the returns on data centre projects. Markets are debating more interest rate increases. But the Federal Reserve has not promised three consecutive hikes.

2 · MARKET

Nvidia's Five Hundred Billion Dollar Plan Is Not Five Hundred Billion in Loans

On November twelfth, Nvidia officially confirmed Jensen Huang's resignation. Finance chief Colette Kress became interim chief executive. Nvidia must now decide how its future management team will handle large investments and financing arrangements. In August, Nvidia announced partnerships with six major financial institutions.

They included Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and K K R. The goal was to mobilize more than five hundred billion dollars of outside capital over time. That was a financing target under preliminary agreements. It was not cash already lent to customers.

Recent reporting suggests that some partners want Nvidia to provide stronger financial protection for projects. The main question is whether the first deal becomes a binding agreement, and who takes the risk if the equipment loses value.

3 · MARKET

A Large Commitment Is Not Always a Current Debt

Nvidia disclosed several different categories of future commitments in its July financial filing. Supply and capacity commitments made up two hundred and seventy nine billion dollars. Cloud services, leases, future investments and capital spending added another eighty seven billion dollars.

Together those categories total three hundred and sixty six billion dollars. The filing also lists fifty six billion dollars in separate arrangements and up to one hundred and eight point five billion dollars of guarantees. These figures add up to five hundred and thirty point five billion dollars, but they are not all loans due today.

Some commitments relate to purchasing components over several years. Some guarantees would only require payments if specific customers default and other conditions are met. That distinction matters when estimating the company's real credit exposure.

4 · CAPEX

Nscale Shows Why Financing and Cash Flow Matter

Data centre builder Nscale filed an updated prospectus on November twelfth. During the first half of the year, it generated about one point seven billion dollars of operating cash flow. It also used about three point three billion dollars in investing activities.

This gap does not prove that Nscale will default. It does show how much capital is required to build A I computing infrastructure. A company can sign a large customer contract before it secures every dollar needed for construction.

If funding costs rise or power connections are delayed, deliveries and cash receipts can slip. This is why investors are examining circular financing. The term describes situations where a hardware supplier helps finance a customer that then buys the supplier's equipment.

The structure can work when the final users pay for computing services. But repeated borrowing cannot replace paying customer demand forever.

5 · MARKET

Rivals Still Need to Win Real Orders

A M D and Broadcom initially gained attention as possible alternatives to Nvidia. Both faced selling pressure on Friday. Customer diversification requires more than a leadership change at Nvidia. Buyers need to validate hardware, adapt software and sign new purchase agreements.

Marvell's optical networking business also depends on customer qualifications and real shipments. Taiwan Semiconductor serves several chip designers, but it can still be affected by changes in customer spending. Cisco's earnings offered a useful contrast. It reported eighteen point five billion dollars of quarterly revenue and raised its full year outlook.

So A I infrastructure demand has not simply disappeared. The more important question is which orders are backed by stable cash and financing.

6 · MARKET

Anthropic's IPO Is Another Test

Anthropic has proposed an initial public offering at seventy to seventy eight dollars per share. Its preliminary prospectus covers eight hundred and twenty five million public shares. At the top of that range, the public offering would total about sixty four billion dollars.

A separate twenty six billion dollar private placement is also proposed. The offer price and listing date are not final. The proposed market valuation is not the same thing as the cash Anthropic will receive. Investors should also check how many shares existing holders may sell and when lockup limits expire.

The next important Nvidia event is its earnings report on November seventeenth. For now, watch whether actual A I customer payments, financing contracts and project deliveries support the industry's growth plans. Big announcements are not the same as cash in the bank.
