Last Week Broke the Market's Defensive Levels

For the week ending Friday, November sixth, twenty twenty six, the S and P five hundred lost about two point seven percent. The Nasdaq Composite dropped about three point four percent. The Dow lost nearly one percent. The Russell two thousand slipped just over two percent.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell by roughly twelve percent. Chip stocks were at the centre of the selling. Last week's plan had identified seven thousand five hundred as a defensive zone for the S and P five hundred. On Wednesday, the index rose above seventy six hundred. But Thursday brought a steep reversal.

On November fifth, the S and P fell more than four percent. It closed near seven thousand three hundred and fifty four. Friday's rebound left it below seventy four hundred. The earlier plan correctly flagged the risk of a break. It underestimated how fast prices could fall once that level failed.

Here is the unusual part. The ten year Treasury yield fell by about seven basis points over the week. The two year yield also declined. Brent oil prices fell more than thirteen percent, while the dollar edged higher. But the Vix volatility index rose from about nineteen to nearly twenty six.

Lower yields and cheaper energy did not make technology stocks safer. Investors were worried about something else.

1 · CAPEX

A I Infrastructure Has a Cash Flow Problem to Solve

After the midterm elections, stocks briefly rallied on Wednesday. Investors bought some large cloud companies while selling chip suppliers. Thursday's selloff made the risk much clearer. The Nasdaq lost more than five percent in one day. One concern is the time needed to build new data centres. Texas's newly elected governor has proposed slowing new projects. That proposal is not yet an active statewide ban.

Permits, power connections and local approvals can delay construction. When a project falls behind, equipment deliveries and customer payments may arrive later. But the borrower still has to pay interest. That is a problem for data centre operators relying heavily on debt.

Microsoft, Amazon and Google already have large cloud networks and customers. Existing computing capacity may become more valuable if new projects take longer. Still, those companies are building new facilities too. They are not immune to the same constraints.

Oracle offers another example. On October twenty eighth, it said it would limit additional funding through fiscal twenty thirty to twenty billion dollars. On November second, it priced ten billion dollars of stock and ten billion dollars of bonds. Oracle stayed within its announced funding limit. But it used that allowance almost at once.

Demand for artificial intelligence may be strong. The difficult question is how quickly signed contracts turn into cash. On Friday, the government reported that the United States added one hundred and sixty five thousand jobs in October. Unemployment was four point one percent.

That was stronger than many analysts expected. It kept the possibility of another Federal Reserve rate increase in the discussion. Bond yields can fall because investors are seeking safety. That is very different from an economy with lower inflation and steadily growing profits.

2 · MACRO

Inflation and Earnings Are This Week's Main Events

The main review period is November second through November eighth. Now we look at November ninth through November thirteenth. On Monday, some consumer and energy companies are scheduled to report earnings. On Tuesday morning, November tenth, the Bureau of Labor Statistics will release October consumer price inflation.

The number has not been published yet. Investors will focus on services, housing and the effect of lower energy prices. On Tuesday evening, CoreWeave reports quarterly results. Its earnings call is scheduled for five o'clock Eastern time. Investors should look beyond revenue growth. Capital spending, operating cash flow and borrowing costs may matter more.

On Thursday evening, November twelfth, Cisco and Applied Materials both report. Cisco can help investors understand demand for networking equipment. Applied Materials can show whether semiconductor manufacturers are delaying equipment orders. On Friday morning, November thirteenth, the government releases October producer price inflation.

Lower inflation would help. But the technology market also needs signs that companies can collect cash and finance their expansion.

3 · MARKET

The First Goal Is to Recover Seven Thousand Five Hundred

The S and P five hundred ended last Friday near seven thousand three hundred and seventy seven. First, the index needs to stop making new lows around seven thousand three hundred and fifty. Then it must climb back above seven thousand five hundred. Recovering seven thousand five hundred and eighty, with stronger participation from chip stocks, would be more convincing.

Another break below Thursday's low would argue for tighter risk control. Do not judge the entire market by one strong day in Microsoft or Amazon. Watch whether more stocks advance, whether semiconductor shares stabilize and whether the Vix begins to fall.

The semiconductor fund S O X X also completed a three for one stock split. Prices from before and after the split need to be adjusted when comparing charts. For a portfolio already concentrated in technology, calculate how much cash might be needed if short puts are assigned.

Protective puts and collars can limit some downside. They also cost money or restrict upside. The question for this week is simple. Will lower inflation arrive at the same time as stronger cash flow from A I infrastructure? Lower Treasury yields alone have not been enough. Investors now need proof that the investment cycle can pay for itself.
