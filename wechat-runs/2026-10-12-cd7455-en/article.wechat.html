0 · MARKET

Bonds were shut. The style did not change.

Monday was Columbus Day. The bond market was closed. Stocks stayed open.

The Dow closed up zero point three eight percent. The S&P 500 closed almost flat, down zero point zero six percent. The Nasdaq composite closed down zero point four four percent.

Volume was light because bonds and stocks were not trading together.

The Philadelphia semiconductor index was the weak spot. Twenty-eight members fell. Only two rose.

Inside the S&P 500, three hundred and twenty-one stocks rose. One hundred and seventy-nine fell.

Eight sectors rose. Three fell. Two of the weak ones were the tech-heavy groups.

Last week's style is still here. Non-tech is doing the better work. Tech hardware is paying it back.

A flat index is not a flip in risk appetite.

1 · EARNINGS

What this earnings week checks

Wednesday is the September consumer price index. Thursday is producer prices.

Those are the last inflation prints before the Federal Reserve meeting on October twenty-eighth.

Thursday also has retail sales. Friday has industrial production.

Earnings open with the large banks, then ASML and TSMC.

The banks speak to credit. ASML and TSMC speak to whether A.I. spending is still rising.

Do not redraw the chip map off one light-volume day before those reports.

2 · SEMIS

Memory is a margin question

The memory basket fell more than four percent, then dropped again after the close.

ChangXin said its next memory-cell design had made progress. Samsung and S.K. Hynix are working on the same kind of design.

Samsung's sub-ten-nanometer target sits around twenty twenty-eight.

ChangXin is about nine and a half to ten percent of global DRAM revenue, fourth in the world.

This industry prices off capacity. One more competitor hits gross margin first.

The idea that margins peak in twenty twenty-eight can get pulled forward.

Micron never got far after clearing one thousand and ninety-six. It fell back into the zone from eight hundred and fifty-eight to nine hundred and ninety-two.

Monday's close was nine hundred and eighty-nine dollars, down three point eight eight percent, already inside that zone.

If big buyers defend the zone, eight hundred and fifty-eight is still a floor. If they do not, it is an exit.

SanDisk has to defend one thousand, three hundred and fifty-four. Monday's close was one thousand, four hundred and eighty-seven dollars, down five point nine seven percent.

Flash and DRAM are not the same line. They were sold in one basket.

The forward multiple looks cheap because next year's earnings still assume gross margins above seventy percent.

A normal year for this industry is closer to fifty-five percent. That gap rewrites the earnings. There is no target multiple here. Follow the levels.

3 · MARKET

Meta lifted the price with a new product

Meta closed at seven hundred and forty-three dollars, up three point three eight percent, on heavier volume.

The broad market was quiet. Meta was not.

The product is Muse Wonder. It was framed as a step toward personal superintelligence.

Not everyone can use it. Inside Muse it costs twenty dollars a month, or one hundred.

The zone is six hundred and ninety to seven hundred and thirty. After seven hundred and thirty broke, the view was new highs.

Last week's pullback was a repair. If the name is still a long, that pullback was the add.

Monday does not change that view. Do not fill the position at the top of this candle.

4 · MARKET

Microsoft is grinding out records

Microsoft closed at five hundred and forty-one dollars, up one point one four percent.

Friday was already a record close. Monday extended it.

Weekly and daily charts are close to overbought, and not there yet.

This grind does not last. Five hundred and forty, five hundred and fifty, and five hundred and sixty can be walked. Then comes a volume pullback.

After the breaks of five hundred and fourteen and five hundred and thirty-four, a small add is enough.

Do not put full size on the launch. Wait for the pullback that packs the shares.

5 · MARKET

Hedge funds sold hardware and bought industrials

Hedge-fund exposure was still low last week.

At the single-stock level it was a third straight week of net selling, mostly from new shorts.

Short positions are nearly twice the long positions.

Information technology was the most sold sector. Hardware and semiconductors were sold the hardest.

Software was bought the most. That is the I-G-V group.

Net long exposure to the Big Seven is at a record. The seven are Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon, Tesla, and Nvidia. Broadcom is not in that set.

A record net long is crowding, not safety. Software was bought. Semiconductors were sold. Monday showed that split.

Industrials took the largest buy. After five weeks of selling, hedge funds bought them at the fastest pace since early January, about one point eight standard deviations.

Almost all of it was new longs. Aerospace and defense, construction and engineering, airlines, and industrial groups led the buy.

6 · MARKET

Where industrials and staples sit

The industrial E.T.F., X-L-I, has one strong ceiling left, from one hundred and seventy-two to one hundred and seventy-six.

Spot is the aggressive entry. The next band is one hundred and sixty-two to one hundred and sixty-five.

If that band fails, do not catch the knife. Wait for under one hundred and fifty-six.

The range has been sideways since September.

Staples are pushing toward eighty-four point eight. A break would mean the recent low near eighty may not be revisited. Lows can lift toward eighty-two or eighty-three.

Nvidia is back at its first medium support. There is nothing new to add.

A.M.D. still has five hundred and forty to five hundred and sixty-two well overhead. This stretch is profit-taking after a large run.

ASML and TSMC wait for this week's reports.

Bonds are back tomorrow. Volume can reconnect.

Monday's light tape stays with Columbus Day. The style did not flip.

Tech hardware is being sold. Industrials and software are being bought.

Memory is about eight hundred and fifty-eight to nine hundred and ninety-two.

Industrials are about one hundred and seventy-two to one hundred and seventy-six.

Staples are about eighty-four point eight.

Those three matter more than the S&P 500 sitting on seven thousand, eight hundred.
