Buyers Return Selectively Before a Major Listing

On November eighteenth, the Dow fell one point one one percent. The S&P 500 slipped zero point two three percent, while the Nasdaq rose zero point four two percent and the Russell two thousand index gained zero point four six percent. The exchange traded fund tracking the S&P 500 touched seven hundred twenty six dollars and seventy nine and a half cents before closing at seven hundred thirty dollars and twenty cents. Buyers returned from the low, but the indexes told different stories.

A recovery from the low can come from short covering as well as fresh buying. Trend following funds may add equity exposure if markets remain flat or rise. The amount depends on future prices and the funds' models. Those scenarios do not tell us the probability of a rally.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller suggested in a television interview that the central bank could raise rates by half a percentage point in December or communicate a faster pace of tightening in twenty twenty seven. Futures still mostly reflected a quarter point increase.

A company buyback authorization also gives management permission to purchase shares. It does not mean those purchases have already happened.

Anthropic Brings Its Growth Story to Public Investors

Anthropic priced its offering at eighty three dollars a share. The base offering covers nine hundred eighty million shares, worth about eighty one point three four billion dollars. About nine hundred fifty six point three five million are new shares, while existing shareholders are selling twenty three point six five million. The company receives proceeds from the new shares, and the selling shareholders receive the proceeds from theirs.

The indicated valuation is one point five trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis. That is separate from the value of the shares being offered. Underwriters have an option to buy up to another hundred forty seven million shares, but those should only be counted if the option is exercised. The offering still has to close. It also arranged a separate twenty six billion dollar private placement of nonvoting shares.

Anthropic reported thirty three point two four two billion dollars in revenue for the first nine months of twenty twenty six. At the end of October, it reported an annualized revenue run rate of eighty one billion dollars. A recent annualized pace is different from revenue earned over a full calendar year, and it is not a promise of future sales.

Investors will have to weigh revenue growth against the cost of computing capacity. A strong debut would show demand for the deal. Follow through in the new shares and broader market participation are separate tests, and an early jump could reverse.

Nvidia Falls After Strong Results

Nvidia closed at one hundred fifty dollars and ninety six cents, down two point eight nine percent. Earlier it traded as high as one hundred fifty nine dollars and seventy cents. The reversal came after the company reported fiscal third quarter revenue of one hundred fifteen point eight billion dollars, including one hundred seven point six billion from its data center business.

Nvidia expects about one hundred thirty billion dollars in revenue for the following quarter, plus or minus three percent. That quarter includes an additional week, and the forecast assumes no data center compute revenue from China. The results show strong demand, while the reversal leaves questions about expectations, future margins and valuation. The day's move cannot tell us what drove every sale.

One market commentator said he reduced half of his own Nvidia position after it fell below one hundred seventy four dollars on November ninth, then exited the remainder near one hundred fifty eight dollars on November eighteenth. That is his account of his own trade, not a verified execution record.

The area from one hundred thirty two to one hundred fifty dollars is the next range to watch. Above that, one hundred seventy four and one hundred ninety four dollars are reference levels where a rebound could meet supply. Those levels frame scenarios; they do not predict the next move.

Palo Alto Reports After the Closing Bell

Palo Alto closed the regular session at three hundred thirty nine dollars and ninety two cents, up nine point nine percent. The company published its earnings after the close, so the regular session gain came before the report. The later after hours response is a separate move.

Quarterly revenue reached three point four four billion dollars, up thirty nine percent from a year earlier. Adjusted earnings were one dollar and five cents per share. Its next generation security annual recurring revenue rose sixty six percent to nine point seven three billion dollars.

Management raised its full year revenue forecast to between fourteen point five two and fourteen point five seven billion dollars. The company also agreed to buy Applied Compute in an all stock deal valued at four point five billion dollars, subject to adjustments and closing conditions. The acquisition has not closed; its returns depend on integration and execution.

Three hundred forty eight dollars is the first price level to watch, followed by the range from three hundred seventy three to three hundred eighty six dollars. After hours trading can be thin. The clearer test will come when investors have had time to assess both the results and the acquisition.

Disney and Netflix Face Different Tests

Disney closed at one hundred twenty two dollars and thirty six cents, up four point six three percent. On November seventeenth, it reported fiscal fourth quarter adjusted earnings of one dollar and seventy seven cents per share. For the next fiscal year, management expects approximately eleven billion dollars in free cash flow and double digit adjusted earnings growth, excluding the impact of an extra week in the prior fiscal year.

That extra week helped the earlier results and makes comparisons less straightforward. Disney also has to support its cash forecast while investing in content and parks. The price levels to watch are one hundred eight, one hundred sixteen, and one hundred forty two dollars. They are reference points, not guaranteed floors or targets.

Netflix closed at fifty nine dollars and eighty two cents, up zero point two three percent. The first level to watch is sixty dollars and fifty cents, followed by sixty four dollars. A brief move above a level is different from holding it as trading continues.

In an October twentieth shareholder letter, Netflix reduced its estimate for twenty twenty six advertising revenue to about two point seven billion dollars from roughly three billion. It cited a slower rollout of some advertising capabilities. That was an earlier disclosure, not a new announcement on November eighteenth.

Disney combines streaming with parks and sports, while Netflix depends more directly on subscriptions and advertising. Both must turn audience engagement into durable cash generation.
