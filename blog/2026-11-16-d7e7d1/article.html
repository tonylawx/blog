0 · MARKET

The Chip Rout Is Worse Than the Broader Market

U.S. stocks sold off on November 16, with AI-linked names taking the heaviest hit. The S&P 500 lost 1.92% to close at 7,416.59. The Nasdaq fell 2.49%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped about 5.3%. Within the S&P 500, 353 constituents declined and 149 advanced. Only consumer staples and utilities closed higher among its eleven sectors.

Apple and some European benchmarks have held up better than the semiconductor complex. That does not mean they have no exposure to AI. It does suggest investors are separating companies generating cash today from companies depending on another round of infrastructure spending tomorrow.

A sharp correction is not automatically an overreaction. The key test is whether analysts begin cutting their 2027 earnings forecasts across the index. If the earnings base holds, falling prices may reflect deleveraging and a higher risk premium. If expectations fall with prices, the valuation reset may not be finished.

1 · POLICY

U.S. Export Controls Now Reach Remote Computing

The Commerce Department's November 16 rule expands oversight of advanced chips and access to computing power through overseas data centres. The 12 million aggregate TPP threshold is not a blanket rule covering every large facility worldwide. It applies to specified end uses involving computing access for companies based in China and other restricted jurisdictions.

Advanced chip shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil face additional licensing requirements. The rule also includes conditions under which U.S.-headquartered companies qualify for exceptions and reporting requirements.

For Nvidia, a licensing requirement does not instantly cancel every overseas order. Watch the pace of approvals, customer verification, server deliveries, and eventual revenue recognition. Those are the points at which regulatory changes show up in financial statements.

2 · EARNINGS

Nvidia Earnings May Test the Circular-Financing Model

Nvidia closed at $156.14 on November 16, down 4.12%. Its fiscal third-quarter results are scheduled for approximately 4:20 p.m. Eastern on November 17, followed by a 5 p.m. earnings call. Interim CEO Colette Kress, previously the chief financial officer, now faces investors following Jensen Huang's departure.

Beyond revenue and margins, investors are watching financial support for AI infrastructure customers. The circular-financing concern is straightforward: an equipment vendor helps fund a customer that then purchases the vendor's equipment. If Nvidia scales back investments, guarantees, or other financing support, its own credit exposure could improve. But capital-constrained cloud partners may then find it harder to finance new GPU orders.

That is especially relevant to newer, capital-intensive cloud providers such as CoreWeave. Lower credit risk for Nvidia does not guarantee higher future earnings if some customers reduce purchases. Just as important, a possible pullback is still a scenario, not an announced decision. On the call, listen for updated funding commitments, data-centre guidance, payment terms, and delivery schedules.

An options-derived reference of roughly $155 plus or minus $15 implies a working event range around $140–$170. It is not a guaranteed trading band or a precise probability forecast. A heavily oversold weekly chart could produce a rebound toward $170–$180 without resolving the governance or customer-financing issues.

3 · TSMC

Why TSMC Is Different From Memory Chips

TSMC's U.S.-listed shares closed at $373.45 on November 16, down approximately 6.34%. The bullish case rests on the economics of advanced foundry capacity, not immunity to an AI spending downturn.

Memory chips face a more volatile commodity cycle, with multiple competitors and rapidly changing prices. Leading-edge logic manufacturing involves long development cycles, yield improvements, equipment availability, and close customer integration. Capacity cannot be added overnight. TSMC's 2-nanometre ramp supports the competitive case, but claims that 2027 high-end capacity is more than fully booked still require direct confirmation.

One commentator's stress test assumes AI investment disappears, non-AI demand still supports 6%–7% growth, and the stock trades at 15.5 times earnings. That produces a rough value near $300. The assumptions matter more than the headline number. If growth, margins or earnings deteriorate further, $300 is no hard floor.

The cited staged entry zones of $360–$378 and $320–$350 are reference levels, not automatic buy signals. The better checks are utilization, monthly revenue, gross margin, and capital spending.

4 · SETUP

The S&P 500 Options Range Is Not a Forecast

With the S&P 500 at 7,416.59, a roughly 7,200–7,900 year-end options reference range leaves about 2.9% to the downside and 6.5% to the upside. That asymmetry in distance does not mean an upward move is twice as likely. Options prices reflect skew and tail risk as well as the distribution of expected returns.

SPY at roughly $728–$753 can be treated as an initial watch zone, with an S&P 500 level near 7,000 representing a deeper stress case. Either zone can fail if the next earnings revisions are large enough.

Three checks matter now: what Nvidia says about partner financing and guidance, how the export rules affect actual shipments, and whether 2027 index earnings estimates hold up. A technical bounce may come first. Durable upside needs a credible earnings path behind it.
