0 · MOMENTUM

A Broad Rebound Left One AI Giant Behind

US stocks rallied hard on Monday, November 9. The S&P 500 climbed 2.35% to 7,549.94, the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74%, the Dow added 2.15% and the Russell 2000 rose roughly 3.15%. The Nasdaq 100 advanced about 3.40% to 27,907.63. Brent crude fell nearly 6%, taking some pressure off the inflation narrative, while the VIX closed near 19.92, down from about 25.64 on Friday.

The outlier was NVDA. While AMD gained 13.26% and AVGO advanced about 8.78%, Nvidia dropped 4.27% to $178.67. Roughly $190 billion of Nvidia's market capitalization vanished in a session when most technology stocks rebounded.

The rebound did not erase the previous week's damage. The S&P 500 closed November 6 at 7,376.86. Monday's rally reclaimed 7,500 but left the benchmark below its November 4 close of 7,688.62. A broad advance can reflect relief after a sharp drawdown, short covering and some fresh buying. The exact contribution of each flow cannot be inferred from one closing print. Investors should avoid declaring the correction over simply because one session was strong.

1 · SOFTWARE

Nvidia's Leadership Investigation Is Now an Equity Risk

Nvidia's chief executive faces more than a personal reputational issue. On November 8, the company published a message in which Jensen Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and apologized. Nvidia's board separately announced an independent investigation into reports that Huang may have been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Independent directors and external counsel are overseeing that inquiry.

Two facts need to stay separate. Huang's admission is public. Allegations about another person's intelligence connections, chip diversions or compromised trade secrets have not been established by the inquiry. The board said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia's secrets had been compromised and expressed confidence in Huang and his management team. On November 9, shortly after the close, shareholders filed a proposed class action alleging inadequate disclosure. The existence of that lawsuit is verifiable; the allegations are not judicial findings. Nor could a complaint filed after the close have caused the entire regular-session decline.

For equity investors, the relevant transmission channels are governance, regulation and key-person risk. A formal inquiry can introduce an uncertainty discount around a founder-CEO whose relationships and execution are central to the company's investment case. If evidence emerges of export-control violations or compromised confidential information, the possible effect extends to legal costs, customer contracts and future sales. If no company-related wrongdoing is identified and leadership continuity holds, the market may gradually remove some of that discount.

Monday's trading illustrates why a technical plan must specify what constitutes a break. Nvidia opened around $173 and traded down to $170.24, not merely $173 as some summaries suggested. Yet it recovered to close at $178.67. An intraday stop below $174 would have triggered; a rule requiring a close below $174 would not. Neither discipline is inherently superior, but switching between them after the fact is not a strategy.

Watch the November 9 low near $170.24, then the $174–$194 recovery band. A sustained breakout beyond $194 would be stronger evidence of stabilization. A renewed breakdown below $170 could put $150–$160 back on a risk watchlist, though neither that range nor any other technical level guarantees a destination. For a concentrated position, map exposure against three possibilities: an inquiry finding no substantive problems, an extended governance overhang, and new evidence of material legal or regulatory risk.

Nvidia has officially scheduled its fiscal 2027 third-quarter earnings discussion for November 17, after results are released. That event will bring reported demand, margins, capacity and governance questions together. A strong earnings release could offset some uncertainty, but it cannot by itself close an independent legal investigation.

2 · SEMIS

AMD Has Its Own Catalysts, and TSMC Still Needs Confirmation

AMD's 13.26% jump to $508.58 should not be explained entirely as investors abandoning Nvidia. AMD announced on November 9 that its fifth-generation EPYC CPUs will power the host infrastructure for Google Cloud TPU 8tx. Oppenheimer also upgraded the stock to Outperform with a $600 target. The EPYC processors run the host layer alongside Google's TPUs. They are not replacing the TPU accelerators, and the companies did not disclose a contract value that investors can simply capitalize.

The balance sheet and cash-conversion debate remains relevant. AMD generated $554 million of free cash flow in the third quarter, compared with $1.558 billion in the second. Inventory and receivables also expanded. If the company delivers its approximately $17 billion fourth-quarter sales guidance, investors will still want to see whether new deliveries translate into stronger cash collection. The $501–$528 area can be treated as a price-confirmation band rather than proof of a durable trend. A pullback below $501 would weaken an immediate breakout case.

TSM approached $390 during the rebound. Its leading-edge manufacturing capacity and customer relationships provide important advantages, but they do not make the stock immune to repricing in the broader semiconductor complex. Look for sustained price and volume confirmation above the nearby trading area, with $424 as a higher reference. The $360–$378 area is a possible location to reassess a secondary pullback, not a mechanical buy signal.

3 · FOCUS

CTA Thresholds Are Scenarios, Not Pre-Programmed Orders

The S&P 500's Monday close of 7,549.94 sat almost exactly at the widely discussed 7,549 near-term threshold. Some systematic-strategy scenarios identify 7,676 as a potential upper buying trigger and 7,084 as a more severe downside risk level. Numbers such as $74 billion of possible buying or $149.5 billion of selling have also circulated.

These should not be mistaken for verified orders sitting on an exchange. CTA estimates change with volatility, trend lookback, fund positioning and the time horizon. Without the original model and its timestamp, exact flow totals are not independently reproducible. They can inform a scenario map, but they are not promises of what funds must transact.

A rally powered partly by covering short positions may look identical to genuine new long demand for several hours. The more valuable confirmation will come from the days ahead. Does the S&P hold around 7,549 and progress toward 7,676? Does the advance remain broad? Can QQQ clear the upper end of its shorter-term trading range near $687 and eventually challenge the higher area around $728? If market breadth fades and the VIX climbs back toward 25, chasing becomes harder to justify.

SOXX has also undergone a three-for-one forward share split. Its post-November 5 levels must be compared on a split-adjusted basis. A support discussion around $128–$134 and potential resistance around $160–$178 is meaningless if the chart mixes old and new per-share prices.

4 · LIQUIDITY

CoreWeave, Anthropic and the Next Cash-Flow Test

CRWV is scheduled to discuss third-quarter results at 5 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10. The most important questions concern capital expenditures, debt costs, project delivery and the conversion of contracted AI demand into operating cash flow. Leverage makes any delay in bringing new computing capacity online more expensive.

Options markets may price a large two-sided post-earnings move, but a specific plus-or-minus 20% estimate requires an identified expiration and actual option quotes. Without those, it should not be presented as an established implied move. Stronger cash generation and stable financing would support an AI infrastructure recovery; larger cash burn and more expensive debt would challenge it.

Anthropic is also preparing for a potential IPO. The company announced an S-1 filing on October 26, and reporting has suggested investor marketing may take place during the week of November 9. But on November 10 Reuters reported that preliminary price details circulated to bankers were later withdrawn as inaccurate. There is no verified final offering price or guaranteed listing date in that report. Watch the amended prospectus and the actual order book instead of treating early valuation rumours as completed transactions.

October CPI is also due November 10. Nvidia's November 17 earnings are the next major single-stock catalyst. A durable recovery would ideally pair more stable inflation expectations, credible cash flow from leveraged infrastructure companies, improved semiconductor breadth and clarity around Nvidia's investigation. If that combination fails to emerge, one spectacular rally may be just a pause in a wider repricing. For accounts holding concentrated technology stocks or short puts, first calculate assignment cash needs, one-month drawdown tolerance and the price of protective puts or collars. Markets can move on from a personal scandal quickly. Regulatory risk and missing cash flow are harder to dismiss.
