Inflation Above Forecast Keeps Funding Pressure On

October CPI came in above expectations in the Bureau of Labor Statistics release on November 10. Headline prices rose 0.4% month over month, against 0.3% in a Reuters poll of economists. Core prices increased 0.3%, versus 0.2% in the same survey. Shelter rose 0.3% and contributed more than 30% of the overall increase, while food and energy gained 0.5% and 0.7%. Price pressure remains sticky. The report gives investors little reason to assume monetary policy is about to ease.

Actual readings are from the November 10 BLS release; forecasts are from the Reuters economist poll. All figures are seasonally adjusted monthly changes, not annual inflation rates or forecasts of future inflation.

Fed Governor Waller said core monthly inflation around 0.3% or higher would warrant considering 50 basis points of tightening. That is his conditional position, not an FOMC decision. Long-end yields are testing valuations too: the 10-year Treasury yield moved above 5% intraday, while the Treasury's daily reference reading around 3:30 p.m. was 4.98%, up 10 basis points from 4.88% the previous day. For AI infrastructure built before customers pay, higher rates affect both new borrowing costs and the present value of future revenue.

The equity market did not show broad panic. The S&P 500 closed at 7,520.93, down 0.38%, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 0.60%. Regular-session volume declined from the previous day, while the VIX eased to 19.46. Index weakness and funding stress at individual highly leveraged companies need separate judgments. Borrowing terms and the timing of customer receipts will say more about the next stage of risk than one day's price move.

SoftBank's $3.73 Billion Loss Spans Different Cash Dates

SoftBank reported a net loss attributable to shareholders of about ¥1.12 trillion for July through September on November 10. Its financial statements for the six months ended September 30 also recognized a $3.73 billion equipment procurement loss. That comprised $2.85 billion on previously paid, nonrefundable advances and $880 million in contract modification and settlement costs. Reserving equipment capacity carries a cost when requirements and construction plans change. The loss recognition does not mean another $3.73 billion of cash left during one quarter.

These are components of recognized losses for the six months ended September 30, totaling $3.73 billion. The $2.85 billion relates to earlier payments; cash payments for the $880 million item are expected from October 2026 onward. The total is not one quarter's cash spending.

SoftBank's accounting presentation says cash payments associated with the $880 million are expected from October through December and beyond. Future purchases could earn conditional rebates, but amounts and timing remain uncertain, and no related asset was recognized at September 30. Paid deposits, recognized losses, and future payments are different stages of the same commitments. Adding them together would double-count costs, while deducting unrealized rebates would recognize a recovery too early. The next checks are whether procurement changes end and project specifications, payments, and delivery stabilize.

Companies pay early or reserve manufacturing slots to secure equipment for AI expansion. If power plans, equipment requirements, or project schedules change later, those commitments may leave less flexibility than expected. Cancellation terms, refundable amounts, and modification costs therefore matter alongside order and project sizes. The largest signed commitment does not establish the lowest eventual construction cost.

GEV Has Not Confirmed an Order Loss

The SoftBank news brought renewed scrutiny to power equipment suppliers. That market reaction needs to be separated from confirmed operating effects. Reuters reported on November 10 that a GEV spokesperson said SoftBank's recent announcement had no positive or negative effect on GE Vernova. SoftBank also denied a default and said there had been no discussion of reducing the Ohio project. The evidence does not establish a confirmed order cancellation at GEV or a sudden disappearance of power equipment demand.

GE Vernova sells power, electrification, and wind equipment and services. Customer advances, manufacturing, and delivery schedules affect its cash collection; it should not be valued as though it were a regulated utility with stable returns. Reserved manufacturing slots, firm orders, and revenue from delivered products also need separate checks. For GEV, the next evidence is whether order and delivery guidance changes, advances convert into revenue, and contract changes enter formal disclosures. Long-term returns depend on orders, delivery, profit, and working capital together.

CoreWeave's Backlog Still Requires Near-Term Funding

In its November 10 third-quarter results, CRWV reported $132.5 billion in revenue backlog at September 30. Revenue recognition depends on delivering capacity and making services available. Backlog is neither balance-sheet cash nor an amount that can be netted directly against debt. On a consistent basis, debt carrying amounts rose from $35.068 billion at June 30 to $46.873 billion at September 30, an increase of $11.805 billion in one quarter. That change in the debt balance is not an equivalent net financing cash inflow.

Both periods sum current and noncurrent recourse and nonrecourse debt carrying amounts, excluding separately reported finance and operating lease liabilities. The balance increase is not net borrowing cash inflow and does not represent all future payment obligations.

Liquidity assessment also requires a payment timeline. Current debt was about $10.572 billion at September 30, against cash and equivalents of about $4.122 billion. Including both categories of restricted cash brings the cash total to roughly $6.404 billion. Restricted funds cannot be assumed available to repay any debt. Equally, subtracting one day's cash balance from obligations due over the next 12 months does not establish an immediate shortage. Operating receipts, new financing, advances, and construction spending will change the position; their timing matters.

CoreWeave expects fourth-quarter interest expense of $1.05 billion to $1.15 billion. That is expense guidance, not cash interest already paid. Multiplying the midpoint by four gives an annualized pace, not a full-year bill already incurred. Construction and interest require funding before backlog is recognized as revenue. The useful checks are new borrowing costs, collateral and draw conditions, and whether delivery delays also postpone revenue. Large orders alone cannot prove safety, just as rising debt alone cannot predict bankruptcy.

Management offered a potential buffer: it expects about $9 billion in customer advances and deposits over the next three months and plans financing such as loans matched to customer contracts. The company release, Reuters, and MarketWatch reported the expectation. It cannot be added to September's cash balance in advance. Watch whether receipts arrive on schedule, financing closes on affordable terms, and delivery progresses. Meeting those conditions would ease near-term funding concerns. Delayed receipts, more expensive borrowing, and slower delivery would compound the pressure.

Anthropic Starts Its IPO Roadshow and an Office Push

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on November 10 and filed an amended prospectus, proposing to trade on Nasdaq under ANT. Reuters reported an expected trading start on November 19, but final pricing and the offering remain pending. A proposed listing is not a completed listing. Its new office products also have different release status: Sheets and Whiteboards remain in beta, while the former Dashboards became Claude Data and is generally available. The suite cannot all be treated as mature software.

Sheets addresses some spreadsheet and collaboration tasks. Its official help page still lists limits involving macros, VBA, Power Query, and other functions. That creates another route into office workflows without establishing damage to Microsoft 365 revenue. MSFT and Anthropic also have investment, cloud, and model-distribution relationships. Application rivalry can coexist with infrastructure cooperation. Microsoft's intraday share-price move does not establish a loss of paid office seats.

The next tests are retention of paying business users, compatibility and migration costs for complex workflows, and measurable changes to Microsoft's seats, renewals, and monetization. An IPO can broaden financing channels; office product delivery and commercialization still need to be established separately. Shares can price competitive expectations quickly. Revenue effects need time and financial results, rather than a conclusion drawn from one launch event.

BofA's Top-Pick Removal Leaves Buy Ratings Intact

Bank of America removed NVDA, MU, and LRCX from its US1 investment list on November 10 while retaining Buy ratings. US1 is a narrower selection within the bank's Buy-rated coverage. Removal from that group and a downgrade to Sell are different decisions. Without a publicly stated explanation from the bank, outside views on valuation, governance, or capital spending remain outside interpretations. They cannot be presented as BofA's stated reasons or as evidence that the investment case for the entire semiconductor sector has failed.

With the S&P 500 at 7,520.93, the first nearby reference is the session low around 7,470, followed by the previous session's low near 7,422. Losing those areas as selling broadens and volume rises would weaken the recovery case. Stabilizing prices and yields that stop climbing would allow it to be tested further. The 7,000 to 7,100 range belongs to a deeper-pullback scenario, well below the current close. It is neither support being tested now nor a guaranteed bottom.

Demand, contracts, delivery, and cash require separate checks. Strong demand does not make financing costs irrelevant, and large contracts do not prove all spending is funded. Procurement losses and top-pick list changes also need to be judged on their own terms. Tracking yields, actual receipts, loan terms, and delivery will help distinguish market moves that amplify concern from an increase in fundamental risk.
