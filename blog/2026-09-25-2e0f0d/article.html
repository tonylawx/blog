2 · FOCUS

Durables were flat. Core capital goods were the hard print

August durable-goods orders were basically unchanged, better than the expected decline. Transportation fell 0.6% and did the damage. Ex-transport orders rose 0.3%, short of the 0.6% expected. Nondefense capital goods excluding aircraft rose 1.6% versus 0.5% expected. July was revised to about 0.6%. That line is up about 14% year over year. It is the cleaner proxy for business equipment spending. Computers and equipment are still adding. The semiconductor bid did not shut itself off in the order book. Strong orders do not let the index ignore a 5.2% discount rate. They only say the AI capex leg has not broken in the data.

Michigan's September final sentiment printed 48.1, above the 47.6 forecast and the 47.8 prelim, but still below August's 51.7. One-year inflation expectations are 4.6%. Five-year are 3.4%. The one-year rate has already come up from 4.0% in August. Inflation expectations are not rolling over. Jobs are steady, equipment demand is still there, and inflation expectations are not coming down. The discount rate will not ease itself.

It is not a calendar. Nobody can date a move to 600 by year-end. Long-term holders stay. Anyone who sold and waited for a dip already has a right-side break. A pullback probably still lives in 490 to 514.
