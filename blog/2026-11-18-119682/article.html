A Mixed Close Ahead of a Major Listing

The Dow fell 1.11% on November 18, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.23%, the Nasdaq rose 0.42%, and the Russell 2000 gained 0.46%. SPY, the ETF tracking the S&P 500, traded as low as $726.795 before closing at $730.20. The rebound off the low helped, but the split between the indexes showed buyers were still selective.

The next test is whether demand broadens. An intraday recovery can reflect short covering as well as fresh buying.

Systematic trend-following funds, often called CTAs, may add equity exposure in flat or rising market scenarios. The scale depends on subsequent prices and the models those funds use. A potential source of demand is not a guarantee of a rally, and no probability can be assigned from those scenarios alone.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller suggested in a CNBC interview that the Fed could raise rates by 50 basis points in December or communicate faster tightening in 2027. Futures still mostly priced a 25-basis-point increase.

A buyback authorization gives management permission to repurchase shares; it does not mean those purchases have already occurred.

Anthropic's Offering Puts AI Demand to a Public Test

Anthropic priced its offering at $83 per share, with 980 million shares in the base offering. That puts the offering value at about $81.34 billion. The total comprises 956.35 million newly issued shares and 23.65 million shares sold by existing shareholders. The distinction matters because the company receives proceeds from the new shares, while the sellers receive proceeds from the secondary sale.

The indicated $1.5 trillion valuation is on a fully diluted basis. That is a different measure from the value of the shares in the offering. The underwriters also have an option to purchase up to 147 million additional shares; those shares should not be counted as sold unless the option is exercised. The offering still has to close, and a priced deal does not establish where the shares will trade once the market opens. Anthropic also arranged a separate $26 billion private placement of nonvoting shares.

Anthropic reported revenue of $33.242 billion for the first nine months of 2026. It also reported an annualized revenue run rate of $81 billion at the end of October. Those numbers describe different periods and calculations. The run rate is a snapshot of a recent pace, not reported revenue earned over a full calendar year or a promise of future sales.

Investors will have to weigh revenue growth against the cost of computing capacity. An ambitious valuation does not establish future profitability.

A strong debut would show demand for the deal. Follow-through in the new shares and broader market participation are separate tests; an early jump could reverse.

Nvidia's Results Did Not Stop the Selloff

NVDA closed at $150.96, down 2.89%, after trading as high as $159.70. The reversal came after Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion and Data Center revenue of $107.6 billion. For the following quarter, management guided to revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%. The forecast includes an additional week and assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China.

The results show strong demand, while the reversal leaves questions about expectations, future margins and valuation. The day's move alone cannot establish what drove every sale.

A market commentator described reducing half of his Nvidia position after the stock fell below $174 on November 9, then exiting the remainder near $158 on November 18. This is his account of his own trade. It is an illustration of managing exposure around a broken level, not a verified execution record or a statement about anyone else's holdings.

The $132 to $150 area is the next range to watch. A durable recovery would require buyers to establish support rather than merely touch a price. On the upside, $174 and then $194 are reference levels where a rebound could meet supply. Those levels frame scenarios; they do not predict the next move.

Palo Alto's Earnings Arrived After the Closing Bell

PANW closed the regular session at $339.92, up 9.90%. The company released its fiscal first-quarter results after the close, so the regular-session gain came before the report. The subsequent after-hours response should be kept separate from the day's closing return.

Palo Alto reported revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39% year over year, and adjusted earnings of $1.05 per share. Its next-generation security annual recurring revenue rose 66% to $9.73 billion. Management raised its fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion. The company also announced an agreement to acquire Applied Compute in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments and closing conditions.

The acquisition extends Palo Alto's ambitions in enterprise AI. The deal has not closed, and future returns will depend on integration and execution.

For price action, $348 is the first level to watch, followed by $373 to $386. After-hours trading can be thin, and an early reaction can change when the wider market opens. The clearer test will come when investors have had time to assess both the results and the acquisition.

Disney's Cash Outlook and Netflix's Growth Challenge

DIS closed at $122.36, up 4.63%. Disney reported fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.77 per share on November 17. Its fiscal 2027 outlook includes approximately $11 billion in free cash flow. Management continues to expect double-digit adjusted earnings growth, excluding the impact of the extra week in fiscal 2026.

The extra week lifted fiscal 2026 results and complicates comparisons. Disney must support its cash forecast while investing in content and parks.

For Disney, watch $108, $116, and $142 as reference levels rather than guaranteed floors or targets.

NFLX closed at $59.82, up 0.23%. The first level to watch is $60.50, followed by $64. Clearing a level briefly is different from holding above it as trading continues. A failed move would leave the question of demand unresolved.

Netflix's advertising outlook is useful context, but its date matters. In its October 20 shareholder letter, the company reduced its estimate for 2026 advertising revenue to approximately $2.7 billion from about $3 billion, citing a slower rollout of some advertising capabilities. That was an earlier disclosure, not a new announcement on November 18.

Disney combines streaming with parks and sports, while Netflix depends more directly on subscriptions and advertising. Both must turn audience engagement into durable cash generation.
