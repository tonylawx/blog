A Strong Open Gave Way to Broad Selling

The S&P 500 closed at 7,175.83, down 2.02%, on November 19. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25%, the Dow dropped 2.19%, and the Russell 2000 lost 2.18%. More than 90% of S&P 500 constituents declined. The VIX reached 29.13 before ending at 28.44. A strong open gave way to selling across the market.

The New York Stock Exchange reported the early advance began to fade around 10 a.m. Eastern. The Philadelphia Fed's November survey showed slower activity and hotter input prices. A prime-broker sample for the week through November 12 already showed heavy net selling in technology.

SPY rose to $739.90 before closing at $715.87. QQQ ended at $645.04. The reversal matters because buyers had an opportunity to defend the early advance and failed to hold it through the close. It does not establish that any single event caused the entire day's decline.

For SPY, the first area to watch below the close is $702 to $710. A deeper scenario puts $671 to $694 in view. For QQQ, $642 is the first nearby reference, followed by $600 to $635, including $617. These are areas for assessing the market's response, not guaranteed floors. A recovery above a level that has been lost would be a different signal from a brief intraday touch.

Anthropic's Debut Could Not Hold Its Opening Price

ANT entered the market at an $83 offering price and opened at $111.50. The shares reached $115.10, fell as low as $92.72, and closed at $97.17. That was about 17.1% above the offering price but 12.9% below the opening trade. At 8 p.m. Eastern, the after-hours quote was $92.51. After-hours prices can change on relatively light volume and are separate from the regular close.

Bloomberg reported that more than half of roughly 1,200 institutional bidders received no stock. Allocations show how the offering was distributed. The stock price after the initial turnover will better reflect whether new buyers are coming in. Investors who received shares at the offering price and those who bought in the open market faced very different first-day outcomes. An enthusiastic opening did not prevent a subsequent decline; one session also cannot determine the value of Anthropic's long-term business.

The next question is whether buyers return after the momentum of the debut fades. Watch the share price together with trading conditions and broader market participation. An isolated rebound in a new listing would not, by itself, establish that the wider AI trade has found support.

AMD Faced a Short Report as Chips Sold Off

AMD closed at $489.19, down 7.72%. Hunterbrook Capital disclosed a short position, and an affiliated report raised concerns about AMD's ties to Chinese entities and potential export-control scrutiny. AMD rejected the report's characterization, said it follows applicable export controls and described its compliance processes. The allegations are contested; a short report is not a regulatory finding, and AMD had already retreated from its high before the 10:27 a.m. Eastern report, then fell further.

For a recovery scenario, $501 is the first reference and $528 a higher hurdle. Reclaiming the first level would not automatically clear the second. If buyers cannot sustain a move above those areas, the downside remains open. The size of the decline also matters when assessing how much risk an investor is taking between the current price and a chosen exit.

An Earnings Scenario Is Not an Index Forecast

One way to frame the valuation debate is to start with approximately $388 in estimated index earnings per share and apply earnings multiples of 18 to 22 times. On that rough input, the result is around 6,986 to 8,539 on the S&P 500. That range depends entirely on the estimate and chosen multiples; it is an illustration, not a forecast or a guaranteed destination.

The assumptions can change together. Suppose a component representing 25% of the earnings base experiences a 30% earnings decline, while the remaining 75% is unchanged. The aggregate hit would be 7.5%. Applying that reduction to the rough $388 earnings estimate gives about $359. At the same 18-times multiple, the result is approximately 6,460.

At 6,700 to 6,800, the same revised earnings would trade at about 18.7 to 18.9 times. That makes the band a separate stress scenario. Actual earnings, the weight of the affected companies and the multiple investors pay could all differ. The value of the exercise is to expose which assumptions would need to hold.

Microsoft and TSMC Show Where the Levels Actually Sit

MSFT closed at $498.30. It traded down to $498.16, so it did not breach the $490 reference on November 19. The $469 to $485 area is a lower scenario, not a range the stock reached that session.

Microsoft's latest reported quarter ended September 30. Operating cash flow was $58.0 billion, while cash paid for property and equipment was $39.367 billion. The company reported about $18.6 billion in free cash flow and said it expected full-year fiscal 2027 free cash flow to remain positive. Finance leases and other investing outflows are different cash-flow items and should not be silently combined with that calculation.

Microsoft priced $25 billion of senior unsecured notes on October 29 and issued them on November 4. The company described general corporate purposes that may include capital expenditure, buybacks, acquisitions or debt repayment. Borrowing preserves financing flexibility, but the stated use of proceeds does not establish that every dollar was earmarked for AI infrastructure.

TSM closed at $352.09 after an intraday low of about $351.14. That was near, but still above, the $320 to $350 reference range. A market commentator described a plan to add four percentage points from a six-percent position only after the stock reaches that range and shows signs of stabilization on stronger volume.
