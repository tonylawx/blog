The 10-year is at 5.16%. The auction was still soft

The 10-year yield tagged 5.18% and closed 5.16%, five basis points above Wednesday's 5.11%. The 5-year closed 5.03%. The 30-year closed 5.46%. TLT closed 79.42, down 1.3%. VIX closed 15.67.

Equities did react. Most members still fell. The index only closed flat because the top of the tape carried it. The 7-year auction was soft: about a 0.7 basis-point tail, light indirect demand, and the 10-year pushed after the results. Wednesday's 5-year was worse. Auctions can spike yields for an afternoon. What matters is whether 5.16% holds. If it holds, growth keeps paying the discount rate. If it comes in, the index gets air. Institutions will not smash their own book here. The retreat stays orderly. Give it time and equities still pay for the higher discount rate.

The labor market is still firm, so the Fed stays on inflation. New York Fed's Williams called another hike by year-end a reasonable way to think about it. Cleveland's Hammack said inflation risks remain tilted to the upside. Odds of another hike this fall have not faded. Some prints sit above 70%.

2 · LIQUIDITY

Meta jumped 4.5% on volume. 778 is inside the supply band

META opened 757, high 780, low 748, closed 778, up 4.5%. After Connect it added two more tools: Horizon Create and Horizon Studio. Text prompts can generate 2D and 3D games with levels and multiplayer, then publish into Facebook and Instagram feeds. Glasses, Quest titles, and hardware were the other Connect line. Thursday priced a grab for the gaming entry point, not just another up day in ads.

The daily RSI divergence repaired the strong way: volume-up, not dump-first. RSI is already overbought. Higher from here makes that worse. A pullback is due. 745 to 785 is medium supply. 778 is already inside the band. Supply is not heavy. That is not the same as turning immediately. Today's candle is a strong up day. Momentum is still on. Holders watch for stalling, rather than chasing a second leg at 778. A new high is the same rule: wait for the shape, then roll. Flat cash does not need the first clip at 778.

4 · FOCUS

Oracle invoked force majeure

ORCL opened 137, high 140, low 133, closed 140, down 3.5%. The morning was worse. The close took some of it back. Project Jupiter in New Mexico — a Blue Owl developer campus tied to OpenAI — got a force majeure notice from Oracle. Locals already fought the site on water, air, and power permits. Force majeure is the contract clause for delays outside your control. It can push payments out. The developer says the commitment is unchanged. Oracle's spokesman says the project is still on plan and they are confident in New Mexico, and did not explain the notice. Confidence and the notice on the same day do not match.

The problem is not one missed floor this week. If the campus cannot deliver compute on time, expected revenue slides and the financing already out the door carries more interest. CDS printed a record. The credit tape spoke before the stock did. The bounce from 133 to 140 is a washout snapback, not risk cleared. ORCL still sits in IGV. The path has already decoupled from software. It is a data-center delivery tape. Holders need to underwrite financing and delivery, not wait for a software multiple to mean-revert. The variance is too wide to argue that you must sit.
