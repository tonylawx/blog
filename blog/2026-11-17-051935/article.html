Semiconductors Rebound as the Market Stays Under Pressure

The S&P 500 fell 1.03% on November 17 to 7,340.53, just below its 200-day average of 7,344.53. SPY traded down to $727 before closing at $732.44, above its dividend-adjusted 200-day average of about $729.80. The index and the ETF have separate price histories. For SPY, $728 is a level to watch. QQQ fell 0.73% to $656.95. SOXX recovered from a 3.58% intraday drop to finish up 0.17% at $141.46. The VIX rose about 11% to 25.46. Chip stocks recovered their losses while the broader market and volatility still pointed to caution.

U.S. retail sales rose 0.2% in October, matching the Reuters poll after September's 0.8% gain. The retail control group, which excludes autos, gasoline, building materials, and food services, rose 0.6% against a 0.4% forecast. Headline growth slowed, but the stronger control group points to resilience in some categories.

Nvidia Secures Supply While Guarantees Depend on Delivery

On November 17, Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.844 billion, up 20% from the prior quarter and 103% from a year earlier. That cleared the $109.11 billion LSEG consensus cited by Reuters. Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion. Adjusted earnings were $2.68 per share and adjusted gross margin was 74.9%. Free cash flow came to $43.777 billion, with the timing of tax payments contributing to the sequential change.

Nvidia expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, compared with consensus of $125.25 billion. The quarter has 14 weeks and the guidance excludes Data Center compute revenue from China. Adjusted gross margin is expected at 71% to 72%. Management raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook on the call from about 70% to about 75%. In September, Nvidia added $150 billion to its buyback authorization, with $227.3 billion of authorization remaining at quarter end.

Supply and capacity commitments increased nearly 40% from $279 billion to $390 billion, driven primarily by multiyear memory purchases. Suppliers get a basis for planning production and Nvidia secures memory capacity for future shipments. About 93% of the commitments, or $364 billion, fall through fiscal 2029, with another $26 billion thereafter. Nvidia did not disclose allocations by supplier.

The figures come from Nvidia's CFO commentary. Multiyear memory purchases accounted for most of the increase.

Nvidia also disclosed $32 billion in neocloud purchase agreements. Partners must secure financing and present operational capacity that Nvidia accepts. Nvidia can reduce or cancel the commitments if those conditions go unmet.

Separately, Nvidia reported maximum gross guarantee exposure of $118 billion, up from the prior disclosure of $108.5 billion, which already included Ohio. The total includes $105 billion for the Ohio campus first disclosed in August, $9.5 billion for power and systems, and $3.5 billion for neocloud land, power, and building shells. Nvidia had made no payments under these guarantees as of disclosure. The Ohio guarantee activates in phases as buildings are ready, with the first phase expected in fiscal 2029. For emerging cloud providers, financing and acceptance of operational capacity still determine when the agreements can support delivery and revenue.

The $118 billion is maximum gross exposure. Nvidia reported no payments as of disclosure, and the terms of individual guarantees determine when they activate.

Nvidia's Board Continues Its CEO Search

Chair Harvey Jones said the board has no plans at this point to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO. Interim CEO Colette Kress remains CFO while Nvidia considers internal and external candidates. The board also formed a committee to oversee national security and export controls. Shareholders can watch how the next CEO handles export compliance and whether scrutiny of financing for new customers tightens.

Muddy Waters disclosed a short position and said it planned to cover most or possibly all of it after releasing its report. How much it actually covered remains unconfirmed. Demand and margins held up in the latest quarter. Investors now have to assess how the next management team will choose which customers to support and control its financing exposure.

Buyers Face Another Test Near $728 on SPY

Bank of America's November fund manager survey showed the net overweight in technology falling from 37% to 12%, the largest drop since June 2006. Respondents still held a net overweight. Goldman Sachs prime brokerage clients' technology long-to-short ratio fell to 1.50, the lowest in a series that began in 2016. Both samples show lower appetite for tech exposure. Neither measures marketwide short positions.

Watch whether SPY can recover if it slips through $728 and takes out the November 17 low of $727. Failure to reclaim those levels would weaken the case for a rebound. If the S&P 500 reclaims 7,550 and more sectors participate, short covering could accelerate the advance. A bounce led by a few chip stocks still needs buyers after the covering runs its course. Anthropic is expected to begin trading on November 19, subject to completion of the offering. Demand for the deal and follow-through in the broader market offer two separate tests of risk appetite.
