Rates and funding risk hit risk appetite

All four major U.S. equity indexes closed lower on October 14. The Nasdaq and Russell 2000 each fell 1.9%, while the S&P 500 lost 1.1%. September CPI did not deliver the continued cooling investors wanted. At the same time, the cost of financing AI infrastructure moved into focus. Both pressures weighed on risk appetite, but the evidence does not support assigning the full market decline to a single catalyst.

1 · MACRO

Inflation ran hot, while equipment demand held up

September headline CPI rose 0.6% month over month and 3.7% year over year. Core CPI rose 0.3% month over month and 2.6% year over year. Gasoline prices climbed 9.1% for the month and accounted for more than half of the headline increase. Energy lifted the top-line reading, while monthly core inflation did not accelerate in tandem. Still, headline inflation at 3.7% makes near-term rate-cut bets harder to sustain. Oil prices and core services are the next checks.

ASML reported third-quarter sales of €11.662 billion and net income of €4.96 billion. Its full-year sales outlook is €44 billion to €45 billion. Orders and guidance show that advanced-node equipment demand remains intact; the semiconductor pullback alone is not evidence of weaker demand. Rates are the other pressure point. Higher yields raise the discount-rate burden on long-dated cash flows, especially for high-multiple stocks.

2 · FOCUS

AI's funding bill is part of the valuation debate

Reports that CoreWeave's bond yields topped 15% raise a practical question: can AI infrastructure orders convert to cash fast enough to cover buildout and financing costs? This is a company-specific credit signal, not proof that funding has frozen across the AI industry. But higher financing costs make investors scrutinize customer commitments, capital spending, and cash conversion more closely.

CoreWeave's second-quarter net interest expense was $640 million, up from $267 million a year earlier. Its net loss was $626 million, versus $290 million in the prior-year quarter. The interest burden has grown alongside expansion. If elevated yields persist, highly levered compute providers may remain under pressure. Revenue conversion and stronger operating cash flow would help ease the concern.

3 · RISK

Let key levels set the risk budget

These are technical reference zones from the October 14 market review, not fundamental valuation targets. QQQ needs to reclaim $700–$728; a break below $687 would increase downside risk. Around $486 is the reference support for SOXX. If the indexes hold their levels, semiconductors stabilize, and long yields ease, the pullback would look more like a digestion of high valuations. If QQQ breaks below $687, SOXX loses $486, and yields keep rising, reduce high-beta and funding-sensitive exposure. Reassess after prices reclaim those levels.

Taiwan Semiconductor's results this week are the next catalyst. In a bullish scenario, advanced-node demand and the capital-spending outlook stay firm, helping semiconductors stabilize before the broader market. In a neutral scenario, orders are healthy but guidance is not raised, leaving rates and valuation in control. In a bearish scenario, guidance or gross-margin commentary cools while funding costs rise; a break below $486 in SOXX could spread semiconductor weakness into tech indexes. Wait for the post-earnings price reaction and support levels to confirm the signal. Do not chase the order headline alone.
