Waller Turns Dovish as Services Inflation Simmers

On Thursday, September 3, hawkish Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller struck a noticeably dovish tone, pushing Treasury yields lower and fueling a broad rally across all four major US equity benchmarks. The S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow Jones Industrial Average all gained more than 1%, with 341 advancers against 160 decliners in the S&P 500 and 8 out of 11 sectors finishing higher. In contrast, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index SOXX lagged, up just 0.1% as constituents split evenly. The price action mirrors the earlier rotation seen in enterprise software: capital is rotating away from crowded semiconductor exposure and back into mega-cap platforms and enterprise software.

Waller stated that he leans toward keeping the policy rate unchanged at the September meeting rather than hiking, noting that accelerating inflation appears contained and waiting one meeting carries minimal economic cost. He indicated that a three-month annualized inflation rate of 2.8% remains acceptable. Following his remarks, fed funds futures pared September rate-hike odds to 50%. Meanwhile, economic data sent mixed signals: August Challenger layoffs rose to 52,881, the highest August tally since 2022, led by consumer goods companies including Procter & Gamble and Estee Lauder. Tech layoffs fell to 6,103, and corporate restructuring replaced AI as the primary reason for job cuts for the first time since February. Simultaneously, the August ISM Services PMI rose to 55.4, new orders jumped to 60.9, and the prices paid index climbed to 72.3, while initial jobless claims ticked up to 206,000. Services inflation shows little sign of decelerating, leaving Friday nonfarm payrolls as the decisive macro catalyst.

To manage near-term swings, the $315 to $368 region subdivides into a key pivot between $331 and $368. A shallow pullback that holds above $331 would represent constructive digestion, offering an attractive tactical entry for swing traders. Conversely, a break below $331 would shift the center of gravity downward, significantly raising the odds of testing the lower bound at $315. Technical structure on weekly and monthly timeframes remains firmly bullish, though the daily chart retains an unreset bearish momentum divergence dating back to August 21. Such divergences typically resolve either through a high-volume surge that overrides the signal or a controlled pullback that consolidates gains. Given elevated volatility, TSLA warrants disciplined swing execution around defined risk levels rather than long-term position building at current prices.

Microsoft Overhauls Segments to Showcase AI Monetization

MSFT advanced 2% on Thursday, fully erasing three sessions of declines and reclaiming near-term overhead resistance. Fundamentally, Microsoft announced a comprehensive restructuring of its public financial reporting beginning in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Its three legacy operating segments—Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing—will be consolidated into two streamlined divisions: Agent and Infrastructure, and Devices and Consumers.

This reorganization merges commercial cloud and enterprise software into a single unified AI infrastructure stack while isolating consumer hardware and services into a separate bucket. When enterprise AI monetization lags expectations, management typically obscures performance inside broader conglomerates. By proactively breaking out AI-driven commercial operations into an explicit reporting line, Microsoft signals high conviction in its AI revenue run rate. Transparently exposing this foundation to institutional investors should also alleviate concerns over rising CDS credit default swap spreads tied to free cash flow consumption.

In terms of market structure, MSFT rebounded sharply after testing a low of $496 on Wednesday. The critical gating level remains $514. A valid breakout requires an open or close above $514 rather than an intraday spike. As a core portfolio anchor, short-term chop requires no reaction, with the intermediate-to-long-term valuation objective pegged above $600.
