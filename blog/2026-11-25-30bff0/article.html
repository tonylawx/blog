The close looked calmer than the session

U.S. stocks recovered most of their early losses on November 25. The S&P 500 finished down 0.3%, and the Nasdaq lost 0.6%. The Dow gained 0.3%, while the Russell 2000 added 0.7%. The intraday lows told a much harsher story. SPY touched $695.45, down about 3.25% from the prior close of $718.78. QQQ fell as low as $607.74, a 4.76% drawdown from $638.10. SOXX briefly dropped to $118.18, or 8.12% below its prior close of $128.62.

By the close, SOXX had recovered to $127.98, down only 0.50%. SPY finished at $716.70, down 0.29%, and QQQ closed at $633.93, down 0.65%. Buyers absorbed much of the early supply. The close near the top of the day's range shows demand at the lows, but it does not prove institutions have rebuilt a durable long position.

The VIX did not confirm a return to calm. It reached 34.95 intraday and closed at 28.29, still 7.3% above the prior close of 26.36. Equity prices recovered, but the options market continued to charge a higher price for protection. Eight of eleven S&P sectors finished higher, so the session was not a uniform liquidation. Technology remained one of the weaker groups. Calling this an intraday recovery is fair; calling it a confirmed market reversal is premature.

Three headlines may have helped, but none proves the cause

The FCC's decision on SpaceX's Starmind orbital data-centre system was real. The order, released on November 25, granted part of SpaceX's application and deferred part of it, subject to conditions. It authorizes backup and emergency telemetry, tracking, and command communications for a proposed constellation of up to one million orbital data-centre satellites. The first satellite is not required until 2033, with later deployment milestones extending to 2040. It is a meaningful long-term infrastructure headline, not new capacity that can start serving customers today.

Michael Burry also confirmed that he sold the remaining half of his Nasdaq-100 put position. In his November 25 post, he said his portfolio was temporarily almost entirely long, with about 25% in cash. He also said he would keep assessing future short opportunities. That change could have reduced the market's supply of fresh bearish bets and encouraged some short-term traders to take profits. One investor's trade, however, cannot explain the full index move or prove that institutions broadly turned bullish.

Foreign exchange was volatile as well. The yen strengthened while the U.S. dollar slipped, and dollar-yen traded through a wide intraday range. That timing coincided with the equity rebound. It does not establish that yen carry-trade money flowed back into stocks. Yen strength can also reflect safe-haven demand or leveraged positions being cut.

My read is that short covering and fast-money buying likely amplified the rebound. The SpaceX authorization and Burry's update gave traders plausible headlines to trade around. The more important test comes next: do cash equity buyers keep showing up, does volume confirm the move, and do credit spreads stop widening? A fast move off the lows cannot answer those questions by itself.

Strong growth, sticky inflation, and resilient claims

October headline PCE inflation rose 0.3% month over month, slower than September's 0.5%. The year-over-year rate edged up from 3.7% to 3.8%. Core PCE rose 0.3% on the month, while its annual rate moved from 3.1% to 3.3%. Monthly inflation cooled a little, but the annual core trend did not. The report gives the Federal Reserve no clean reason to rush toward easier policy.

The second estimate of third-quarter GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualized rate from the advance estimate of 3.4%. The Bureau of Economic Analysis attributed the revision mainly to stronger investment and consumer spending. The official GDP tables do not identify 65% of growth as coming from A.I. investment. A.I. infrastructure spending is an important part of the investment story, but the specific 65% attribution is not an official BEA statistic.

The labour market also remained resilient. Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000 in the week ended November 21, below the 200,000 consensus. Continuing claims declined to 1.697 million for the week ended November 14. Before the data, markets were pricing roughly a 98% chance of a 25-basis-point Fed rate hike in December, according to Reuters.

That combination cuts both ways for equities. Strong activity supports revenue expectations. Sticky inflation and firm employment also make it harder to price in a quick policy pivot. High-multiple technology stocks and companies that rely on external funding remain sensitive to that rate path.

Nvidia's AA rating is on negative watch, putting AI financing back in focus

On November 25, S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's issuer and senior unsecured debt ratings on CreditWatch Negative. The ratings remain AA. This is not a downgrade. S&P said it could cut the rating by one notch within 90 days if concerns over board independence, management oversight, and key-person risk are not addressed. A larger move would require further evidence of damage to Nvidia's reputation or business outlook.

The bigger read-through is Nvidia's role in the financing chain. On its Q3 fiscal 2027 earnings call, the company disclosed $118 billion of total guarantees at quarter-end. That included $105 billion of credit support for land, power, and shell capacity tied to a site that will host Nvidia infrastructure under a 20-year lease to OpenAI. Nvidia also disclosed $32 billion of neocloud commitments and slightly more than $100 billion of data-centre lease and cloud-service commitments. These are different categories of exposure and should not be added together. They are also not the same as cash debt due immediately.

A CreditWatch action does not automatically change the ratings of Nvidia's customers or make existing guarantees payable. It can, however, affect how lenders assess future credit support, collateral haircuts, loan spreads, and covenant terms. Cash-rich hyperscalers may be able to absorb those changes. Smaller compute operators that depend on refinancing and concentrated customers have less room to manoeuvre.

Credit markets have not offered the same relief as equities. Reuters reported record or near-record credit-protection costs for several AI-linked issuers, including Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX, and CoreWeave. Separate coverage also reported fresh highs in credit-risk measures for Nvidia and CoreWeave. That is a different time horizon from a half-hour equity squeeze. Stocks can recover quickly; multi-year financing risk takes longer to reprice.

December needs confirmation, not a Christmas-rally prediction

The VIX closed at 28.29, which is still a high-volatility reading. Short covering may have reduced immediate selling pressure. Seasonal allocations from ETFs and long-term household accounts could add support in early December, but seasonality is not a guarantee. Any new buying can still be offset by active selling.

For SPY, the key breakout level in the current scenario is $753. At the November 25 close of $716.70, that is still about 5.1% higher. Until SPY clears $753 on convincing volume, a range-bound approach makes more sense than chasing the first rebound. A fast, volume-backed break above that level in the first week of December would strengthen the case for a sustained year-end rally.

QQQ closed at $633.93. The $633–$636 area is the immediate pivot zone. The $585 area is a deeper support reference, while $700 is the next major overhead level in this framework. QQQ has higher beta than SPY, so watch for volume and confirmation from large-cap technology leaders rather than relying on the index level alone.

SOXX has room to rebound, but the bear-market repair is not finished

SOXX reached a three-month intraday high of $198.81 on October 2 and closed at $127.98 on November 25. That leaves it about 35.6% below the high. The intraday low of $118.18 was met with buying, but a rebound from the lows does not repair the medium-term trend.

The first rebound zone is $137 to $144. From the November 25 close, that represents roughly 7.1% to 12.5% upside. A routine round of short covering could help the ETF test that range. The second target is around $163, or about 27.4% above the close. That move would need more than positioning relief; it would need a genuine improvement in fundamentals and sustained buying. The $180–$181 area is a much higher resistance zone, roughly 41% above the close. Only a high-volume break and hold above that area would justify revisiting the call that the technical bear market has ended. These are scenario levels, not promised returns.

TSM closed at $342.34 and traded as high as $343.17. That puts it right at the prior range ceiling around $342–$343. It touched the level, but still needs follow-through. A hold above the range and a successful retest would strengthen the breakout case. A move back below it would weaken the signal.

NFLX closed at $59.41. The near-term test is still a volume-backed break above its descending trendline, followed by a hold. One rebound session alone is not enough to call a trend change.

My conclusion is that November 25 improved the short-term price structure and may have eased crowded bearish positioning. It did not remove the financing risks in the A.I. buildout. Watch three things together: SPY clearing $753 on volume, SOXX holding its first rebound zone, and whether Nvidia-related guarantees and neocloud funding costs stop deteriorating. Price can turn first. Credit risk needs more time to confirm.
