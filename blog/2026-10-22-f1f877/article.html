The selloff was concentrated, but semiconductors took the hardest hit

On October 22, the Nasdaq Composite fell about 2.6%, the S&P 500 lost 1.21%, and the Dow edged higher. Semiconductor stocks underperformed sharply. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped roughly 6.7%, while the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) closed at $475.69, down 6.79%. SOXX volume reached roughly 15 million shares, up from about 8.4 million in the prior session.

This was not a uniform liquidation across every asset. The damage was concentrated in AI-linked technology and semiconductors. Still, a high-volume breakdown changes the short-term setup. A falling semiconductor ETF should no longer be treated as an automatic buying opportunity.

1 · MARKET

The market is testing AI returns, not just AI demand

Long-term demand for AI infrastructure has not disappeared in a day. Investors are asking whether the data-center buildout can be financed at a reasonable cost and eventually generate enough revenue and free cash flow.

Data centers, compute, and power infrastructure require heavy upfront investment. If financing stays expensive or credit becomes less available, some externally funded operators may delay projects, reduce equipment purchases, or struggle to pay suppliers on schedule. That could pass through to server, chip, and equipment vendors. Demand can exist without every customer being able to finance it, and a signed order is not the same as cash collected.

The chain of risk is plausible, but it is too early to call this an industry-wide collapse. The stronger conclusion is that investors are demanding clearer evidence on financing and returns. CapEx guidance, free cash flow, and order quality matter as much as headline AI growth.

2 · FOCUS

Meta's new Class C shares sharpened the funding debate

Meta's regulatory filing proposes a new class of non-voting Class C common stock, with the amended charter expected to take effect on January 19, 2027. Subject to the required conditions, the company plans to distribute one Class C share for every outstanding Class A and Class B share. The initial distribution is expected to have the economic effect of a two-for-one stock split, without immediately changing shareholders' economic interests.

The separate issue is that Meta expects to use Class C shares for future equity offerings. The company has not set the size, timing, or price of any future offering. Selling new shares to raise capital could dilute existing holders economically; distributing the new class to current shareholders is a different transaction.

The funding concerns also come against a demanding investment backdrop. Meta guided to 2026 capital expenditures of $135 billion to $145 billion, and reported negative free cash flow of about $2.12 billion for the third quarter. News reports said the stock fell around 7% on the day. Those facts help explain the market's focus on returns, but they do not prove that one filing alone caused the full selloff.

3 · RISK

Four groups of tech stocks face different risks

Chip and equipment suppliers. NVDA, AMD, TSM, AVGO, MRVL, and ASML still have long-term exposure to AI infrastructure. The issue is whether growth, margins, and valuation can all meet expectations. A company can keep growing and still see its multiple compress if growth falls short of what investors already paid for.

Highly leveraged data-center operators. Here the balance sheet is more immediate. Track free cash flow, debt maturities, financing costs, liquidity, capital commitments, and customer payment capacity. Revenue growth or a large contract book is not enough if the business needs repeated external funding to meet its commitments.

Large platform companies. MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, and META also invest heavily in AI infrastructure, but their broader businesses generally provide more internal cash-flow support. That is an advantage, not immunity. The key test is whether incremental AI revenue and profit can increasingly cover the additional capital and operating costs. Meta also faces uncertainty around the scale and pricing of any future equity raise.

Tech companies with less direct AI-capex exposure, plus defensive sectors. They may hold up relatively well when money seeks shelter, but relative strength does not make them cheap or guarantee continued gains. Earnings quality, valuation, and each industry's own cycle still matter.

4 · SEMIS

After the SOXX breakdown, wait for repair

SOXX closed at $475.69, below the $486 area cited as a previous technical reference. That level is a market observation, not a guaranteed support. Until the ETF can reclaim and hold that area, rallies should be treated cautiously. One green session alone would not confirm that the breakdown has failed.

A more useful sequence is to watch whether selling pressure eases, whether price builds a base with higher lows, and whether volume and sector breadth improve during a rebound. If semiconductor stocks diverge more sharply from one another, the ETF may also recover less smoothly. Investors should decide whether they want broad semiconductor exposure or a smaller set of companies with stronger cash generation, balance sheets, and more reasonable valuations.

5 · FOCUS

Three things to watch next

First, watch capital-spending guidance, free cash flow, and AI monetization at the large platforms. If revenue and profits increasingly cover new investment, concerns over returns can ease.

Second, watch financing conditions at data-center operators: debt maturities, borrowing costs, liquidity, and customer payments. If projects require repeated outside funding to stay on track, revenue growth alone will not tell the whole story.

Third, watch price structure in semiconductors. Whether SOXX can reclaim the $486 area, whether trading volume normalizes, and whether losses spread beyond AI-linked assets will help distinguish a sector correction from a broader risk-off move. Clearer evidence of returns could support a rebound. More spending, delayed monetization, and higher funding costs could prolong valuation pressure.

6 · FOCUS

The bottom line: demand is real, but returns still have to be proven

The October 22 selloff does not establish that the AI investment cycle is over. It does show that the next winners may be the companies that can finance their plans, deliver projects, and turn spending into cash—not simply the companies with the largest order books.

Do not treat every pullback as a crash, but do not ignore a high-volume breakdown either. Check cash flow and funding conditions first, then wait for price confirmation. For SOXX, patience may be more valuable than trying to call the exact bottom before it reclaims a key technical area.
