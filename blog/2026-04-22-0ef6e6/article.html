0 · Market Read

The near-term bias remains constructive, but it depends on the Middle East conflict not worsening. The more likely path is consolidation near the highs or a modest grind higher rather than an immediate deep pullback. JPMorgan raising its 2026 S&P 500 target from 7,200 to 7,600 also reflects renewed optimism around AI and broader risk appetite.

Three factors support that view. First, markets increasingly expect the US and Iran to reach some form of negotiated agreement, with reopening the Strait of Hormuz still viewed as the most likely outcome. Second, downside protection remains relatively inexpensive, suggesting investors are not paying aggressively for an imminent drawdown. Third, the corporate buyback window has reopened while short covering continues, providing additional support on weakness. Overall, the setup looks more like bullish consolidation near the highs than another one-way acceleration.

1 · Risk Watch

The biggest risk remains headline volatility, particularly abrupt changes in ceasefire negotiations, Strait access, or the timetable for talks. A renewed escalation would quickly invalidate the current consolidation thesis and push investors back toward a more defensive posture.

The second risk is valuation and earnings validation. JPMorgan’s higher target largely reflects stronger AI sentiment and risk appetite, while its more neutral valuation framework remains closer to 7,400. That suggests 7,600 still includes a meaningful sentiment premium rather than being fully supported by earnings. With mega-cap technology earnings concentrated over the next two weeks, weaker results, guidance, or AI monetization could quickly trigger profit-taking and multiple compression.

2 · Names to Watch

The first focus is the software ETF IGV. The key breakout level has moved slightly higher from 88.6 to 88.9 after heavy volume appeared near 88.8 intraday. A decisive close or open above 88.9 would provide stronger breakout confirmation and could trigger additional short covering across software.

The second focus is ISRG and MSFT. I do not see a compelling reason to add ISRG at current levels; a break below 430 would create a cleaner point to reassess the setup. Until then, it looks more like consolidation near the highs than an attractive chase. MSFT is also waiting on broader software confirmation. If IGV breaks out, the probability of a sustained MSFT advance improves. On weakness, the stock can still be accumulated gradually for the long term.

3 · Trading Strategy

At this stage, I would favour conditional, level-based trades over emotional chasing. At the index level, the constructive view holds as long as the geopolitical backdrop does not deteriorate. If headlines turn materially worse, the priority should be reducing risk rather than fighting the move.

At the sector and single-stock level, the key question is whether IGV can hold a breakout above 88.9. If it does, software becomes more attractive after confirmation. If it fails, treat the group as range-bound and avoid chasing. For ISRG, wait rather than front-run the setup and reassess below 430. For MSFT, even a move through resistance is better traded after confirmation than on the first touch.
