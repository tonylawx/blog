Yields climb, tech takes the hit first

The new week opened soft: major indexes finished slightly lower on light volume. Crude jumped more than 6%, and energy led S&P sectors with a gain near 4.6%, while real estate, utilities, and tech lagged. Money rotated toward inflation-sensitive and real-asset exposure; long-duration growth got pushed back in line.

The sharper pressure came from bonds. Treasury yields rose across the curve, and US10Y clawed back the decline since last Tuesday. Small- and mid-cap growth and tech felt it — not because fundamentals broke overnight, but because discount rates moved first. Wednesday’s CPI is the next pricing anchor. Into the print, odds of a September hike sit around a coin flip; FOMC voter Harker sounded hawkish on full employment and the possible need for multiple hikes. That mix rarely rewards blind dip-buying.

1 · SEMIS

NVDA: a $500B financing loop cuts both ways

Reports said a group of institutions would partner with NVDA to supply about $500 billion for AI infrastructure. The company then confirmed deals with six major Wall Street firms: lenders will extend preferential-rate loans to NVDA customers. Jensen Huang framed it as more than selling chips — helping customers build “AI factories.”

The mechanism is straightforward: credit lands on customers’ books, customers buy GPUs, near-term orders and revenue get amplified. Short term, that is a demand accelerator. Longer term, it is circular credit — some demand is financed into existence rather than funded by proven AI cash flows. Cisco walked a similar path before 2000; when the cycle turns, that elasticity can hit valuation hard.

So today’s tape is not one-sided. Funding can lift near-term shipments; if AI monetization disappoints and borrowers fail, guarantee and reputation risk can rebound onto the chip vendor. Semiconductors and NVDA sold off as the market digested “demand purity.”

Technically, NVDA still sits in the rising channel after the 216 breakout. Hold; do not overtrade a one-day narrative. Exit only on a clear trend break. Large capital is still writing real checks into AI — until the trend breaks, follow the money rather than call the top.

2 · CONSUMER

MSFT: custom silicon thickens the moat; no tinkering

The Information reported MSFT plans a sharp ramp in next-gen custom AI chip output. After Maia and follow-ons, attention is on a “300-series,” with talks involving TSM to secure delivery of more than 300,000 custom chips in 2027. Google, Amazon, and Microsoft are all building silicon to cut external GPU dependence and tune for their own clouds. For MSFT, that is a multi-year moat and efficiency project, not a day-trade headline.

It is also not a simple negative for the NVDA ecosystem: hyperscaler silicon often absorbs specialized/inference loads, while high-end training GPUs remain scarce. For Microsoft, it reduces “supply choke” risk premium and cleans up the fundamental story.

Price still stalls under 516, with daily overbought and bearish divergence. Two repairs: volume break through 516 toward ~534 then retest, or a direct pullback that resets both conditions. Even on a pullback, the main watch band remains 469–487 (~5%). The top holding stays put — no short-term flip trades while the fundamental map is clear.

BRK.B: faster buybacks are the hard signal

BRK.B Q2 after-tax operating earnings (ex-FX) were about $12 billion, up ~6% year over year. The growth is ordinary; the signal is repurchase: ~$235 million in Q1, ~$4.5 billion in Q2, plus ~$3.4 billion more in July. The rule is unchanged — management buys when price sits below intrinsic value and cash stays ample (well above the $30 billion floor).

From the May 2024 pause on rich valuation to the Q2/July acceleration, the 470–500 zone looks like a clear undervaluation band to Buffett’s team. Cash on the bid beats verbal “cheap.”

Forward ranges: 2026 about 406–674 (mid 540), 2027 about 321–698 (mid 560). Spot near 529 is not expensive versus the 2027 mid, but EPS growth is slow (~3% in each of the next two years, ~7% five-year CAGR) — do not expect fireworks. Technically, the move above 510 put it on the right side; 529–538 is resistance into new highs. Use it as a low-beta ballast, not a momentum lottery.
