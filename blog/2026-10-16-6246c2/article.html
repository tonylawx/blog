US Stocks · Options · News · Views

Friday's selling was concentrated in technology and semiconductors. The Nasdaq fell about 2.1%, the S&P 500 lost roughly 1.1%, and the Dow slipped 0.3%. For the week, the Nasdaq was down about 4.5% and the S&P 500 1.9%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index posted its eighth consecutive decline.

That does not prove AI demand is collapsing. The market is asking a tougher question: how much future growth is worth paying for when infrastructure requires more capital and financing costs are rising? Even a company that keeps growing can see its multiple compress if growth falls short of an already aggressive expectation.

1 · FOCUS

Anthropic and the repricing of AI growth

Some investors are discussing an Anthropic IPO valuation near $1.5 trillion, down from expectations around $2 trillion. The company reported third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, up 45% quarter over quarter, and adjusted profitability for the quarter. Revenue is still growing quickly, but the growth rate has cooled from the prior quarter.

The signal is not that AI adoption has stopped. It is that investors are becoming less willing to pay any price for it. They are scrutinizing the capital required to build data centers, the time needed to earn a return, and how much cash remains after financing costs. Recent stress in AI infrastructure stocks and debt markets shows that funding conditions are now part of the sector's risk equation.

2 · FOCUS

TSMC is executing, but valuation still matters

TSMC reported third-quarter revenue of NT$1.494 trillion, or $46.72 billion, up 50.9% year over year. Net income rose 68.7% to NT$763.1 billion, and diluted EPS was NT$29.43, equivalent to $4.60 per ADR. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance is $52.3 billion to $54.3 billion, with a 65% to 67% gross-margin range.

Gross margin was 66.4%, a touch below the roughly 66.5% market expectation cited in the discussion. Early ramp costs for 2-nanometer production may affect near-term margins, even as the core business remains strong. A strong report does not insulate the stock from a broader reset in AI growth expectations.

The working price framework for TSM is $410–$424 as an aggressive entry zone, $399–$406 for possible staged buying, $360–$378 as a more attractive add zone, and $320–$350 if a deeper market panic develops. Around $412, the stock is still in the aggressive zone rather than at a clear bargain. These are conditional levels, not a prediction that price must reach them.

Index and semiconductor levels to watch

For SPY, the $759–$776 area needs confirmation; $729 is the more important downside reference. For QQQ, watch $700–$728, with $687 as a more significant bearish trigger and $687–$694 as a key nearby zone. An intraday break is not the same as a confirmed breakdown; the close and the ability to reclaim support matter.

For SOXX, the $486–$532 area is in focus, with $486 as the key lower level after an extended losing streak. For SMH, watch support near $557; a move through $534–$547 would make the setup more vulnerable. Semiconductor exposure should not be treated as one all-or-nothing position. Company quality, entry valuation, and the actual support level all matter.

4 · SEMIS

META, AVGO and AMD have different risk gates

For META, $609 is the important downside reference. A confirmed break could open the door to a larger adjustment, while the $656–$730 area should be assessed in the context of the broader market. Profitable tactical positions can be trimmed before the final model level if the setup deteriorates.

For AVGO, the $324–$365 band matters, with $324 the key lower level. The current closing pattern is weak; a small stop distance alone is not a reason to buy. A better setup requires selling pressure to stabilize and important levels to hold.

For AMD, watch $506–$528. Its valuation embeds high growth expectations, so a downward revision to the growth outlook could produce a larger multiple reset than in more resilient businesses. A positive long-term view of AI does not make every entry price attractive.

5 · SETUP

Keep cash optionality and trade the conditions

Separate holdings into three groups: durable businesses that become more interesting at lower valuations; positions that have broken important levels and need risk reduction; and high-volatility names that simply have not offered a clear entry yet.

Cash still has option value. Buying early means carrying the drawdown and waiting through volatility. Waiting preserves the ability to act when the reward-to-risk improves. Next week, watch whether index support actually fails, whether semiconductor selling broadens, and whether any rebound is confirmed by price action. A valuation reset in the AI trade is not proof that every strong business has lost value, but a long-term story is no reason to ignore price.
