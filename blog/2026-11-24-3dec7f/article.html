0 · MACRO

A split market, with pressure concentrated in tech

U.S. stocks did not sell off across the board on November 24. The S&P 500 closed at 7,201.09, down 0.50%. The Nasdaq fell 1.48%, while the Dow gained 0.85% to 50,018.70. Breadth across the S&P 500 remained positive, with 348 advancers and 154 decliners. Consumer staples and health care held up better. The pressure was concentrated in technology and semiconductors.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about 7.8%, with all 30 constituents lower. The index had surged 14.03% in the prior session, then gave back 7.88% the next day. That reversal points to unstable demand rather than a durable wave of buying. Nvidia fell 5.86%, AMD dropped 8.86%, Broadcom lost 3.98%, and Micron slid 11.01%. The tape looked more like concentrated de-risking in technology than broad market panic.

Policy headlines added uncertainty. The U.S. Trade Representative was nearing the release of findings from an industrial-capacity investigation covering 16 major trading partners. The findings could lead to another round of tariffs, but the final rates remained unclear. The tariff story added to the risk backdrop; it does not, on its own, explain the semiconductor selloff.

Consumer confidence moved in the other direction. The Conference Board index rose 11.4 points to 103.3 in November, above the 92.5 consensus and its highest reading since January 2025. Consumers also became more optimistic about the labour-market outlook. Stronger confidence did not stop investors from reducing exposure to high-multiple technology shares.

1 · RISK

Credit markets are flagging the funding risk

The A.I. trade is shifting from the size of capital spending to the cost of funding and the return on that spending. Building data centres is no longer the only question. The bigger test is whether the new capacity can produce revenue, earnings, and free cash flow.

Public credit-trading data put Oracle's five-year credit default swap spread around 360 to 370 basis points, while CoreWeave was above 1,000 basis points. Nvidia was around 143 basis points and Broadcom around 180. Meta was near 115, Amazon and Alphabet were around 100, and Microsoft was near 52 basis points. The latest print dates differ across names, and single-name trading can be thin. These figures are useful for tracking direction, not as firm executable quotes.

A wider credit default swap spread means investors are demanding more compensation for credit protection. It does not mean a company is about to default, and it is not a substitute for a rating-agency assessment. Some reported rating changes could not be matched consistently to agency announcements, so they should not be treated as confirmed facts.

The funding mix is the real point of divergence. Large cloud companies with enough operating cash flow to fund investment are in a different position from infrastructure providers that rely on repeated debt issuance, equity financing, or customer prepayments. Wider spreads raise refinancing costs and can make lower-return data-centre projects less attractive.

My view is that demand for A.I. infrastructure has not disappeared because stocks fell for one session. But investors are raising the bar for returns. Watch data-centre utilization, cloud revenue growth, capital-spending delivery, depreciation, and free cash flow. Orders and construction plans only become durable business returns when end customers keep paying for the capacity.

2 · SEMIS

Semiconductors are repricing before earnings estimates turn

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is down more than 20% from its recent high, putting it in technical bear-market territory. That describes the price decline; it does not prove that the industry's fundamentals have collapsed. Much of the current pressure still looks like multiple compression. The next stage would be more serious if earnings estimates began moving down across the sector.

Memory stocks need a separate lens. Micron fell more than 11% on the day, but memory pricing, inventories, and supply-demand conditions can change quickly. A low forward price-to-earnings multiple does not automatically make a memory stock cheap. When peak earnings roll over, earnings per share can fall quickly and make the headline multiple misleading.

Nvidia has its own set of variables. Beyond demand for computing, investors need to watch customer funding, data-centre buildout schedules, supply-chain commitments, and corporate governance. A management change does not automatically fix financing links, customer concentration, or cash-flow quality. To tell whether the sector is moving from valuation pressure to a fundamental reset, track earnings estimates for Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom rather than focusing on one day's index move.

3 · SETUP

Stock setups — wait for support to hold

AMD closed at $413.27, down 8.86%, and fell below its 200-day moving average again. The $400 to $424 area needs to be reclaimed, but support there remains fragile. If the stock cannot recover the $400 area, the next notable historical trading zone sits below $366. That is an area to monitor, not a guaranteed downside target.

TSMC and ASML both filled the gaps created by the prior session's rebound. Their short-term setups have weakened. The next test is whether each can stabilize inside the previous trading range and attract a second wave of buyers. One quiet session after a gap fill is not enough to confirm a reversal. A stress-case valuation floor for TSMC is separate from a technical support level and should not be treated as an entry price.

Broadcom broke below the prior week's low, while its rebound has lagged parts of the group. When the stock closes at a fresh low, even an intraday lower wick deserves less weight as a reversal signal. Marvell has been volatile since losing $160. A long lower wick, a recovery back into the prior range, and follow-through buying are needed before calling a near-term base.

Nvidia's support area sits around $132 to $150, where buyers still need to show up. Overhead resistance sits around $174 to $194. A bounce after a weak open is not a new uptrend unless volume improves and price can reclaim key levels over more than one session.

4 · FOCUS

The S&P 500 is sitting on 7,200

The S&P 500 closed at 7,201.09, almost exactly on 7,200. That is the near-term line to watch; 7,100 is the more important downside stress level. If the index can hold 7,200 and form a second leg of strong buying near the prior lows, staged entries become more reasonable. A break below 7,100 would make the short-term structure materially weaker.

There is little reason to rush orders in the first 30 minutes after the open. The final hour matters more, especially the last 30 minutes, when buyers have to show whether they are willing to absorb supply. For index trades, confirmation is more useful than trying to pick the exact low.

The core view is straightforward: semiconductors are in a technical bear market, but that alone does not invalidate the A.I. investment thesis. The real test is whether spending turns into cash returns before higher funding costs squeeze the expansion cycle. If earnings estimates avoid broad, sustained cuts, the market may remain in a volatile valuation reset. If earnings expectations start falling as well, the risk map for the entire A.I. capex chain needs to be reassessed.
