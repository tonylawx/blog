Light volume ahead of CPI

Tuesday stayed quiet on light volume.

Of the four major indexes, only IWM finished higher. The others chopped sideways with little volatility.

Communication services lagged, mostly because of weakness in GOOG.

SPX has consolidated near record highs for about a week after the breakout.

NDX has not made another high, but it cleared prior resistance and is also consolidating on lighter volume. There is still no meaningful breakdown.

SOXX continues to lag. Its rebound remains below key resistance.

Wednesday’s CPI is the next major macro catalyst.

Weak payroll data has already pulled hike odds lower. CME pricing for a September hike slipped from roughly 51% to 48%.

Unless CPI comes in much hotter than expected, those odds could keep easing and support risk assets.

A clear upside surprise would likely lift long-end yields and put fresh pressure on valuations.

1 · MACRO

Three drivers: oil, rates, and AI

The tape is still driven by three themes: oil, rates, and AI.

On oil, the U.S.-Iran path remains uncertain. But both sides still look motivated to avoid a major escalation, so an overnight move above $100 looks less likely.

Volatility is still possible. A panic spike is a higher bar.

Energy feeds inflation expectations. A capped oil move would give the Fed more room to stay on hold.

The bigger risk is the Fed’s renewed focus on incoming data.

Officials are more data-dependent and less willing to give firm forward guidance.

One or two hot inflation prints could quickly revive hike expectations, push 10Y, 20Y, and 30Y yields higher, and compress equity multiples.

On AI, Q2 results from the major platforms and semiconductor names were broadly solid.

META lagged a bit on monetization. The others looked cleaner.

More importantly, 2026 CapEx plans have not been cut. Some companies raised them. AMD and peers also lifted outlooks.

That leaves August sentiment less stressed than during July’s pullback.

Three risks still matter: a geopolitical deal that drags into the midterm cycle, slower hyperscaler CapEx growth rather than an outright decline, and memory-order cuts from pricing or financing stress that could feed credit risk back through the chip supply chain.

2 · RISK

GOOG pullback still looks constructive

GOOG fell about 3.61% in an otherwise quiet market, with no clear company-specific negative.

A more plausible driver is the investment book. The SpaceX stake added large unrealized gains to Q2 earnings. A lower SpaceX mark could reverse part of that and weigh on reported Q3 profit.

If the valuation case already excludes those investment gains, the fundamental thesis does not need to be rewritten.

Technically, the breakout structure above 144 remains intact.

For underweight investors, this still looks more like a buyable pullback than a confirmed trend break.

3 · SOFTWARE

Software: IGV still needs 107

IGV remains capped at 107.

It failed there for two straight sessions and closed around 103–104. The key range is 101–107.

A convincing breakout above 107 on volume would open another leg higher.

Another rejection would raise the risk of a topping structure.

Until that breakout is confirmed, there is little reason to front-run it.

Individual names are still highly dependent on the sector:

• ORCL: mostly tracking IGV, with limited company-specific momentum. Resistance sits at 209–226, then 232–240. Major support is 147–159.

• PLTR: stuck in 175–192. A clean move above 192 would put new highs back in play. Failure leaves 134–145 as a possible retest. Swing traders who already caught the move can trim and add back after a confirmed break above 192.

• CRM: near 197 and needs a volume-backed break above 198. Structural resistance sits at 235–277, implying roughly 20%–30% upside if the sector cooperates. IGV above 107 remains the key confirmation signal.

• INTU: still more rebound than independent trend. Resistance is 346–363. A reclaim of 426 would be needed for stronger confirmation. Volume has not fully dried up, and there are signs of buying lower.

• APP: gapped down about 6% to around 318 after a downgrade questioned roughly 30% growth in 2027. Key support is 317–350. A close or open below 317 is a de-risking signal and could open more downside.

• ADBE: similar to ORCL, but in a somewhat better spot. Support is 235–248. The stock is testing 255–268, including the 200-day moving average. Reclaiming the 200-day could attract systematic buying, with 322–361 next.

The cybersecurity trio PANW, CRWD, and FTNT continues to consolidate near record highs.

Valuations are not cheap, but the breakout structures remain intact for now.

4 · SETUP

How I’m positioning this week

There is still no obvious catalyst for a broad panic selloff. Realized volatility remains subdued.

CPI is the main event this week. Retail and discretionary earnings follow next week. Jackson Hole comes later in the month.

As long as SPX stays near record highs, there is little reason to de-risk indiscriminately.

In software, IGV above 107 is the key confirmation signal.

I would treat the GOOG pullback as a potential entry, not a confirmed trend reversal.

Keep APP’s 317 level on watch, and wait for sector confirmation before adding to the rest.

The priority is simple: do not call the top too early, and do not chase strength before the breakout is confirmed.
