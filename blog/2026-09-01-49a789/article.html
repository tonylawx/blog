Sticky Inflation and Surging Oil Weigh on Manufacturing

On Tuesday, September 1, the first trading day of September, market volatility abruptly awoke from its August slumber. Crude oil prices continued to climb and Treasury yields pushed higher, putting broad pressure across the four major US equity indices. The S&P 500 triggered its first CTA sell trigger intraday, sparking a brief selloff before dip buyers engineered a late-session V-shaped recovery to close above 7,620. A sustained break below this level would invite further systematic CTA selling.

Macro economic data exhibited distinct stagflationary pressures. The US August ISM Manufacturing PMI dropped from 55.6 to 54.6, missing expectations of 55.2. Under the hood, the Employment Index slid to 51.2 (versus 52.5 expected), the Prices Index remained elevated at 71.1 (above the 70.5 forecast), and New Orders tumbled to 53.7 from 56.8. In the August report, 58% of respondents expressed negative sentiment, citing price volatility (57%), geopolitical war risk (30%), extended delivery lead times (46%), and tariff uncertainties (29%). Meanwhile, July JOLTS Job Openings came in at 7.271 million (revised prior 7.182 million, expected 7.300 million), with the quits rate softening to 1.9% and the hiring rate edging down to 3.2%, confirming a cooling labor backdrop.

Market breadth reflected broad-based defensiveness, with 339 S&P constituents falling against 160 advancing. Among megacap tech names, only META closed higher, while MSFT, GOOGL, and AMZN all slid more than 1%. Surging 10-year Treasury yields increased DCF discount rates, hitting high-multiple growth equities and small caps disproportionately.

Russell 2000 Probes 100-Day Moving Average; QQQ Confronts 702 Pivot

The Russell 2000 (IWM) fell for a third straight session under higher yields and mounting short pressure, closing near $290.00. This marks its first test of the 100-day moving average since its April 2026 breakout five months ago. Goldman Sachs prime brokerage data indicates that small-cap selling has been primarily driven by fresh short positions: while long financing costs and positioning declined, Russell futures open interest jumped by $2.3 billion in a single week, with daily open interest changes running at 2 to 3 times the 2026 average.

IWM has arrived at a pivotal juncture between $288 and $290. A failure by bulls to hold $288 would confirm the high-volume consolidation zone since June as a distribution top, opening downside risk toward $267, representing an 8% to 10% pullback. However, this level also offers an asymmetric risk-reward setup: long positions risk a tight 2 to 3 dollar stop, while a sharp V-shaped rebound could ignite a short squeeze against congested short interest.

The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ), currently near $707, is also hovering near key technical thresholds. $702 serves as the near-term line in the sand—a break below it would significantly weaken momentum and require bulls to reclaim $724 to restore the uptrend. A break below $687 would confirm a broader downtrend. With QQQ typically exhibiting a 1.2x to 1.5x Beta relative to the S&P 500, a 12% to 15% tech correction against a standard 5% to 10% S&P pullback represents healthy volatility rather than a structural breakdown, offering medium-to-long-term entry opportunities.

S&P 500 Holds High Ground; Software and Semis Diverge

Among major benchmarks, the S&P 500 (SPY) remains the highest and most insulated, with major structural support spanning $729 to $756 (with a mid-point pivot at $745). SPY is currently trading above $761, well clear of the $756 upper boundary. This buffer explains why the VIX remains subdued near 16, as broad-market tail risk has not yet materialized.

Semiconductors (SOXX / SMH) are navigating a weak bottoming process. SOXX maintains its acceleration-risk trigger at $486 (currently near $499), leaving a roughly ten-dollar cushion; holding $486 preserves a potential left-side accumulation zone ahead of AVGO earnings. SMH remains weak after breaking its $557 to $581 range, currently at $545, with downside confirmation at $534; Tuesday’s low close did not yet establish a reversal signal.

Software (IGV) experienced a high-volume technical pullback after breaking above $107 to reach $110, driven by daily and weekly overbought momentum divergences. As long as IGV holds above $101, the medium-term outlook for new highs remains intact. A break below $101 would return the ETF to its $90 to $107 trading range. Relative to semiconductors, software enjoys a clearer earnings-and-valuation recovery narrative, and consolidating above $101 would position the sector well to lead once broader markets stabilize.
