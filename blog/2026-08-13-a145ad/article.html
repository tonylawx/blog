0 · MARKET

Soft PPI, indexes push higher

On August 13 (U.S. Eastern), the three major indexes finished higher. The Nasdaq led and kept pressing toward record territory. About 319 SPX components rose and 183 fell; eight of eleven sectors advanced. Financials, consumer discretionary, and consumer staples carried the clearest volume.

July PPI undershot: headline flat m/m (vs +0.2% expected) and +4.7% y/y from 5.5% (vs 4.9% expected); core +0.2% m/m (vs +0.3%) and +4.2% y/y from 4.7%, in line. Initial jobless claims rose to 209k (vs 202k); continuing claims fell to 1.777M (vs 1.795M).

Inflation gave risk assets another breath. A September Fed hold is easier to price, but the labor print is not clean — do not read soft PPI as a confirmed cut path.

1 · SEMIS

SNDK: bigger buybacks, memory chain stirs

SNDK jumped about 13.7%. The story is buybacks and guidance, not slogans.

Management plans to return 100% of excess cash to shareholders. With no dividend, that means repurchases. April’s $6B authorization was followed by roughly $4.5B executed through July 3; August added another $10B, lifting total authorization to about $15.5B. Against an ~$200B market cap, that is close to 7% of the float — large enough to harden near-term bid.

Longer-term targets for FY2028–2030 (roughly from July 2027) point to ~50% adjusted FCF margin, 15%–25%+ revenue growth, ~80% non-GAAP gross margin, and ~75% operating margin. The numbers are aggressive; the quarterly path still has to deliver.

Technically, 1681 was the break-and-hold line; clear it and 1824 comes next before any serious retest of highs. In the same chain, MU’s breakout line sits near 969, with after-hours prints already around 960. Once trapped longs get relief, do not blindly re-lever — Korean broker data showed about 62% of accounts in the 20–30 age cohort blew up or went into deficit in this drawdown. Capital preservation and stops come first.

2 · LIQUIDITY

NFLX: volume lift, heavy supply above

NFLX reclaimed 78.2 on volume after three weeks of light-volume sideways trade — a real impulse, helped by 13F disclosures of institutional buying.

The structure is still a wide range. Supply and structure between 85.7–94.6 are thick, so a clean sprint through 100 is a high bar near term. If the cost basis sits below 77, a one-year-plus hold can still work, with roll-downs to cut cost rather than treating it as a must-own growth chase.

Fundamentally, FY ending Dec 2026 still grows fast, then decelerates into Dec 2027. Forward fair ranges roughly 71–107 for 2026 and an upper band near 115 for 2027. Ads remain early; do not bank on an explosive near-term contribution.

3 · MOMENTUM

TSLA: range highs — do not chase a breakout bet

TSLA is pinned at the top of the lower box 315–368. Only a clean break of 368 turns that band into support; the next ceiling sits at 415–465, a very strong resistance zone.

Chasing here is a bet that the breakout must stick. If it fails and the stock slides from the 330–340 area toward 280, most accounts will not tolerate the drawdown. Wait for confirmation instead of paying for hope.

4 · CONSUMER

WMT and XLP: staples wake up, valuations already rich

After institutional deleveraging, re-entry shows up first in volume. XLP gapped and surged, clearing the 85.3 pivot with room toward prior highs after mid-tier resistance.

WMT rose about 2.4% on volume yesterday and chopped lightly today ahead of the 200-day. Nearby hard resistance is 117–121; a break completes the base and opens a path toward ~135. Spot already sits well above forward fair value and outside the historical volatility band — the market is re-rating traditional retail into an e-commerce-transition premium. Holders below can stay patient; do not lever a breakout that has not printed yet.

5 · SEMIS

IGV over SOXX: one-day rotation, not a short-chip thesis

SOXX finished up about 0.7% but sold off hard around 2:30 p.m.; IGV ripped higher on volume in the same window and sits closer to the top of the 101–107 box. Yesterday looked like long semis / short software; today flipped. One session does not make a trend.

SOXX is near 550, still about 30 points shy of the 580 break-and-hold line. CapEx in semis has not shown a contraction; NVDA locked in roughly $500B-scale revolving credit capacity, and cash is still flowing into the complex with EPS support intact. A persistent long-software / short-semi stance does not fit that fundamental set. If IGV clears 107 first, SOXX is more likely to follow higher than to stay permanently suppressed.

The risk is inside IGV: daily RSI top divergence is already obvious. Divergence usually resolves with a decline, or a violent squeeze that then sells off hard. Even a break of 107 should expect a pullback — trade software, but do not treat the afternoon spike as a reason to chase.
