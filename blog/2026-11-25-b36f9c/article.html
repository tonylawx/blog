0 · MARKET

The indexes recovered most losses, but still closed mixed

U.S. stocks suffered a sharp early selloff on Wednesday, November 25, then staged a fast rebound around midday. The S&P 500 was down as much as 3.2% intraday before closing 0.3% lower. The Nasdaq Composite briefly fell about 4.6% and finished down 0.6%. The Dow gained 0.3%, while the Russell 2000 rose 0.7%. Around three-quarters of S&P 500 constituents finished higher. The weakness remained concentrated in large technology and A.I.-related stocks.

The semiconductor ETF SOXX had an even wider intraday range. It closed at $128.62 the prior day, fell to $118.18 on November 25, then recovered to $127.98. The intraday low was about 8.1% below the prior close, but the ETF ended down just 0.50%. The closing price nearly erased the day's losses. The trading path still included a sharp wave of selling.

The tape sent two different messages. Breadth did not collapse with the major indexes, which means investors were not selling every sector. But the Nasdaq and SOXX still closed lower. Technology buyers had not yet reversed the broader trend. The VIX hit 34.95 and closed at 28.29, up 7.3% from the prior close. Fear eased from the intraday high, but volatility remained elevated.

1 · FOCUS

Three possible catalysts explain the timing, not the cause

The market began to rally quickly between roughly 11:50 a.m. and 12:20 p.m. Eastern time. Three explanations circulated. The FCC authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million orbital compute modules. Michael Burry said he had closed the remaining half of his Nasdaq-100 put position. He said his portfolio was temporarily almost entirely long and held about 25% cash. A third explanation pointed to possible unwinding of yen-funded carry trades.

All three developments are worth tracking, but the evidence is not equally strong. The FCC decision authorizes deployment. It does not mean one million orbital data centres have been built, launched, or started generating revenue. It adds a possible long-term source of compute capacity. It does not prove the decision triggered the intraday buying.

Burry's disclosure is a real position update, but it describes one portfolio. Closing put exposure can reduce some bearish positioning. It cannot prove that the entire market covered shorts at the same time. Daily USD/JPY data also do not show a move large enough to confirm that yen carry trades drove the reversal. The pair traded in a range of roughly 158.66 to 159.49.

My read is that the reversal looked more like short covering after an abrupt selloff, amplified by short-term money in a thin liquidity window. That can explain a rapid price bounce. It does not prove institutions have rebuilt long exposure. A rebound needs three separate tests: whether shorts are covering, whether institutions are adding net longs, and whether fundamental expectations are improving. The first can happen quickly. The other two need more trading sessions and data.

2 · MACRO

Inflation is still sticky, while growth and jobs remain resilient

October headline PCE inflation rose 0.3% month over month, slower than September's 0.5%. Year over year, it accelerated from 3.6% to 3.8%. Core PCE rose 0.3% month over month, unchanged from the prior reading. The annual core rate increased from 3.1% to 3.3%, above the 3.2% consensus.

The slower monthly headline reading is a relief. But the annual rate is still moving higher, and core inflation is not cooling enough to give the Federal Reserve much room to ease policy.

The second estimate of third-quarter real GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualized rate from 3.4%. The revision mainly reflected stronger investment and consumer spending. The BEA did not attribute 65% of GDP growth to A.I. investment. Its release identified investment, consumer spending, and government spending as contributors, partly offset by weaker exports. A.I.-related capital spending is an important part of the investment cycle, but the exact share of GDP growth is not an official BEA figure.

The labour market also showed little immediate weakness. Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000 for the week ended November 21, below the prior week's revised 202,000. Continuing claims declined to 1.697 million for the week ended November 14. Hotter annual inflation, stronger GDP, and lower initial claims together make an imminent shift to easier policy harder to justify. The discount-rate backdrop remains challenging for high-multiple technology stocks.

3 · FOCUS

Nvidia's AA rating is on negative watch, with governance at the centre

On November 25, S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's AA long-term issuer and senior unsecured debt ratings on CreditWatch Negative. The review period is up to 90 days. The concerns focus on board independence, management oversight, CEO succession, and the governance framework. This is not a statement that Nvidia is struggling to meet its debt payments.

S&P said a potential downgrade would likely be limited to one notch, unless further investigations or disclosures harmed the company's reputation or business prospects.

Two risks need to be kept separate. Nvidia's own balance sheet and debt-service capacity are one issue. The dependence of downstream compute operators on Nvidia's ecosystem, equipment orders, and financing arrangements is another. Even if Nvidia's own cash generation remains strong, a rating review may lead some lenders to reassess financing structures tied to customers, equipment, or contracts. That does not mean every downstream loan will automatically become more expensive. There is also no verified public evidence for a single blanket guarantee amount that would be triggered immediately.

The timing matters. The sharp intraday rebound began around midday. S&P's rating action was published at about 1:38 p.m. Eastern time. It therefore cannot explain the initial reversal. It is a separate credit and governance risk for investors to assess after the rebound.

4 · RISK

Positioning pressure may be easing, but confirmation is still ahead

The VIX retreated from 34.95 intraday to 28.29 at the close. That shows the sharpest fear had eased, but the index still finished 7.3% higher than the prior session. It also remained well above the calmer levels near 20. One investor closing puts may reduce some selling pressure. Seasonal allocations into ETFs and long-term household portfolios after Thanksgiving could also provide marginal demand. Neither is a substitute for confirmation in volume and price.

SPY closed at $716.70 on November 25. The $753 level is about 5.1% above the close. It remains an upside confirmation level, not a breakout that has already happened. A high-volume move above $753 that holds during the first week of December would strengthen the case for a year-end rally. Until then, the market is still in a range. Avoid chasing near the upper end and watch how buyers respond near the lower end.

QQQ closed at $633.93, back near the $633–$636 area. That is a near-term pivot zone, not proof of a trend reversal. The more important lower support is around $585, while resistance sits above $700. If QQQ can hold the pivot, attract better volume, and reclaim nearby moving averages, the rebound structure would improve. A move back below the pivot would leave $585 as the next major downside reference.

5 · SEMIS

SOXX has room to rebound, but the technical bear market is not over

SOXX closed at $127.98 on November 25 after touching $118.18 intraday. From the close, the $137–$144 range implies roughly 7% to 13% upside. The next reference is $163, about 27% higher. The $180–$181 area is about 41% above the close and remains a major ceiling. These are potential rebound zones, not promised price targets. Ordinary short covering could be enough to reach the first zone. The next levels would need help from improving fundamentals, earnings expectations, and fund flows.

SOXX remains about 26.7% below its October 28 high of $174.64. The ETF is still in a technical bear market. Even a rally to $163 would not, by itself, end that condition. A sustained, high-volume move above $180–$181, together with stable earnings expectations, would provide stronger evidence of a larger trend repair.

Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) closed at $342.34, after reaching $343.17 intraday. The $342–$343 area is close to the top of its prior consolidation range. The stock tested that level, but the close did not provide a strong confirmation above it. A close above the band, followed by a successful retest, would make the breakout more credible. A quick move back below the range would look more like a short-term bounce.

Netflix (NFLX) closed at $59.41, up 0.92%. Volume reached about 45.6 million shares, above the prior session's roughly 36.1 million. Participation improved, but one stronger session does not replace a confirmed break of the short-term downtrend line. The next test is whether price can close above that line for more than one session and hold the retest. Without follow-through, patience remains the better approach.

6 · RISK

How to tell short covering from a real recovery in risk appetite

Three things need to improve together. First, price needs to confirm the rebound: SPY must break above $753 on stronger volume; QQQ must hold the $633–$636 pivot and make further progress; and SOXX should at least reclaim $137–$144. Second, fear and credit pressure need to ease: the VIX cannot remain stuck in the high twenties, and credit spreads across data-centre borrowers need to retreat from elevated levels. Third, operating fundamentals must support the valuation: A.I. customer spending, compute utilization, and the earnings outlook at major technology suppliers cannot keep deteriorating.

If indexes rebound quickly while the VIX stays high and credit remains tight, the move is more likely a positioning repair. If volume, credit, and earnings expectations improve together, the case for a durable return of risk appetite becomes stronger. So far, the evidence confirms that immediate selling pressure has eased. It does not yet confirm a sustained new bull trend.
