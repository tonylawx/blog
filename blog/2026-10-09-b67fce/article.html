0 · MARKET

All four indexes rose, and the S&P did not clear Tuesday

Friday finished higher across the four indexes. The S&P 500 gained 0.6% to 7,811.54, close to the record close set three days earlier, and did not take it out. The Dow gained 0.8% to 51,654.95. The Nasdaq composite gained 0.6% to 27,366.17. The Russell 2000 gained 0.5% to 2,806.98.

For the week the S&P 500 rose 1.2%, the Dow 0.9%, and the Nasdaq 0.6%. The Russell fell 0.9%. Thursday was the Nasdaq giving blood back to the rest of the tape. Friday took part of that down candle back at the index level. The 10-year yield closed at 5.24% after swinging. Brent erased an early drop and finished slightly higher.

A green index close is not a full risk-on day. Friday's price was in the carriers and the towers, not in a few tenths on the indexes.

1 · FOCUS

Starlink bought indoor spectrum. The carriers fell first

SpaceX agreed to buy a nationwide license portfolio from Grain Management, up to 14 megahertz of paired 800 MHz spectrum. The Wall Street Journal put the price at about 8 billion dollars in cash. SpaceX already holds higher-frequency 2 GHz licenses. The low band is meant to reach indoors and push Starlink toward mobile service. The news hit Thursday evening and traded on Friday.

TMUS closed at 148.58, down 22.73, or 13.27%. The prior close was 171.31. The session low was 147.90. Verizon and AT&T were sold with it. During the session Verizon was pacing its worst day since 2002, down about 7%, and AT&T was down about 10%. At the open the three carriers had shed about 39 billion dollars of market value, close to five times the price of the spectrum. SPCX was up only about 1% at midday.

The mechanism is a competition rerating, not proof that subscribers have already left. 800 MHz travels far and gets through walls. Owning the license is not the same as having the network built. FCC Chair Brendan Carr said more competition in spectrum is good for consumers, and that more than 100 billion dollars of spectrum is likely to come to market over the next two years. That is industry supply. It is not Friday's earnings for these three companies.

This is a valuation and market-structure reset. Without evidence of lost subscribers, do not treat 13% as share already taken. Do not read SPCX up about 1% as the market dismissing the deal. The carriers lost several times the cost of the licenses.

2 · FOCUS

The tower rally is a tenant guess

CCI closed up 11.39% at 76.74. American Tower was up nearly 7% during the session. The story is that indoor low-band coverage could mean a satellite operator eventually rents sites, power, and backhaul. Crown Castle said on its second-quarter call that it would welcome satellite operators. There was no company announcement on Friday, and no new contract. Bernstein's point is that buying spectrum is not a commitment to build.

Confidence is medium. AT&T is about 28% of Crown Castle's site-rental revenue. If the carriers cut spending because of a new competitor, the towers' biggest customers get weaker. Carriers down and towers up cannot both be treated as confirmed.

The 11% is a guess that got bought. Without a lease, 76.74 is not confirmation. The carrier trade is competition. The tower trade is capital spending that has not happened.

3 · SEMIS

Semis leaked again on light volume. 550 is still intact

SOXX closed Friday at 560.26, down 0.54%. It opened at 570.65, high 570.94, low 557.29. Volume was about 3.91 million shares. Thursday's close was 563.28, down 3.35%, on about 10.36 million shares. On a day the indexes rose, the semiconductor fund did not reclaim. It just softened a little more, on much less volume.

The low at 557.29 is still above the 550 to 577 band from Thursday. A break of 550 only extends the chop down to 486. The view changes at 486, about 15% under the close. Friday was not a high-volume break.

Thursday's structure is intact. While 550 holds, the extra half point is not an exit. A return to leadership needs volume back through 577. A 0.6% Nasdaq gain does not substitute for that.
